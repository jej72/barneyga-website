    Probate records: households and town businesses

    Brooks County probate returns preserve more than estate totals. Their vouchers record purchases at local stores, school and town taxes, payments to guardians, and rents. The entries below come from inspected scans of the county's recorded returns. They establish what those records say; matching a name to a census household is a separate question.

    Joyce guardianship receipts

    Annual Returns Book M, p.80, names B. M. Williams as guardian of Willie Joyce. It also records separate settlement receipts from J. P. Joyce, dated March 30, 1909, and Willie Joyce, dated June 11, 1909. J. P. says he has reached age 21; the statement does not give an exact birthdate. Two Walker & Moody receipts dated at Barney in March and April 1906 appear with the account.[1]

    The page contains conflicting years. Its heading says 1908; expenses and the oath bear 1906 dates; the court allowed the final return on September 6, 1909, describing it as for the year ending July 1, 1909. These dates are retained as written.

    The names are relevant leads for the Joyce entries discussed with the Bryant Williams household. This return alone does not establish the wards' parentage, their relationship to one another, or their census identities.

    Ingram estate and Bank of Barney

    The I. B. Ingram estate vouchers include a receipt signed in the register as B. F. Long, cashier, Bank of Barney. Dated September 22, 1906, it acknowledges $206.67 on a note given May 15 and due September 15, 1906. Another voucher records $44 paid to Annie A. Ingram as part of year's support.[2]

    The separately reported sale account has now been inspected. Bills of Sales, page 43, records F. B. Walker, administrator of I. B. Ingram’s estate, selling 190 acres, more or less, of lot 414 in land district 12, Brooks County, to H. F. McWhite for $2,001 at public outcry in Quitman on November 6, 1906. The oath bears the same date. The administrator is a strong-probable match for Fleming Bates Walker, based on the initials, date, place, business activity, and public role, although this probate record does not expand the name. This supplies a parcel and purchaser absent from the annual return’s brief entry; it is a recorded sale bill, not the administrator’s deed or a complete title chain.[11]

    The adjoining accounts distinguish other estate assets. Page 44 records livestock and equipment sold at Barney on November 10, 1906, with five price rows totaling $120.35. Its oath appears dated November 9, before the stated sale, and its total differs from the annual return’s typed livestock amount. Neither discrepancy has been silently corrected.[12] Page 45 records five shares in the First National Bank of Quitman for $555, describing the sale day as the first Tuesday in January 1907, while the oath is January 7. The annual return uses January 7 for the bank-stock entry. Those dates are retained as different record statements; the shares are not Bank of Barney stock.[13]

    Mortgage references to Book N, p.580, and Book M, p.293, remain further leads, with the record series still to be confirmed. Lot 414 is kept distinct from lots 523 and 371; the sale bill does not establish that those parcels were the same.[2][3]

    The administrator's initials are inconsistent: one heading appears as P. B. Walker, while the oath, signature, and several vouchers read F. B. Walker. The widow-support record below strengthens the connection to the Isom Barney Ingram household through America’s name, the 1906 death, and Walker's administration. A chain of land ownership is not established by initials or this support proceeding.

    Widow support and the home place. Book B, pages 72–73, names America Ingram, widow of I. B. Ingram, reports his death July 6, 1906, and states twelve unnamed minor children. Walker, recorded as F. B. Walker, acknowledges notice January 7, 1907; the appraisers sign January 9; the court admits the report as its judgment February 4, 1907. The $4,868 award includes 245 acres, more or less, of lot 371 in land district 12, Brooks County, called the Ingram home place, along with personal property and household/kitchen furniture.[14] The branch name in the boundary description remains an uncertain reading. This is a support award, not a deed or purchase price. It does not equate lot 371 with the separate lot 414 sale or with lot 523. Its twelve-minor statement differs from the eleven children in Barney’s 1906 newspaper notice; no named child list is inferred.

    A Walker & Moody account in the same estate was receipted by J. I. Moody on January 5, 1907. It documents a store transaction, not ownership or the location of the store building.[4]

    Separate Joyce estates and the town's businesses

    Annual Returns Book N records three distinct administrations. They must not be combined because they share a surname:

    Estate as recorded Administrator Selected evidence
    J. J. Joyce Fleming Bates Walker (recorded as F. B. Walker; strong probable identification) Drug-store rent, merchant accounts, store insurance, and Barney town tax
    G. S. Joyce G. W. Alderman Estate expenses and receipts for children's clothing
    J. W. Joyce W. R. Knight Final administration return and legal expenses

    The J. J. Joyce return covers November 4, 1912, through July 5, 1913. It records drug-store rent from C. W. Lane. A separate voucher describes insurance on a one-story frame store in Barney for January 9, 1913, to January 9, 1914. The pages do not prove that the insured store and the rented drug store were the same building. A town-tax receipt records $14.40 for 1912 on property returned at $1,800.[5][6]

    The G. S. Joyce vouchers include Lebbie Ryall, receiving $25.25 for the children's clothing on October 21, 1912, and J. H. Ryall, receiving $15 for children's clothing on December 2. These receipts establish transactions, not kinship or guardianship.[7][8]

    W. R. Knight's final return for J. W. Joyce was allowed on August 4, 1913. It belongs to a separate proceeding; it is not evidence that J. W., J. J., and G. S. were the same person.[9]

    Thompson wards

    Book N, p.172, records W. R. Knight as guardian of William and Damon Thompson. A voucher records $110 paid to Bertha Moore for twelve months' board, clothing, washing, and schooling. The final return was allowed on August 4, 1913. The page does not name their parents or link them to either similarly named 1900 household.[10]
