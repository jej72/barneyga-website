    Family Narrative

    • Head: Henry Jackson, recorded in 1900 as a day laborer in a saw mill

    • Second person in the household: Thomas Jackson, age 18, born in Florida

    • A user-supplied 1900 census detail screenshot places Henry Jackson in Barney, Brooks County, Georgia, on sheet 2, dwelling 44, family 44.

    • That 1900 record shows Henry Jackson as a 28-year-old Black male born in Feb 1880 in Georgia, single, and working as a day laborer in a saw mill.
    • The same record reports that Henry and both of his parents were born in Georgia; that he could not read or write but could speak English; and that the household lived in a rented house rather than on a farm.
    • The 1900 census places Henry Jackson in a two-person household with Thomas Jackson, age 18, listed as partner.
    • A user-supplied Thomas Jackson detail screenshot from the same household shows Thomas as a Black male born in Mar 1882 in Florida, single, working as a day laborer in a saw mill, with both parents also born in Florida.
    • The Thomas Jackson detail view confirms the same sheet, dwelling, family number, and relationship label for the shared Barney household.
    • The Ancestry detail view also preserves a bracketed alternate reading for Henry as "Heuriat Jackson."
    • A user-supplied possible 1910 census candidate for Thomas Jackson places a Black male identified as Thos L. Jackson, age 28, born in Florida to Florida-born parents, in Quincy, Gadsden County, Florida, as a married farm laborer who could not read or write. That candidate is plausible on age and birthplace grounds, but it shifts from sawmill labor in Barney in 1900 to a married household in Quincy in 1910 and is not yet securely bridged to the Barney Thomas Jackson.
    • The present draft remains difficult because the meaning of "partner" in this household still needs careful treatment and the entry does not yet preserve later records clarifying the relationship.
    • The current draft also preserves a negative Brooks County FindAGrave search.

    Research Notes

    • The 1900 image-level detail now preserves the basic household citation and the relationship label "partner," but that label still needs historical interpretation rather than automatic modern assumptions.
    • Thomas Jackson's role in the household is still unresolved. The 1900 detail view clearly preserves the relationship label "partner," but the present file does not yet preserve later records strong enough to determine whether that label meant work partner, household partner, kin, boarder, or something else in this local context.
    • No verified 1910 or later census chain is preserved here for Henry Jackson or Thomas Jackson. A possible 1910 Thomas Jackson candidate in Quincy, Gadsden County, Florida, is now noted, but it remains unproven.
    • The current file does not preserve marriage, death, burial, land, probate, church, or employment records for either person.
    • The earlier mention of possible later candidates, including J. D. Jackson variants and HH#159 Thomas Jackson, should remain tentative unless direct bridge records appear.
    • A negative FindAGrave result is useful as a search note, but it is not evidence of identity or later migration.

    Research Status

    • Status: Partially researched household with a substantially better 1900 anchor but no verified continuity beyond that census.
    • Strengths: The entry now preserves Henry Jackson's age, birth month and state, marital status, literacy profile, rented-house status, and sawmill occupation, while also preserving Thomas Jackson's age, birth month and state, Florida-born parents, single status, matching sawmill occupation, and the shared relationship label.
