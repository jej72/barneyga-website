    Household #86: Jones, Frank

    Back to the 1900 Census & Household Index

    In the 1900 Barney census, Frank Jones headed a 9-person household. The table below preserves the exact names, relationships, ages, birthplaces, and occupations recorded for the household.

    Household Snapshot

    Frank Jones headed a 9-person household in the 1900 Barney census with 6 children and 2 extended-family members.

    Frank Jones's listed occupation was Farmer.

    All listed household members were reported as born in Georgia.

    Children and Later Trails

    Family Narrative

    The 1900 census places Frank Jones in Barney as a widowed farmer born in December 1843. That schedule indexes him as "Jones Frank," and several children in the household are likewise indexed with Frank as though it were their surname. In context, those entries are best read as Jones family members with reversed or distorted given-name/surname order. Cornelia had died before that enumeration, and several younger children or recently married children still clustered around the household. The entry also fits the larger census trail that follows him from childhood into old age.

    That trail begins with the 1850 census, where Franklin Jones, age six, appears in Thomas County, Georgia, in the household of Malake Jones, forty-one, and Sara Jones, thirty-six. By 1860, a strong match appears in Brooks County as Malachi F. Jones, age sixteen, in the household of M. Jones and Sarah R. Jones. On 17 December 1867, Brooks County records the marriage of Frank Jones and Cornelia E. Powell. In 1870, Malachi Jones, twenty-six, appears in Quitman, Brooks County, with wife Cornelia, twenty-four, and sons James and Edgar. In 1880, Frank Jones, thirty-seven, appears in Nankin, Brooks County, with Cornelia and children James P., Edgar, Alberta, Maliki F., Thomas J., Emma A., and infant Samuel. In 1900, he is the widowed head of HH#86 in Barney. In 1910, he appears as Malichi Jones Sr., age sixty-seven, living with son Malichi. In 1920, M. Frank Jones, age seventy-six, appears in the household of son Edgar J. Jones as father. Taken together, those records support a stable identification from the 1850s through the final known census appearance in 1920.

    Walter C. Jones's 1913 biographical sketch identifies Frank's mother as Sarah “Reissier” Groover. Compiled Groover family work identifies Sarah as a daughter of Charles Groover and places Charles as a brother of John Groover Sr. On that reconstruction, James Groover was Sarah's first cousin. The Sarah-to-Frank relationship is supported by Walter's near-contemporary family testimony and the census households. Sarah's parentage and the older Groover sibling link remain strong secondary evidence rather than an original-record proof chain.

    Several non-census records add useful detail. A Brooks County reconstruction oath entry dated 8 July 1867 records Malachi Jones in the Quitman precinct of Brooks County, placing him in the county only months before his marriage. The Brooks County marriage record dated 16 December 1867 and returned 17 December 1867 anchors Frank's marriage to Cornelia E. Powell, and the original document identifies the groom as "Mr. M. Franklin Jones" and the bride as "Miss Cornelia E. Powell." A tax digest entry for Malachi F. Jones in Nankin District 659 during 1878-1882 shows him on the tax list but, in the cited page, does not show land held in his own name. A 14 Oct 1899 Valdosta Times item names Frank Jones among Barney-area farmers, placing him in the local farming community just before the 1900 census. A Confederate soldier profile for M. Frank Jones gives enlistment on 15 May 1862 at age nineteen, residence in Brooks County, birth about 1843 in Thomas County, and service in Company K, 50th Infantry. A Confederate prisoner-of-war record identifies Frank Jones as a private in Company K, 50th Georgia Regiment, imprisoned on 6 April 1865 and received on 15 April 1865. The imprisonment place is given as Sailor's Creek, Virginia in the soldier profile and Harpers Farm in the prisoner record, so that point should be left as a documented discrepancy pending the full compiled service file. Frank's 1909 Brooks County Confederate pension packet confirms that he applied as an indigent soldier under the name M. F. Jones. The visible application image supports the broad picture of age- and poverty-based need and appears to show no meaningful property, though the handwriting should be transcribed from the full packet before exact quotations are relied upon. That evidence fits the broader picture of economic decline after the stronger 1870 showing, though the exact course of his finances still needs fuller documentation.

    The family connection to HH#71 is strong. The 1870 census places Edgar in Frank's household as a child, the 1920 census places Frank in Edgar J. Jones's household as father, and Edgar J. Jones's 1928 Georgia death certificate names his father as F. Jones and his mother as Cornelia Powell. On that basis, Frank Jones can be treated as the father of HH#71 Edgar Jelks Jones and therefore as James E. Jones's great-great-grandfather. The broader proposed connection to HH#56 William Fletcher Jones is still only a working hypothesis and should remain separate from the proved line unless direct evidence turns up.

    Military Service

    The current file supports treating Frank Jones as a Confederate veteran. A soldier profile for M. Frank Jones gives enlistment on 15 May 1862 and service in Company K, 50th Georgia Infantry; a prisoner-of-war record identifies Frank Jones as a private in Company K, 50th Georgia Regiment, imprisoned on 6 April 1865 and received on 15 April 1865. The imprisonment location differs between the preserved records, so that detail should stay flagged until the full compiled service file is reviewed. His 1909 Brooks County Confederate pension packet further anchors the identification under M. F. Jones.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    • Frank Jones is the father of HH#71 Edgar Jelks Jones.
    • Through Edgar Jelks Jones, whose 1928 death certificate names parents F. Jones and Cornelia Powell, Frank Jones is in James E. Jones's direct line as great-great-grandfather.
    • Frank's mother, Sarah Reiser Groover, provides the family's collateral connection to the early Groover settlers; the older generations remain evidence-graded on the linked profiles.
    • Nathan Culpepper appears in the 1900 household as "uncle," but the exact relationship still needs direct-record proof.
    • A possible connection to HH#56 William Fletcher Jones has been suggested, but it remains unproved.

    Open Research Questions

    • Parentage audit: Keep head-of-household and spouse parentage as unresolved unless a cited direct record names the parents or the entry explains the census chain supporting the identification.
    • Child census audit: Continue direct-record tracking for each child named in the 1900 household where later census, marriage, death, burial, obituary, or other parent-link proof is not already cited above.
    • Other household member audit: Verify every non-nuclear household member, including in-laws, boarders, servants, laborers, uncles, grandchildren, and collateral relatives, before treating the stated relationship as part of the proved family structure.
    • Frank's full name is often given as Malachi Franklin Jones or Malachi Franklin "Frank" Jones. The record set now includes "Malachi F. Jones," "M. Frank Jones," and "Mr. M. Franklin Jones," which strongly supports that expansion, though the exact preferred everyday form across his lifetime still rests on stitched evidence rather than one single definitive identity document.
    • A published Bible-record compilation also gives "M. Frank Jones b. 25 Dec 1843, d. Nov 1920, m. in 1866 to Cornelia Powell", but that should be treated as secondary derivative evidence until matched against primary records.
    • Cornelia E. Powell Jones is well anchored as Frank's wife through the 1867 marriage record and the censuses. A Find a Grave memorial provides a likely burial at Camp Ground Cemetery, Mount Zion Church, Morven, Brooks County, but her death date and burial still need stronger direct-record support if available.
    • Frank's death date, death place, and burial have not yet been documented here with a death certificate, obituary, cemetery record, probate file, or estate record.
    • The military summary should still be checked against the full compiled Confederate service record. The soldier profile and prisoner-of-war record agree on identity, regiment, and April 1865 imprisonment timing, but they differ on the imprisonment place, listed as Sailor's Creek, Virginia in one source and Harpers Farm in the other.
    • The 1909 pension application packet should still be reviewed in full and transcribed carefully, especially for residence, disability, financial status, witness statements, and any exact wording about property and family support.
    • The tax digest evidence should be extended across additional years, because one Nankin page showing no land in his name does not by itself explain the 1870 property valuation.
    • Nathan Culpepper's appearance as "uncle" in 1900 needs a direct proof chain; the proposed Powell-French-Culpepper explanation should not be treated as established without supporting records.
    • The proposed relationship between Frank Jones and HH#56 William Fletcher Jones needs direct evidence such as probate, deed, guardianship, Bible, or other kinship records.
    • Each child should be tracked with direct marriage, death, burial, or later census records so the family group rests on more than household appearances alone.

    Research Status

    Well documented, but not source-exhausted. The census chain from 1850 through 1920 is strong, the 1867 marriage record and 1909 pension application are important anchors, and the father-son link to Edgar Jelks Jones is persuasive. Cornelia now has likely cemetery evidence, but the main gaps are Frank's death and burial, stronger direct support for Cornelia's death and burial, fuller documentation of the military and pension files, and proof for the more extended kinship claims.
