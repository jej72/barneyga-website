    Family Narrative

    • Head: Emery Manns, age 21 in 1900, born Jul 1878 in Georgia
    • Wife: Dallis Manns, age 18 in 1900, born in Georgia

    • Other household member: George Manns, father, age 55 in 1900

    • The 1900 census places Emery Manns in Barney, Brooks County, Georgia, on sheet 3, dwelling 45, family 45, as a sawmill laborer in a rented household with wife Dallis Manns and widowed father George Manns. The same record gives Emery's birth month as Jul 1878 and reports that he could read, write, and speak English.

    • The present file now preserves a strong continuity chain showing that the 1900 Emery Manns household is the same family later recorded under the surname Manus.
    • In Jackson County, Georgia, Emry Manus and Dallis Tolbert were licensed to marry on 26 Oct 1897 and were married on 27 Oct 1897; the marriage was recorded on 20 Dec 1897. That record directly anchors the couple before the 1900 Barney household.
    • A 1910 census record in Jackson County, Georgia, shows Emory Manus, age 31, with wife Dallis and children including Maud, Grover, John, Adelle, and Willie. The age, wife, and child pattern fit the 1900 Barney household.
    • A 1920 census record in Barrow County, Georgia, shows H. E. Manus, age 41, farming with wife Dallis and children including Grover, John, Adell, Willie, Mildred, Walter, and Marielle. A 1930 Barrow County census continues the same family under the name Emory Manus.
    • Hugh Emory Manus's World War I draft registration card gives his full name as Hugh Emory Manus, birth date 6 Jul 1878, residence Winder, Georgia, occupation farmer, and nearest relative Dalloe Manus. That card ties the later Manus records to the same birth date and spouse line seen in the Barney household.
    • Hugh Emory Manus's Georgia death certificate shows that he died 18 Mar 1939 in Athens, Clarke County, Georgia, while residing in Winder, Barrow County, Georgia. The certificate names his parents as George Manus and Mary Timbs Manus and his burial place as Chapel Cemetery in Winder.
    • The 19 Mar 1939 Atlanta Constitution obituary for Mr. H. E. Manus names his widow, children, siblings, funeral at Chapel church, and burial in Chapel cemetery. Dallas Manus's 11 Jul 1966 Atlanta Constitution obituary continues the same Winder-centered family and names the adult children.
    • Taken together, the marriage record, 1900 census, later censuses, draft card, death certificate, and obituaries support the conclusion that Emery Manns, Emry Manus, Emory Manus, H. E. Manus, and Hugh Emory Manus were the same person.
    • FindAGrave memorials for Hugh Emory Manus and Dallas Manus agree with the death and burial evidence, but they remain derivative support rather than primary proof.

    Research Notes

    • Full citation details should still be preserved for the 1910, 1920, and 1930 census entries if not already extracted elsewhere.
    • The Manns-to-Manus shift is now well supported by cumulative evidence, but the change should still be described from the records themselves rather than treated as a simple spelling preference.
    • The death certificate names George Manus and Mary Timbs Manus as parents, but the certificate does not give their birthplaces.
    • FindAGrave family links are useful as clues and burial support, not as stand-alone proof of each child relationship.
    • Additional land, probate, church, or cemetery records could strengthen the Winder and Chapel church context, but the present file already preserves the core identity chain.

    Open Research Questions

    Research Status

    • Status: Well-supported household with a substantially resolved Manns-to-Manus identification.
    • Strengths: The entry now preserves direct marriage evidence, a full 1900 household citation, a later census chain, an exact birth date from the World War I draft card, explicit parent names from the death certificate, and obituary evidence for both Hugh Emory Manus and Dallas Manus.
    • Remaining cautions: The remaining work is mostly citation tightening and ordinary follow-up rather than basic identity resolution.
