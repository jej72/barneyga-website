    Identity

    Charley Thompson appears under multiple name spellings across six decades of federal census records. His surname shifted from Thompson (1900-1920) to Thomas (1930-1940) before reverting to Thompson in the 1950 census. The 1900 census provides the most reliable birth data: April 1880, Georgia, both parents Georgia-born. Later censuses show birth years ranging from 1870 to 1886, a pattern common in African American census records of this era where childhood enslavement prevented families from tracking exact dates.

    The earlier narrative associated this household’s Ellen with the bride Ellen Laurence in an April 2, 1899 marriage and with Tyra in a 1910 household. Those identity links are not established by the marriage entry alone. Two distinct 1900 households contain men named Charley Thompson and wives named Ellen. The later census sequence below is retained as earlier research, not newly revalidated; the proposed death interval and child relationships depend on that continuity being confirmed.

    Candidate marriage, not an assigned household bridge (Ancestry Collection 4766, record 127489)

    Children and Grandchildren

    The household grew substantially between 1900 and 1920. By 1920, Charley and Ellen had 10 children at home plus three grandchildren. The children's names shift between census years, making precise identification difficult. Grandchildren appear consistently from 1920 onward, suggesting married daughters were leaving children with Ellen and Charley while working elsewhere.

    Children identified across censuses: Tommie (1900-1920, age 19), Ora Lee (1910-1920, age 18-20), Ercie/Retha (1910-1920, age 8-18), Tira (1920, age 15), Pauline (1920, age 13), S B (1920-1930, age 12-18), Ellis (1920-1940, age 9-29), Eorca/Corey (1920-1930, age 8-16), Lilly (1920-1930, age 7-14), Charley Jr (1920-1940, age 3-23), Rubin (1920-1930, age 1-10), Robert (1930, age 9), Elbert (1930, age 7), Tyra (1940, age 30).

    Grandchildren present: Ethel (1920, age 2), Frank (1920, age 0), Mattie Rountree (1920, age 0, possible Rountree family connection), Allice M/Alice Mae (1930-1950, age 0-21), Arretha/Ireatha (1930-1940, age 4-16), James (1940-1950, age 10-19), Willie (1940, age 7), Doris (1940-1950, age 6-16), Margaret Lee (1940, age 7).

    Key Findings

    The Thompson household illustrates several patterns common to Black families in post-Reconstruction Georgia. Surname instability (Thompson-Thomas-Thompson) reflects both the casual nature of census recording and the vulnerability of Black families to transcription errors. Birth year discrepancies spanning 16 years across six censuses suggest either the informant changed over time or census takers recorded estimates rather than asking for exact ages.

    Ellen's literacy improved over her lifetime. She could not read or write in 1900 and 1930, but by 1940 she had completed the sixth grade and served as the census respondent, filling out the form herself. This suggests either personal determination or access to adult education programs in the Barney area during the 1930s.

    The 1950 census shows Ellen as the head of household, widowed, age 68, living with four grandchildren. Charley died between 1940 and 1950. The surname reverted to Thompson, indicating the Thomas period was likely a transcription issue rather than a legal name change.

    Laurence Family Connection

    The candidate bride’s surname Laurence resembles the surname of Ancil Lawrence (HH#199), who lived nearby in Barney precinct. However, direct parent-child connection is unlikely: Ancil's daughter Ellen, recorded in the 1920 census at age 11, would have been born around 1909, too late to be the Ellen who married Charley in 1899. The Laurence name was present among multiple Black families in Brooks County, and any kinship between these two households remains unverified.
