    Evidence summary

    The 1900 Barney census places Charley Crenshaw in Brooks County as a Black farmer born in North Carolina about May 1845. His wife Violett was born in Georgia about 1850, and the household was a blended one: Violett's mother Mary Mathews was present, along with Smith-surnamed stepchildren or step-grandchildren and a Brown step-grandson.

    The best earlier candidate for Charley is the 1880 Wake Forest, Wake County, North Carolina household of Charles Crenshaw, a Black laborer of similar age, with wife Rachael and children Burrell, Maggie, Tabbie, and Lizzie. The name, race, age, and North Carolina birthplace make this a strong candidate, but no direct record has yet been found carrying him from Wake County, North Carolina, to Brooks County, Georgia. This should remain a leading identification, not a proven merge.

    Violett's own earlier identity is also partly unresolved. Mary Mathews being listed as Charley's mother-in-law makes Mathews the strongest clue for Violett's birth family, but no independent marriage, death, or parentage record has been found proving Violett's maiden name.

    The Smith cluster is clearer. Arrilla Smith is listed as Charley's stepdaughter, and the indexed 1900 census detail names Violett Crenshaw as Arrilla's mother. Guss Smith is in the household as Charley's son-in-law and is married for the same length of time as Arrilla. The best reading is that Guss and Arrilla were husband and wife, married about 1897, and that Silvester, Gussie, and Mary Smith were their children. This avoids the older overbroad assumption that every Smith-surnamed person in the household was Violett's own child.

    Charley Brown remains unresolved. The 1900 indexed census detail gives him as a Black Georgia-born step-grandson in HH#152, born about October 1882, single, and working as a farm day laborer. Possible forward records include a Black Georgia-born Charley Brown in 1910 Juniper Creek, Calhoun County, Florida, age 27, single, head of household, working as a turpentine-farm chipper; a separate previously tested 1910 Norman Park candidate; a 1918 Brooks County World War I draft card naming Luginia Brown of Pavo as nearest relative; and a 1920 Morven, Brooks County, Georgia, Chales/Chaley/Charley Brown, age 37, single, boarder, working as a naval-stores laborer. These records fit the broad name, age, race, and regional pattern, but they cannot yet be proven to be the same individual and they do not prove his parentage or explain the exact step-grandson relationship.

    A separate Jim/James Brown and Lugenia Brown White cluster was tested. It documents a local Brooks County Brown family, but it does not currently explain HH#152 Charley Brown's parentage. Candidate adult records for Silvester Smith and Gussie Smith were also tested; neither should be merged without parent-linked proof.

    Additional Sylvester Smith documents supplied later show why the adult Silvester question remains open. One World War I draft card belongs to a married Black Sylvester Smith born in 1896 at Fort Gaines, Georgia, living in Clay County and employed by Alex Smith; that is geographically possible but not parent-linked to Guss and Arrilla. A Dade County / Coconut Grove trail belongs to a Black Sylvester Smith born about 1 August 1897 in Dublin or Laurens County, Georgia, with Lear/Lear Smith as contact; that birthplace points away from Brooks County unless another record supplies a family bridge. A 1945 Florida state census and 1950 Miami census show a Black Sylvester Smith born in Georgia about 1898, but a 1942 Dade County death index for a Black Sylvester Smith conflicts with those later census records and may represent a different man. A separate 1942 World War II draft card for a Sylvester Smith born in 1921 at Glensville/Glenville, Georgia, is too young to be the 1900 HH#152 step-grandchild.

    Research Notes

    • Direct proof linking the 1880 Wake County, North Carolina Charles Crenshaw to the 1900 Brooks County, Georgia Charley Crenshaw.
    • Violett's maiden name and parentage; Mary Mathews is a strong clue, not direct proof.
    • Any prior Smith marriage for Violett, if one existed. The Smith surname may enter the household through Arrilla's husband Guss rather than through Violett.
    • Marriage or parent-linked records for Guss Smith and Arrilla Smith.
    • Parent-linked adult records for Silvester Smith, Gussie Smith, and Mary Smith.
    • Reconciliation of the competing Sylvester Smith candidate records before any adult trail is attached to the HH#152 child.
    • Charley Brown's parents and exact relationship to the Crenshaw/Mathews/Smith household.
    • Separation of the competing Charley/Chales Brown candidate records before treating them as one adult trail.
    • The relationship, if any, between the Luginia/Lugenia Brown clue and HH#152 Charley Brown.

    Open Research Questions

    • Resolve the open items flagged in Research Notes above with direct records before expanding this household narrative.

    Research Status

    • Status: Well-Researched; not source-exhausted.
    • Strengths: The 1900 household structure is now clearer; the Arrilla/Guss/Silvester/Gussie/Mary Smith interpretation is source-bounded; the Wake County Charles Crenshaw candidate has been identified and held at the right confidence level; Charley Brown and Brown-cluster leads have been tested without overmerging.
    • Remaining cautions: The key identity bridge, Violett's parentage, the possible Smith marriage, and Charley Brown's parentage all remain unproved. The household should not be marked complete or source-exhausted without courthouse, image-level, or parent-linked records.
