    Children and Later Trails

    This section is reserved for later-life child evidence. No later spouse, death, burial, obituary, probate, separate household, or descendant-line identification has been added here yet from direct records.

    Family Narrative

    • The 1900 Barney census shows William Eatman, age 41, born in North Carolina, as head of HH#49 and working as a laborer at a saw mill. James's review of the original image confirms that Eatman is the surname for the whole household despite muddy index/transcription readings. The household included wife Laura Eatman and children Ida, Walter, Cora Lee, and Isa/Asa Eatman.
    • The 1910 census in Barney, Brooks County, Georgia, shows William M. Eatman, age 51, as a farmer, with wife Laura and children Walter G., Cora L., and Ethel G. in the home. That record provides strong continuity from the 1900 Barney household and shows that Laura was still living in 1910.
    • The 1920 census in Barney shows W. M. Eatman with wife Mary Eatman and mother-in-law J. W. Rouse in the household. That record strongly supports the identification of William's second wife as Mary Rouse and shows that he remained in Barney as a farmer after Laura's death.
    • A Lowndes County marriage record shows William M. Eatman and Mary Rouse receiving a marriage license on 31 Aug 1915 and marrying on 5 Sept 1915. This directly anchors the second marriage.
    • William's Georgia death certificate identifies him as William Mann Eatman, white, married, age 63, born in North Carolina, and employed as a farmer. He died at Barney, Brooks County, Georgia, on 5 Jan 1922 of Bright's disease. The certificate names wife Mary Eatman, gives Walter Eatman of Pavo as informant, and states burial at Camp Ground on 6 Jan 1922. It was filed 14 Jan 1922 and handled by J. H. Culpepper & Son.
    • FindAGrave memorials for William Eatman and Laura Eatman place both at Camp Ground Cemetery, Mount Zion Church, Morven, Brooks County, Georgia. Those memorials are useful derivative support for burial location and for Laura's reported death date of 10 Aug 1912, but they are not the same as direct vital-record proof.
    • The earlier census chain is now reasonably strong. In 1860, William Eatmon, age 2, appears in Nahunta District, Wayne County, North Carolina, in the household of Burtis Eatmon and Polly Eatmon. In 1870, a likely match appears as William Eatmond, age 12, still in Wayne County. In 1880, William Eatman, age 22, appears in Great Swamp Township, Wayne County, North Carolina, single and working as a laborer on a farm. Taken together, these records strongly support treating HH#49 William Eatman as a Wayne County, North Carolina native.
    • Laura's pre-marriage chain is also workable. A likely 1870 census entry appears in Columbus County, North Carolina, as Lanna Phillips, age 8, in the household of Asa Phillips and Jennett Phillips. By 1880, Laura Phillips, age 17, appears clearly in Appling County, Georgia, as daughter of Asa Phillips and Jeanette Phillips. A Georgia marriage index then supports a marriage between William Mann Eatman and Laura R. Phillips in 1883, consistent with the later household timeline.
    • Mary Eatman's later-life trail is visible after William's death. The 1940 and 1950 censuses place widowed Mary Eatman in Hahira, Lowndes County, Georgia, in the household of Hattie Fields, where Mary is identified as sister. Those records strengthen the case that Mary Rouse Eatman survived William by decades and remained connected to her own family network.

    Research Notes

    • The evidence now supports the core identity chain William Eatmon/Eatmond/Eatman (1860-1880 North Carolina) → William Eatman (1900 Barney) → William M. Eatman (1910-1922 Brooks County). Even so, the exact parent identification should still be stated from the census chain, not as something proven by the death certificate, because the death certificate leaves both parents unknown.
    • The 1920 census gives William an age that points to a birth around 1851-1852, while the 1910 census and 1922 death certificate point more closely to 1858-1859. The 1920 age is best treated as a one-record error rather than a broken identity chain.
    • The exact marriage record image for William Eatman and Laura R. Phillips has not been recovered in this pass. The 1883 marriage is supported by an index entry and by the later census sequence, but it is not yet anchored by a checked county-level license or return.
    • Laura's FindAGrave memorial supports a death date of 10 Aug 1912 and burial at Camp Ground Cemetery, but a direct death record or cemetery register has not yet been added here. The earlier claim that she died in Miami, Florida should not be treated as established on the present evidence.
    • The entry now supports the census-proven children Ida, Walter G., Cora L., Isa/Asa, and Ethel G., but a complete child reconstruction beyond those directly observed household members remains unfinished. Earlier claims about additional children, exact birth dates, and later marriages or deaths should be treated as provisional unless separately documented.
    • The older version's claim that William spent time in Yarmouth, Hampshire, England is not supported by the records reviewed in this pass and should be omitted unless direct evidence turns up.
    • The prior lateral claim connecting daughter Ida Eatman to the Knight family may yet prove true, but it is not needed to establish HH#49 and should stay out of the main entry unless supported by direct marriage or death records.

    Open Research Questions

    • Parentage audit: confirm both parents of the household head for HH#49 through direct or near-direct records where possible; mark derivative-only parent links as unresolved until supported.
    • Child census audit: confirm every child named in the 1900 household against later census, marriage, death, obituary, cemetery, probate, or draft records; separate proven child links from same-name candidates.
    • Other household member audit: verify boarders, servants, in-laws, stepchildren, grandchildren, and unrelated residents in HH#49; document the exact relationship instead of assuming kinship.
    • Spouse and marriage audit: locate marriage records, death records, divorce records, or later census records for spouses or absent spouses tied to this household.
    • Direct image review: re-check the 1900 census image and any cited index-only sources before treating ages, relationships, birthplaces, or name spellings as final.

    Research Status

    • Status: Well-researched core household with several direct anchors.
    • What is solid: 1900, 1910, and 1920 census continuity; William's 1915 marriage to Mary Rouse; William's 1922 death certificate; derivative burial support for William and Laura; workable early census chains for both William and Laura.
    • Remaining cautions: The first marriage to Laura still needs a stronger county-level marriage record if one survives; Laura's death still lacks direct vital-record proof in this file; the full child list and later child follow-forward work remain incomplete.
