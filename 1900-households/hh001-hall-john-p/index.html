    Household #1: Hall, John P.

    Back to the 1900 Census & Household Index

    In the 1900 Barney census, John F. Hall headed a 3-person household. The table below preserves the exact names, relationships, ages, birthplaces, and occupations recorded for the household.

    Household Snapshot

    The census records a 3-person household with 1 spouse or marital partner and 1 child.

    The head's listed work was Merchant; that gives this page its occupational context without repeating the census table below.

    All listed household members were reported as born in Georgia, which keeps the census snapshot local unless later records show otherwise.

    1900 Census Household

    Name Relation Age Birthplace Occupation
    John P. Hall Head 70 Georgia Merchant
    Martha Hall Wife 60 Georgia —
    George R. Hall Son 27 Georgia —

    Children and Later Trails

    George R. Hall is the only child listed in the 1900 household. Any adult trail for him or for the three absent children reported by the census should wait for direct supporting records.

    Family Narrative

    • Head: John P. Hall (age 70, Georgia, USA, Merchant) — b. May 1830
    • Note: The middle initial on the 1900 census is partly faded, but the visible portion is better read as P than F when compared with other capital F letters on the census image. The 1898-1899 tax digest and the 1899 railroad item preserve J. F. Hall leads, so an enumerator or record error remains possible.
    • Spouse: Martha Hall (Wife, age 60, Georgia, USA) — b. Feb 1840
    • Both parents born Georgia (both John and Martha)
    • Married 40 years (≈1860)
    • Children:
    • George R. Hall (Son, age 27, Georgia) — b. Mar 1873 | "5 children, 4 living" per 1900 census
    • (Note: Son's name confirmed as George R. Hall from census image — CSV had only initial. Other 3 children not in household)
    • Cemetery: Not found in Hall Cemetery, Morven (1990 survey of 157 memorials includes Col. Enoch Hall, Sion L. Hall HH#7, Bolin G. Hall HH#33, but no John Hall). Burial location unknown.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    John P. Hall lived in the same Barney-area Hall surname cluster as HH#7 Sion L. Hall and HH#33 Bolin G. Hall, but no direct record yet proves how, or whether, he was related to either household. The 1898-1899 tax digest lists "J F Hall" and "Geo R Hall" near other Hall names, which should be treated as a clue rather than proof of one family line.

    Research Notes

    • A 14 Oct 1899 Valdosta Times clipping about the South Georgia railroad at Heartpine states that the trains were under the care of conductor J. F. Hall. The initials make this a possible HH#1 lead only if the census initial or the newspaper initials are wrong, and the identification remains unproven. A stronger competing candidate is HH#46 John B. Hall: his 1946 obituary identifies him as a former South Georgia Railroad conductor, and he was about 35-36 in 1899, while HH#1 John P. Hall was about 69 and listed as a merchant in 1900. Treat the 1899 "J. F." reference as unresolved until the original image and railroad/local records clarify whether it is J. F. Hall, J. B. Hall, John P. Hall under an initial error, or another man.
    • A 16 Mar 1899 Macon Telegraph Quitman item states that Mr. J. F. Hall, who had recently been in charge of the Racket store at Quitman, had gone to Barney on the South Georgia road to open a store. This is a strong J. F. Hall merchant-and-Barney locality lead just before the 1900 census, but it should still be kept as a lead until the John P./J. F. name conflict and the spouse-name conflict are resolved.
    • The same article also lists Barney merchants including Joel Hall, Oglesby Bros. & Co., Hall & Kendrick, and W. C. Edenfield. Joel Hall is a useful Barney-area Hall lead, but his identity and relationship to HH#1 John P. Hall and the other local Hall households remain unresolved.
    • The Barney school-teacher reference naming Mrs. Frank Hall is another unresolved Hall lead. Since no Frank Hall has been identified in the 1900 Barney census, she could point toward a man recorded elsewhere as John P. Hall or J. F. Hall, but the current evidence only supports preserving the lead, not merging the identities.
    • FamilySearch follow-up searches on 21 June 2026 did not locate a useful 1900 census match for an older John F. Hall / J. F. Hall candidate around Adel, Barney, or the nearby counties that would clarify HH#1. Exact "John F Hall" + 1900 + Adel, Berrien, Georgia returned 0 results. Exact "John F Hall" + 1900 + Georgia returned 8 results, but the nearby candidates did not fit the older HH#1/father-profile lead: Dasher, Lowndes John F. Hall born Sep 1864 with wife Ettie A. and child Cleo J.; Berrien District 1148 John F. Hall born 1873 in North Carolina; and a Cat Creek, Lowndes child John F. Hall born 1895. Exact "J F Hall" + 1900 + exact residence Adel, Berrien, Georgia returned 0 results; exact "J F Hall" + exact residence Berrien, Lowndes, Brooks, Colquitt, and Irwin returned 0 results. Exact "J F Hall" + Georgia broadly returned 1 result, but it was not a Georgia residence hit. Treat these as negative search notes only; they do not prove HH#1's post-1900 trail or whether the 1899 J. F. Hall railroad reference belongs to HH#1, HH#46 John B. Hall, or another man.

    Greene County / James F. Hall Leads

    The following records should be treated as leads for the HH#1 research file, not as established facts for the 1900 Barney household. They point to a Greene County, Georgia James F. Hall / J. F. Hall cluster with a strong age, occupation, and family-profile overlap, but they do not yet prove that the 1900 Barney head whose image reads John [middle initial uncertain] Hall was James F. Hall.

    • 1857 marriage lead: James F. Hall married Missouri A. Corry/Curry in Greene County, Georgia. This is a possible spouse/family-origin lead, but it conflicts with the 1900 Barney wife being listed as Martha Hall with a 40-year marriage.
    • 1860 census lead: Jas. F. Hall appears in Greene County with Missouri A. Hall and William E. Hall. This supports a Greene County James/Missouri/William household cluster, not a proved identity for HH#1.
    • 1861 civic/military-support lead: Greene County Inferior Court orders name James F. Hall in relief and supply activity connected to Confederate companies, including the Stephens Light Guards and the Greene Rifles. This supports James F. Hall as a Greene County civic/business figure.
    • 1864 militia lead: The 1864 Census for Re-Organizing the Georgia Militia, Greene County, 19th Senatorial District / 143rd Militia District, lists James F. Hall, age 33 years 9 months, merchant, born Georgia. The age points to about May/June 1830, closely matching the 1900 Barney head's reported May 1830 birth and merchant occupation. This is one of the strongest identity clues, but the John-vs-James name conflict remains unresolved.
    • 1870 census lead: James Hall appears with Missouri, William, Mattie, Frank, and an infant in a later Greene County family cluster. This appears to continue the James/Missouri household but still needs a direct bridge to the 1900 Barney household.
    • 1875-1886 business/civic leads: Greensboro newspaper and county-history references place J. F. Hall / James F. Hall in dry goods or store advertising, mayoral service in 1878-1879, and an 1886 alderman-candidate notice. These fit the merchant/civic-man profile and strengthen the Greene County cluster.
    • 1899 Quitman-to-Barney store lead: A 16 Mar 1899 Macon Telegraph item says Mr. J. F. Hall had been in charge of the Racket store at Quitman until a short time before the article and had gone to Barney, on the South Georgia road, to open a store. This gives the James F./J. F. Hall candidate a direct pre-1900 merchant move into Barney, matching the 1900 household's merchant setting, but it still does not by itself prove that the 1900 census head recorded as John P. Hall was James F. Hall.
    • 1880 census-image confirmation: An 1880 Greene County census image reviewed by James on 23 Jun 2026 confirms James F. Hall, age 50, retired merchant, with Missouri N. Hall, age 40, wife; William G. Hall, age 22, son, law student; Frank H. Hall, age 12, son; and George R. Hall, age 10, son. This is no longer only an Ancestry-indexed lead: the image supports Frank H. Hall and George R. Hall as sons in the James F. Hall household. It is important because the 1900 Barney household includes George R. Hall, but the James F. Hall-to-HH#1 identification still needs caution until the John-vs-James and Missouri-vs-Martha conflicts are resolved by additional records.
    • 1905 obituary lead: A J. F. Hall obituary names George Hall of Montgomery and F. H. Hall of Adel among surviving children and describes J. F. Hall as a prominent citizen of Barney. This may connect the Greene County James F. Hall cluster to the later south Georgia Hall network and supports a Barney locality/status match, but it is not by itself proof that J. F. Hall was the 1900 Barney head.
    • Death-certificate leads: Frank Henry Hall's 1920 death certificate names mother Missouri Curry, and Martha Hall Wilby's death certificate names mother Missouri Ann Hall. These records support the James F. Hall / Missouri Corry-Curry family cluster, while father-name variants on the certificates still need reconciliation.

    Working treatment: preserve the James F. Hall material as a strong identity and family-cluster lead. Do not rewrite HH#1's core 1900 household as James F.
