    Family Narrative

    George Washington Alderman (age 35, farmer) - Born 18 August 1864, Brooks County, Georgia - Died 22 December 1935, Douglas, Coffee County, Georgia - Buried Douglas City Cemetery, Coffee County, Georgia - Parents: Jonathan Howell Alderman and Amy Ann McKinney - Book ID: 15-2-14-1 in Aldermans in America; first child of Jonathan Howell Alderman

    Isabel "Belle" Coulter Alderman. - Born 23 October 1863, Brooks County, Georgia - Died 3 June 1934, Douglas, Coffee County, Georgia - Buried Douglas City Cemetery, Coffee County, Georgia - Parents: John A. Coulter and Sena Ann Saffold; the Saffold parentage is supported by Isabel's death record, while full-name detail comes partly through Find a Grave/Geneanet.

    George Washington Alderman was the first child of Jonathan Howell Alderman and Amy Ann McKinney. The book "Aldermans in America" (1957) assigns him book ID 15-2-14-1, tracing his lineage as David Sr. (1749-1831) to Daniel (Col 15) to George (15-2) to Jonathan H. (15-2-14) to George Washington (15-2-14-1). The SAR application for Jonathan H. Alderman (SAR #90614, filed Nov 6, 1963) lists "George Washington Alderman" as a child, with father George Alderman and mother Nancy Carlton for Jonathan, and spouse Amy Ann McKinney.

    George's middle name "Washington" appears on his death certificate and FindAGrave memorial. Census records use "George" (1870), "George W." (1880), "Geo" (1900), "Geo W" (1910), "Georg W" (1920), and "Geo W" (1930). The 1920 enumerator or indexer rendered the surname as "Aulderman," a variant that also appears in HH#176's 1920 census for Jonathan H.

    Isabel Coulter's maiden name appears with spelling variants: "Colter" in 1870 and 1880 censuses, "Coulter" on her death record, FindAGrave, and Geneanet. The marriage certificate indexes her as "Belle Colter." FindAGrave and Geneanet agree on the full name "Isabelle Belle Coulter."

    Wife's Family: The Coulters.

    Isabel Coulter's parents were John A. Coulter and Sena Ann Saffold. The 1870 census records John Colter (30) and Ann Colter (32) with children Isabella (6), William (5), and Ella (3) in Brooks County. The 1880 census records John (40) and Elizabeth (33) with children Isabella (16), William (14), Ella (12), David J. (9), and James (7) in Dry Lake, Brooks County.

    The name discrepancy between "Ann" (1870) and "Elizabeth" (1880) for Isabel's mother is resolved by the death record, FindAGrave, and Geneanet. The death record lists mother as "Saffold." FindAGrave gives "Ann C. Coulter." Geneanet provides the full name as "Sena Ann Saffold." The census variants are explained: "Ann" in 1870 captures her middle name; "Elizabeth" in 1880 is either a different enumerator's error or a nickname. Her actual name was Sena Ann Saffold.

    An 1889 Brooks County sheriff sale notice (documented in HH#153's notes) lists land adjoining "John Alderman, John Coulter, Dan Alderman, and John Copland." The appearance of John Coulter alongside Alderman landholders places the Coulter and Alderman families as neighbors in Brooks County, which explains the 1881 marriage between George W. Alderman and Isabel Coulter.

    The 1900 census records Isabel as mother of 8 children born, 6 living. Only 3 appear in census records under the Alderman surname. The remaining children may have died young or left the household before 1900.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    George W. Alderman sits at the center of the Alderman family cluster in Barney:

    • HH#176 Jonathan Howell Alderman (Source-Researched): George is Jonathan's first child (book ID 15-2-14-1). The SAR application for Jonathan H. Alderman lists "George Washington Alderman" as a child. George's death certificate names "J H Alderman" as father and "Annie McKinney" as mother, independently confirming the identity of J.H.'s wife Amy Ann McKinney ("Annie" = diminutive of "Ann").

    • HH#177 Isaac McDaniel Turner (Source-Researched): Isaac married Nancy Ellen Alderman, George's sister (book ID 15-2-14-8, child #2). Nancy E. Alderman, age 13 in the 1880 J.H. Alderman household, is the same Nancy Ellen who married Isaac Turner. George is Isaac's brother-in-law.

    • HH#171 Mary Turner (Source-Researched): Mary's daughters Mollie and Nancy Orelia married Alderman men, creating a Turner-to-Alderman link independent of Isaac's marriage. Isaac Turner (HH#177) is Mary's son.

    • HH#175 Charlie W. Turner (Well-Researched): Charlie is Isaac's younger brother, both sons of Job and Mary A.R. Turner.

    • HH#153 Daniel Alderman (Well-Researched): Daniel descends from Daniel Alderman (Col 15), brother of George Sr. (15-2). Daniel is J.H.'s uncle and George W.'s great-uncle. The 1889 Brooks County sheriff sale notice listing land adjoining "John Alderman, John Coulter, Dan Alderman, and John Copland" places the Coulter family as physical neighbors of the Aldermans.

    • HH#155 Mitchell Enoch Alderman (Source-Researched): Mitchell descends from William Alderman (Col 26), another brother of George Sr. Mitchell is J.H.'s first cousin once removed and George W.'s second cousin.

    • HH#144 Jacob Hunter Hiers (Cross-Volume, Complete Research): Phillip Hiers married Mary "Polly" Alderman, daughter of William Alderman (Col 26). This connects the Hiers family to the same Alderman network.

    • The Coulter-Alderman neighborhood: The 1889 Brooks County sheriff sale notice (documented in HH#153's notes) lists land adjoining John Coulter and John Alderman. This John Coulter is Isabel's father. The families lived near each other in Brooks County, which explains the 1881 marriage of George W. Alderman and Isabel Coulter.

    Research Notes

    1. Marriage date conflict: The Ancestry marriage certificate image shows 4 Dec 1881. Geneanet records 24 Dec 1881. The Ancestry image is the primary source; the Geneanet date is from a community tree and may reflect a transcription error.

    2. Death date conflict (George): The death certificate gives Dec 22, 1935. FindAGrave gives Dec 21, 1935. One-day discrepancy; the death certificate is the official record.

    3. 1930 census marriage age: George's "age at first marriage" of 29 conflicts with all other evidence. The 1900 census says married 18 years (marriage ~1882, age ~18). The 1910 census says married 28 years (marriage ~1882). The actual marriage was Dec 4, 1881, when George was 17. The 1930 entry of 29 appears to be an enumerator error.

    4. Isabel's mother name variants: 1870 census "Ann," 1880 "Elizabeth," death record "Saffold," FindAGrave "Ann C. Coulter," Geneanet "Sena Ann Saffold." Resolved: her name was Sena Ann Saffold. The 1870 enumerator recorded her middle name; the 1880 "Elizabeth" is likely an error or nickname.

    5. Children count: The 1900 census records Isabel as mother of 8 children born, 6 living, but only 3 appear in census records. The other 5 children may have died young or left the household. Further research in Coffee County, where the family moved after 1910, might locate additional records.

    6. George's middle initial in 1880: The 1880 census records "George T. Alderman" rather than George W. This is likely an enumerator error, as all other records use W. or Washington.

    7. Child-count reconciliation: Isabel's 1900 census reports 8 children born and 6 living, but only Annie, Louis Cleveland, and William Brinson are documented in the current entry. Direct records are still needed for the other children or for a corrected interpretation of the 1900 child count.

    8. Adult trails for children: Louis Cleveland Alderman Sr. has a direct adult/death trail through SAR, draft, census, Find a Grave, and death-index records. William Brinson is now tied to the household through the 1910 "Brenson" child entry and later Coffee County records. Annie now has a direct Brooks County marriage record to J. Calvin McAlester / McAllister, but her later death trail remains unresolved.

    Research Status

    Source-researched, not source-exhausted. Six federal censuses (1870-1930), a marriage record, two death records, two FindAGrave memorials, and two Geneanet entries document George Washington Alderman and Isabel Coulter Alderman from childhood through death. George is confirmed as the first child of Jonathan H. Alderman (HH#176) through the book "Aldermans in America" (15-2-14-1), the SAR application of Jonathan H. Alderman (which lists George Washington Alderman as child), and the 1870/1880 censuses where George appears in J.H.'s household. The Coulter family's physical proximity to the Alderman family in Brooks County is independently corroborated by an 1889 sheriff sale notice listing land adjoining John Coulter and John Alderman. The remaining work is child-count reconciliation and direct adult/death trails for all children implied by Isabel's 1900 child count.
