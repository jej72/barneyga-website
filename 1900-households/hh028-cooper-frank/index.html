    Household reconstruction - The 1900 census places Frank Cooper and Eliza Cooper in Barney, Brooks County, with infant daughter Carrie Lee Cooper, Eliza's mother Frances Sapp, and Eliza's brother Ansel Sapp. - The name pattern in the preserved entry tradition treats Frank Cooper, William Franklin Cooper, and later W. F. or W. T. Cooper references as the same man. The FindAGrave memorial for William Franklin Cooper fits that identification. - The same entry tradition treats Eliza Cooper as Nancy Elizabeth Sapp Cooper. The memorial evidence and census placement support that reading, though not every later-life step has been independently documented.

    Sapp and Pope connection - The 1900 household ties Eliza Cooper to Frances "Fannie" Pope Sapp and Ansel Turner Sapp. - The preserved source trail also links Frances Sapp to George Washington Sapp and to John A. Pope. - The collected records attribute John A. Pope's death to Civil War service and identify Frances as his daughter. That claim is supported here by the listed military indexes rather than by a local service file.

    Later household trail - The preserved entry states that the Cooper family appears again in Colquitt County in 1910 and 1920. - The 1910 household is summarized as showing four children born, two living. - The 1920 household is summarized as showing the family in or near Moultrie, Colquitt County, with additional surviving children. - Those later census placements appear consistent with the memorial dates for William Franklin Cooper and Nancy Elizabeth Sapp Cooper, but the original census records should be checked before treating the connection as settled.

    Related lines noted in the present record set - Frances "Fannie" Pope Sapp is linked to FindAGrave memorial 90447093. - George Washington Sapp is linked to FindAGrave memorial 90447094. - Ansel Turner "Ancil" Sapp is described in the current entry as later marrying Susan Rosenia Rosa Meredith in Worth County, Georgia, serving in World War I draft or service records tied to Dade County, Florida, and later living in Florida. Those details appear to come from the documented marriage, military, and tree materials named below, but not all of those extracts are preserved locally in this pass.

    Military Service

    The household's military-service lead is Ansel Turner "Ancil" Sapp, listed in 1900 as Frank Cooper's brother-in-law. The current entry ties him to World War I draft or service records associated with Dade County, Florida, but it also notes that not all extracts are preserved locally. Treat this as a service lead for a household member until the underlying military record is checked.
