    Household #187: Rountree, William

    Back to the 1900 Census & Household Index

    In the 1900 Barney census, William Ramtree headed a 4-person household. The table below preserves the exact names, relationships, ages, birthplaces, and occupations recorded for the household.

    Household Snapshot

    William Ramtree headed a 4-person household in the 1900 Barney census with 3 boarders, employees, servants, or other non-kin residents.

    William Ramtree's listed occupation was Farmer.

    All listed household members were reported as born in Georgia.

    Newspaper and Public-Record Trail

    Original Brooks probate records inspected September 20, 2026 name H. V. Rountree temporary administrator of Wm. Rountree on January 16, 1928, and permanent administrator on March 5, 1928. The corresponding bond is also dated March 5. These records identify the administrator and estate; they do not state a family relationship.

    A sale return by H. V. Rountree reports a courthouse sale on the first Tuesday in October 1928, with the cover filed October 8. It includes a town lot in Barney sold to J. I. Moody for $70. The sheet gives no street, lot number, dimensions or building description. Five other tracts—not expressly the Barney lot—refer to G. I. Tillman's survey plat in Brooks deeds Book 42, page 101. The administrator's deed and plat remain to be inspected before assigning a location or earlier title.

    Probate citation correction: the formerly cited Ancestry records 8635:120698 and 8635:129982 are two filmings of the same Lowndes County testamentary appointment, page 177, dated October 3, 1927, for a deceased William F. Rountree, with Mrs. Minnie Rountree as executrix. They are not Brooks 1928 probate evidence and are excluded from support for this household. The independent census, death and family findings below are retained; this correction does not establish a relationship between the two estates.

    Family Narrative

    William F. Rountree was born about 1854 and died in 1928. Death-record evidence identifies his parents as Weston/Wesley Rountree and Edith Folsom, matching the Folsom-Rountree-Joyce network. He was the brother of Henrietta Elizabeth Rountree Joyce of HH#182 and uncle of George S. Joyce of HH#186.

    The 1900 household is racially mixed: William is the White head, while Rilla Rountree, Manda Roberts, and Joe Hendrey are Black boarders. The earlier race line that treated William as Black was incorrect and has been removed. The boarders likely reflect post-emancipation labor, tenancy, or former-enslavement proximity, but no direct record proves a specific kinship or former enslaver relationship.

    Rilla Rountree is the strongest boarder trail. The 1900 census lists her as a 92-year-old Black widow, mother of 10 children with 7 living. A 23 August 1904 Valdosta Times item reprinted from the Adel News describes Rilla Rountree as an "old-time slavery woman" past 100, living on J. H. Kennon's place, with son Si Rountree age 77. The ages are approximate but the name and family pattern support the same Rilla/Rolla Rountree cluster.

    Manda Roberts and Joe Hendrey remain unresolved. Manda may belong to the Roberts family network connected to HH#184 or HH#189, and Joe's surname may connect to Hendry/Henry variants near HH#164, but both are leads only.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    William connects to HH#182 as Henrietta's brother and to HH#186 as George Joyce's uncle. Rilla's surname may reflect historic ties to the Rountree family, but the current evidence does not identify her enslaver, parents, spouse, or exact children beyond the newspaper reference to son Si/Simeon.

    Research Notes

    1. William's death record has now been located directly and should be cited for his death date and parentage.
    2. Rilla's 1884 Brooks County marriage record has been located, but her full child list, especially Si/Simeon Rountree, still needs reconstruction.
    3. Manda Roberts needs an adult trail before connecting her to Prince Roberts, Robert Roberts, or any other Roberts household; no safe continuation was found.
    4. Joe Hendrey/Hendry has a likely same-district continuation in 1920 and 1930 Brooks County, but no marriage/death record was found to connect him to Peter Henry/Hendry.
    5. Any former-enslavement relationship is historical context only unless supported by probate, labor, Freedmen's Bureau, estate, deed, or other direct records.

    Online exhaustion update

    The earlier claim that William's probate trail was closed online is withdrawn. Newly inspected Brooks appointments and the 1928 sale return provide further estate evidence; the administrator's deed, cited plat and estate-to-household details still need direct comparison. Rilla has a new Brooks County marriage hit, and Joe Hendrey/Hendry has 1920 and 1930 Brooks County continuations, but neither trail proves kinship to William Rountree or to the Henry/Hendry network. Manda Roberts remains a 1900-only boarder for this entry after targeted Ancestry searches; the similarly named 1880 Chattanooga result is not safe to attach. Former-enslavement or family-labor relationships remain unproved and should be pursued through estate, deed, probate, Freedmen's Bureau, church, and cemetery sources.
