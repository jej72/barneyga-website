    Family Narrative

    • Other household member: Babe Rountree, granddaughter, born February 1895 in Georgia

    Isaac and Annie Rountree are securely anchored in the 1900 Barney census and can be followed backward through Brooks County records with good confidence. The core census trail runs through 1870, when Isaac and Ann Rountree appear with children David, Franklin, Amy, Emma, Daniel, and James; 1880, when Isaac Rowntree and Ann Rowntree remain in Brooks County with a later child set including E. J., Isaac, Sallie A., Willie, and Dora; and 1900, when Isaac and Annie are in Barney with granddaughter Babe Rountree in the household. That gives the household a solid frame even though several family relationships inside it remain open.

    The main line now under study is the branch of Frank Rountree, which can now be tied more firmly to Isaac and Annie than the earlier draft allowed. The key front-end record is the original Brooks County marriage of Frank Rountree and Maudy Stroud. The license was dated 18 January 1883 and the marriage was performed 19 January 1883. The original record strengthens the marriage itself, though the bride's given name still needs careful handling because the license reads most naturally as Maudy Stroud while the return line looks closer to Mary Stroud. That variation should be preserved rather than smoothed away.

    The later records strengthen the identification of this Frank as the son of Isaac or Ike Rountree and Annie or Ann Rountree. In 1930 Frank Rountree appears in Militia District 660, Brooks County, as a 66-year-old married head of household with wife Amanda Rountree, age 64. The detail page reports that both Frank and his parents were Georgia-born, that he could read and write, that he owned his home, and that he worked on his own account doing odd jobs. The 1932 Brooks County death certificate for Frank Rountree then gives his parents as Ike Rountree and Annie Rountree, reports that he died 1 October 1932 in the Morven district, and names Robert Stroud of Barney as informant. Taken together, those records materially strengthen the case that the later Frank was the same man as Franklin from Isaac and Ann's earlier household.

    The wife-name chain also becomes clearer, though not fully closed. The 1883 marriage gives a Stroud wife under the form Maudy, with the return line suggesting Mary. The 1900 household gives Amandia Rountree. The 1930 household gives Amanda Rountree. The most economical reading is that these records describe one woman under varying forms of the same name, but the file should still note the spelling problem rather than pretending it does not exist.

    The Stroud connection remains the clearest outside family link in the household. Amanda Stroud, age 6 in 1870 and age 18 in 1880, appears in the Henry Stroud and Margarette Stroud household in Brooks County. She remains the strongest current candidate for the Stroud woman who married Frank Rountree in 1883 and later appears as Amandia or Amanda Rountree. That parent placement is still probable rather than fully proved, but the Robert Stroud informant line on Frank's death certificate keeps the Stroud connection active inside the same neighborhood network.

    The best-documented child in the Frank and Amandia branch is still Calhoun Rountree. In 1900 he appears in their household as a farm laborer born in December 1883 in Georgia. He is the one child in that branch who is presently proved by direct census evidence. The central unresolved problem is that Amandia reported two children born and two children living in 1900, while only one child can be identified securely from the surviving evidence. That missing second child remains one of the main open questions in HH#115.

    Babe Rountree, listed as granddaughter in Isaac and Annie's 1900 household, should now be handled more cautiously than in the previous draft. She is a solid household fact, but no follow-up documentation has been found for her, and no direct record yet proves her parentage. She may belong to the Frank and Amanda branch or to another child of Isaac and Annie, but the present evidence does not let her carry the argument about Amandia's missing second child.

    The 1920 layer for the Frank and Amanda or Amandia branch remains open. Targeted FamilySearch work was attempted using narrowed Georgia census searches for Frank Rountree and Amanda or Amandia Rountree, but the site repeatedly returned the error message "Something Went Wrong." That should be treated as site instability on this query path, not as negative evidence against the family.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    • The strongest developed connection is the probable Stroud link through Frank Rountree's wife line.
    • Amanda Stroud in the Henry and Margarette Stroud household is the best current candidate for Frank Rountree's wife Maudy, Mary, Amandia, or Amanda Rountree.
    • Robert Stroud of Barney served as informant on Frank Rountree's 1932 death certificate, which keeps the Stroud connection relevant beyond the marriage alone.
    • The Rountree surname appears elsewhere in the Barney encyclopedia, including HH#119, HH#120, and HH#122, and those households may eventually connect to Isaac's line.
    • Babe Rountree's placement in Isaac and Annie's household may later connect HH#115 to another Rountree branch in the same community, but that parentage line is still open.

    Open Research Questions

    • Franklin Rountree is now much more likely to be the later Frank Rountree, but the full bridge would still benefit from one more direct linking record across the earlier and later household forms.
    • Maudy Stroud, Mary Stroud, Amandia Rountree, and Amanda Rountree still need a full chain of records tying those names to one woman.
    • Amanda Stroud still needs direct proof that she was the daughter of Henry Stroud and Margarette Stroud and the same woman who married Frank Rountree.
    • The second child implied by Amandia's 1900 fertility report still needs identification.
    • Babe Rountree still needs parentage proof and should not be assumed to solve the missing-child problem.
    • The Frank and Amanda or Amandia branch still needs a verified 1920 census record or equivalent substitute.
    • Amanda Rountree likely died between the 1930 census and Frank's 1932 death, but that still needs direct documentation.
    • Isaac and Annie Rountree still need stronger later-life records beyond the 1900 household, especially death, burial, or probate material.

    Research Status

    Further research needed. Isaac and Annie Rountree are securely anchored across 1870, 1880, and 1900. The Frank branch is now materially stronger because the original 1883 marriage record, the 1930 Frank and Amanda household, and Frank's 1932 death certificate all reinforce the same line and support Frank as son of Ike and Annie Rountree. The biggest remaining gaps are the full wife-name chain, the identity of Amandia's second child, Babe Rountree's parentage, Amanda's death before Frank's 1932 certificate, and the still-missing 1920 layer.
