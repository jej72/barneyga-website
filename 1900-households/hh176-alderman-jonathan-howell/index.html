    Book Reference: "Aldermans in America" (1957).

    Jonathan H. Alderman appears as entry 15-2-14 in the genealogical work "Aldermans in America" by Jennie Alderman Park (1957). The numbering system traces his lineage:

    • 15 = Daniel Alderman (1774-1857), David Sr.'s eldest son → stayed in North Carolina
    • 15-2 = George Alderman (1797-1882), Daniel's son → moved to Georgia 1826
    • 15-2-14 = Jonathan H. Alderman (1847-1924), George's 14th and youngest son

    The book lists 16 children for J.H. and Amy Ann, provides full birth/death dates and spouse names for each, and records that J.H. "was also in the ranks with Lee" during the Civil War. Three of George Alderman's sons died in Confederate service: William (killed by shell at Rappahannock, August 1862), Daniel H., and David J.

    Correction: earlier research incorrectly described George, William, and Timothy as "three founding brothers." David Alderman Sr. (1749-1831) is the patriarch. Daniel (Col 15), William (Col 26), and Timothy (Col 27) are David's sons, making them brothers. George (15-2) is Daniel's son, making him their nephew, not their brother.

    Research Notes

    1. Birth date discrepancy: Death certificate says March 14, 1847; FindAGrave, SAR application, and book all say March 17, 1847. The March 17 date has three independent sources but the death certificate is an official record. Unresolved.

    2. Marriage date discrepancy: Ancestry marriage record says August 2, 1863; book says August 2, 1862. The book cites county records directly and may be more authoritative. The actual document image would settle this. Unresolved.

    3. Birthplace discrepancy: Death certificate says South Carolina; 1850 census and typed genealogy say Georgia; FindAGrave says Lowndes County, Georgia; Geneanet says Berlin, Banks County, Georgia. Most likely: born in South Carolina (as death cert states), raised in Georgia from early childhood. Lowndes County was the precursor to Brooks County; the area near the SC/GA border makes cross-state birth and raising plausible.

    4. 1900 census parent birthplace error: 1900 census lists both parents born in Georgia, but 1880 and 1910 censuses confirm both born in North Carolina (consistent with the Alderman family's NC origins). The 1900 entry is an error.

    5. HH#180 James Alderman identity: The James Alderman in HH#180 has children (Enoch, Newton, Florence, Joseph, James, Brooks, Essie) that do NOT match the children of James Alderman (Col 28), David Sr.'s son, whose children include Eliza, Elias, Timothy, Sarah Jane, Nicie, Ezekiel, James, Mary, Charles Henry, Ansel. HH#180 James is likely from a different Alderman line. Unresolved.

    6. Direct child-level records: Several child dates and spouse names come from Aldermans in America, SAR/FindAGrave, or Geneanet-style compiled sources. Direct Ancestry searches already corrected Dudley Vernon Alderman's death to 22 Jul 1965 and Flavius Anderson Alderman's death to 8 Dec 1971, so any remaining book/tree-only child dates need the same primary-record review.

    Military Service

    Jonathan Howell Alderman's military-service evidence is currently source-limited but important. The family book states that he served "in the ranks with Lee" during the Civil War, and the 1910 census marks him as a Confederate survivor. Until a compiled service file, pension record, or unit-specific source is added, the page should avoid assigning a unit beyond those two anchors.
