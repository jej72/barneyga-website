    Household #174: Weeks, Charles Pinkney

    Back to the 1900 Census & Household Index

    In the 1900 Barney census, Pinksuy Weeks headed a 3-person household. The table below preserves the exact names, relationships, ages, birthplaces, and occupations recorded for the household.

    Household Snapshot

    Pinksuy Weeks headed a 3-person household in the 1900 Barney census with 1 spouse or marital partner and 1 extended-family member.

    Pinksuy Weeks's listed occupation was Laborer Saw Mill.

    All listed household members were reported as born in Georgia.

    Family Narrative

    Charles Pinkney Weeks (also listed as Pinksuy Weeks, Pink Weeks) - Born April 23, 1835, Georgia (birth year varies by source: 1834 per Civil War records, 1835 per FindAGrave, 1837 per earlier FindAGrave, 1838 per 1900 census) - Died August 7, 1901, Moultrie, Colquitt County, Georgia (confirmed by FindAGrave and Civil War records) - Buried Murphy Cemetery, Colquitt County, Georgia - Occupation: Sawmill laborer (1900 census) - Nickname: "Pink" — confirmed by Georgia death record listing "Pink Weeks" as spouse of Rodie - Father was James Weeks (born ~1810, South Carolina, per 1850 census) - Mother was Elizabeth Weeks (born ~1815, South Carolina, per 1850 census)

    Rhoda Chason Harrell Weeks (also Rodie, Rhody) - Born August 31, 1840, Thomas County, Georgia - Died October 28, 1920, Moultrie, Colquitt County, Georgia - Buried Murphy Cemetery, Colquitt County, Georgia - Maiden name: Harrell - Middle name: Chason (not a second surname; maiden name is Harrell) - Father was Lewis Harrell (born ~1802, North Carolina) - Mother was Litty (also Letha) Harrell (born ~1804, North Carolina) - Cannot read or write (noted in 1910 census) - Marriage: ~1861 (per FindAGrave), Colquitt or Thomas County, Georgia - Married approximately 40 years by 1900 census (8 children born, 6 living) - Survived Charles by 19 years; appears in 1910 census as widowed mother-in-law in the Moses Pitts household

    • Charles Luke, born ~1865; 1870 Age 5; 1880 Age 15; Listed as "Chas L" on Georgia death record of father
    • Rhoda Ann, born ~1866; 1870 Age 4; 1880 Age 13; Married George A. Johnson, Nov 14, 1886, Colquitt County (HH#173)

    • Thomas J., born ~1876; 1870 —; 1880 Age 4; Only appears in 1880 census

    • William, born ~1880; 1870 —; 1880 —; Appears in 1900 census (age 20); possibly same as William in HH#193

The two deceased children by 1900 are likely among those who appear in only one census enumeration.

    Charles Pinkney Weeks served in the Confederate Army during the Civil War.

    • Unit: Company H, 50th Georgia Infantry, Private
    • Enlisted: March 22, 1862
    • Service Notes: Injured or hospitalized during service; survived the war
    • Soldier Index: Record 1138:1588748 (Confederate Soldiers index)
    • Soldier Records: Record 1555:779070 (full name confirmed as Charles Pinkney Weeks, born about 1834)

    Charles took the Reconstruction-era loyalty oath on July 6, 1867, in Butts County, Georgia, District 26 (Record 1857:1050468). This location is geographically inconsistent with all other records, which place Charles in Colquitt or Brooks County before and after the war. He likely spent time in Butts County during or shortly after the war, possibly while serving or recovering from his injury, before returning to South Georgia.

    2. FindAGrave Memorial #145288617 (Charles Pinkney Weeks): Born April 23, 1835; died August 7, 1901; Murphy Cemetery, Colquitt County, Georgia. (Ancestry-hosted index record — second memorial with different birth year.)
    3. Georgia Death Record #60768027 ("Pink Weeks"): Spouse listed as Rodie; child listed as Chas L. Confirms nickname "Pink." (Ancestry Collection 60525)
    4. FindAGrave Memorial #101884420 (Rhoda Weeks): Born August 31, 1840; died October 28, 1920; married 1861; Murphy Cemetery, Colquitt County, Georgia.
    5. FindAGrave US Index (Rhoda Ann Weeks): Maiden name Harrell; born August 31, 1840, Thomas County, Georgia; died October 28, 1920, Moultrie, Colquitt County, Georgia; buried Murphy Cemetery. (Ancestry Collection 60525)

    6. Georgia Death Record #127759 ("Rhodie Weeks"): Died October 28, 1920, age 81, Moultrie, Colquitt County, Georgia.

    7. Birth year inconsistency: Charles's birth year varies across sources — 1834 (Civil War records), 1835 (one FindAGrave), 1837 (another FindAGrave), 1838 (1900 census). The 1850 census lists him as age 12 (born ~1838), but the 1860 census shows age 23 (born ~1837) and the 1870 census shows age 31 (born ~1839). The most consistent cluster is 1837-1838.

    8. "Chason" is a middle name: Earlier confusion about whether "Chason" was Rhoda's maiden name has been resolved. Her maiden name is Harrell. "Chason" appears to be her middle name.

    9. 1880 middle initial discrepancy: The 1880 census lists "Charles T. Weeks," but no other record includes a middle initial "T." This may be an enumerator error or a name variant that was dropped in later usage.

    10. Mary Ann Weeks = "Janie" Pitts: The 1910 census shows Rodie Weeks (age 69, widow) living as mother-in-law in the household of Moses Pitts (age 46) with wife Janie Pitts (age 40). Janie Pitts (age 40, born ~1870) matches the age and position of Mary Ann Weeks (age 17 in 1880). The nickname "Janie" is a common shortened form of Mary Ann.

    11. James W. Weeks = HH#193: The 1900 census entry for HH#174 lists William Weeks (son, age 20) and Pinkey Weeks (granddaughter, age 11). Meanwhile, HH#193 lists James Weeks (age 28) as head with brother William (age 19). James W. Weeks (age 28 in HH#193) matches the expected age of Charles's son James (age 4/12 in 1870, age 2 in 1880). William in HH#193 (age 19) may be the same William listed in HH#174 (age 20) — the one-year difference is within census rounding.

    12. Butts County oath: The Reconstruction oath places Charles in Butts County in 1867, but every other record puts him in the Colquitt/Brooks/Lowndes area. This suggests a temporary wartime or post-war stay, possibly connected to his military service or recovery.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    1. HH#173 (George A. Johnson): Daughter Rhoda Ann Weeks married George A. Johnson on November 14, 1886, in Colquitt County. This is the primary cross-volume link — HH#173 and HH#174 are connected by marriage.

    2. HH#193 (James Weeks): Son James W. Weeks appears as head of HH#193 (age 28, married to Mary, with brother William age 19). Same age and location as Charles's son James. HH#193 is a child household of HH#174.

    3. HH#55 (James Harrell): James Harrell (age 37, b. ~1863, sawmill laborer) in Barney bears the same surname as Rhoda's maiden name. Lewis Harrell (Rhoda's father, b. ~1802 NC) was in Thomas/Colquitt County. James (b. ~1863) is roughly 60 years younger than Lewis, which makes a grandson relationship plausible. The HH#55 entry was marked research-exhausted before the Weeks/Harrell connection was known. If HH#55 is revisited, the Lewis Harrell line should be the first avenue of investigation.

    4. Pitts family: Daughter Mary Ann "Janie" Weeks married Moses Pitts. The 1910 census shows Rhoda (widow, age 69) living with them in Barney. No Pitts household appears in our 1900 Barney enumeration.

    5. Harrell family (Thomas/Colquitt County): Lewis Harrell (b. ~1802 NC) and wife Litty/Letha (b. ~1804 NC) appear in the 1850 Thomas County census and 1860 Colquitt County census. Ancestry suggested records show a "Chasn Harrell" in non-population schedules and a "Mrs Rhoda Harrell" in Georgia Property Tax Digests (1793-1892), suggesting Lewis died and his widow Rhoda (the elder, mother of our Rhoda) appears independently in tax records.

    Military Service

    Charles Pinkney Weeks served in the Confederate Army during the Civil War. The current page identifies him as a private in Company H, 50th Georgia Infantry, with Confederate Soldiers index and soldier-record references, and notes injury or hospitalization during service. The Butts County reconstruction-oath location remains a context question, but it does not remove the current service identification.
