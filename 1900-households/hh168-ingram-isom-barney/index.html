    Family Narrative

    Household identity and correction

    Isom Barney Ingram was a White farmer in the Barney district of Brooks County in 1900. This household should not be merged with Walter Benjamin Ingram of Monroe County; that earlier association was a separate-family conflation.

    Barney appears in earlier records as Isam B. Ingram in 1870 and Icoum B. Ingram in 1880. Those records place him in Berrien County, Georgia, in the household of John L. Ingram and Martha A. Ingram. By 1900 he was living in Brooks County with his wife, America, and nine children. A 1906 Valdosta obituary reports that Barney died near Barney after a 25-day illness with typhoid fever and left a wife and eleven children. The obituary also names his brother as G. W. Ingram of Adel, tying Barney back to the John L. and Martha A. Ingram family cluster.

    Annie America Folsom is supported as Barney's wife by the 9 November 1884 Berrien County marriage record for America Folsom and Barney Ingram, the 1900 household, the 1910 and 1920 widow-household trail, and her 1923 Brooks County death certificate. Her death record names her father as William Folsom and her mother only as Martha; the mother’s maiden surname is not supplied on the original. It names I. B. Ingram as spouse and records Mt. Zion Cemetery, not the combined Camp Ground / Mt. Zion wording. The relationship between those cemetery names needs separate evidence. The Folsom and Ingram families were near one another in Berrien County's District 1311 in 1880.

    1910 widow household

    By 1910 Barney had died and America appears as a widow, indexed as America Ingham. The household includes eleven children: John, William L., Del B., Walter W., Mattie, Charlie B., Georgie A., Nina, Tennis M., Lonla/Lula M., and Annie B. The surname is a transcription error, but the children's names, ages, and location match the Ingram household.

    The 1910 household contains eleven children, but that later household count is not a list of the children living when Barney died in 1906. The 1910 original separately reports twelve children born and twelve living; the 1907 support petition reports twelve unnamed minors. These statements are retained separately from the obituary’s eleven survivors. Annie B., age 3 in 1910, may be a posthumous or near-posthumous child, but a precise birth record is still needed before using her to revise the child count beyond the obituary and census framework. Her inspected marriage does not supply that date.

    Georgia: a probable Bradford trail, with the discrepancy retained

The original 1946 Lucas County, Ohio, marriage record, page 78, no. 156633, for Evelyn E. Bradford Drought and Richard C. Bechtol, names Evelyn’s father Oscar J. Bradford and mother’s maiden name Georgia Ingram. License and ceremony are dated February 21, 1946. This is the daughter’s marriage, not Georgia’s own marriage and not a record naming Georgia’s parents.

    The photographed marker at memorial 36868602 reads Georgia Bradford / July 5, 1898 / Jan. 9, 1988. The 1900 original gives this household’s daughter Georgia July 1898 birth. The match in name, maiden surname, birth month/year and nearby Cook locality makes the Bradford identification probable. It does not, by itself, explain Proudfoot or prove that “Odell” meant Adel. Antioch Cemetery, Adel, is the memorial’s catalog attribution, not wording on the stone.

    The Georgia death index gives Georgia E. Bradford, 89, death and residence in Cook County, January 9, 1988, filed January 14. The original civil certificate remains uninspected. A suggested 1973 Harry Bradford obituary is another parent-role lead; its source image 889622668 returned “page … not found” and was not read.

    Two Social Security candidates—Georgia Bradford born November 27, 1897 and Georgia B. Ingram born June 23, 1897—lack the necessary parent/locality bridge and remain unassigned. A search-result candidate Georgia Evelyn Bradford with July 5, 1898 birth but February 5, 1988 death also remains unassigned pending record-level reconciliation. Bounded Georgia/Oscar marriage searches did not locate their own original entry in the reviewed results; no absence is claimed.

    Tennie / Tennys Ingram Knight: the later trail

The occupation/industry cells contain overwriting; the earlier clean description “private-school teacher” should not be treated as an exact settled transcription.

    A May 25, 1930 Florida Times-Union notice, page 31, Jasper column, says Miss Tennys Ingram, who had been teaching at Barney, Georgia, was visiting “her brother and sister, Mr. and Mrs. W. W. Ingram.” This is direct contemporary support for her teaching and a family visit, but the initials alone are not expanded to a full identity here.

    The original Brooks white marriage register, Book F, right second entry, names Douglas Knight and Miss Tinnis Ingram, with license and ceremony October 17, 1930. The groom’s handwritten middle initial is not resolved here; the index reads W. The recording date is blank. The entry supplies no parents.

    The 1950 original, Brooks ED 14-1, sheet 10, line 30, begins the household with Douglas Knight, 52, farmer. Sheet 11, lines 1–4, continues with wife Tenny, 48, daughter Charlotte, 15, son Wade, 9, and daughter June, 7. Both pages were inspected; the head is not inferred from the index alone.

    Ninnie Ingram Holloway: original certificate and a niece bridge

    The original South Carolina death certificate, 74-003474, records February 21, 1974, Beaufort Memorial Hospital, death; January 8, 1886, Georgia, birth; age 88; widowed; and residence at Bay View Nursing Home in Beaufort. It records February 23 burial in Beaufort Memorial Gardens. The informant is Mrs. W. R. Cochran, with a Savannah address; no relationship is written in that field.

    The parent fields actually read “Isom Barney Holloway” and “Folsom, Annie America Holloway.” The earlier suggestion that the father’s surname was merely an indexing problem is withdrawn. These are anomalous original entries, not new evidence that Barney’s surname was Holloway. The broader identity chain and independent parent-naming records for other children remain necessary.

    The June 3, 1965 Beaufort Gazette, page 2, obituary headed “Mrs. John G. Jones” identifies Audrey Bernice Holloway Jones as a Barney-born daughter of Ninnie Ingram Holloway and the late Matthew Holloway. It names Miss Mattie Ingram of Beaufort as her aunt. Audrey died May 26; the notice reports the funeral already held and burial in Beaufort Memorial Gardens. It also names daughter Mrs. W. R. (Sue) Cochran of Anchorage. That name is a useful lead for the later informant, but a shared married name alone does not establish that the 1974 Savannah informant was the same person.

    Additional bounded searches did not resolve Georgia’s Proudfoot/Bradford discrepancy or locate Ninnie’s own 1974 obituary. Only the recorded result slices were reviewed; these are not claims that the records do not exist.

    Lula Mae Ingram Webb: marriage and later life

    The original Webster County white marriage register, Book A, page 96, middle entry, names William Claude Webb and Lula Ingram. The license is December 11, 1932, but the ceremony return says December 18; recording followed January 6, 1933. The searchable index’s December 11 date is the license date, not the recorded ceremony.

    The January 1, 1933 Macon Telegraph, page 13, confirms a December 18 wedding in Cordele, at the home of Mr. and Mrs. P. M. Strickland. It explicitly calls Lula Mae the daughter of the late Mr. and Mrs. Barney Ingram of Barney. The January 1 publication date is not the wedding date. The photograph headed “Attractive Bride” beside this article belongs to a different notice and is not a portrait of Lula.

    The announcement reports a University of Georgia education and home-demonstration work, describing “Baker county, with headquarters at Preston.” That county/place wording has not been independently resolved. It describes William Claude as stationed in San Diego with Navy aviation; this newspaper statement is not an inspected service record.

    The 1950 original, Columbus ED 162-46, sheet 76, lines 16–19, lists William C., 46—not the index’s 16—his wife, 44, daughter Nancy A., 12, and nephew Jack Knight, 18. The wife is a welfare worker. Her reported age differs from the later November 1904 birth date and is retained rather than recalculated.

    Her December 19, 1998 Ledger-Enquirer obituary, page 31, reports December 17 death at Sumter Regional Hospital in Americus, age 94, and November 19, 1904 birth in Brooks County. It names parents Isom Barney Ingram and Annie America Folsom Ingram, surviving husband William Claude Webb, and daughter Nancy Webb Petras. It reports teaching and home-demonstration work in Stewart and Webster counties. The notice announces a service at the Chapel of the Pines Mausoleum in Parkhill Cemetery, Columbus; prospective arrangements are not proof of completed burial. The original civil death certificate has not been inspected.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    The 1870 and 1880 Berrien County records place Barney with John L. Ingram and Martha A. Ingram. The 1880 sibling group includes George W. Ingram, Martha J. Ingram, William J. Ingram, William W. Ingram, Nancy P. Ingram, and John G. Ingram. George W. is especially important because Barney's 1906 obituary names G. W. Ingram of Adel as his brother.

    The strongest confirmed nearby connection is HH#169, John Giles Ingram, another son of John L. and Martha A. Ingram. HH#161 George W. Ingram may be part of the broader Ingram network, but the precise connection should not be assumed without renewed proof in that entry.

    Original record reconciliation

    The 1884 Berrien marriage entry, no. 448, names Barney Ingram and America Folsom, with a November 7 license and November 9, 1884 ceremony. It names no parents. This differs from the marriage year estimated from fifteen years married in the 1900 census.

    The 1900 original, ED 9, sheet 9 A, lines 6–16, reports America, 33, born December 1866, with nine children born and nine living. The 1910 original, ED 21, sheet 13 A, lines 12–23, reports America, 41, widowed, with twelve born and twelve living. These are source statements, not independently proved birth histories.

    The July 13, 1906 newspaper, page 4, was re-inspected. “Mr. Barney Ingram Dead” reports age 42, death near Barney “last Friday morning” after twenty-five days of typhoid fever, wife and eleven children, brother G. W. Ingram of Adel, and burial at the camp ground near Morven. The item is credited to the Adel News, so the relative phrase is not treated as an independently dated original report. The support petition below supplies an explicit July 6 date. The brother in this notice is not automatically George W. of household 161, whose separate estate reports a 1903 death.

    The 1923 original death certificate, file 34150, calls her Mrs. Annie A. Ingram, widowed, spouse I. B. Ingram, and records December 8, 1923 death in Barney, Brooks County. It gives December 31, 1865, Berrien County, as birth, age 57 years 11 months, father William Folsom and mother Martha. Her birth date differs from the 1900 entry and her 1910 age. Informant C. B. Ingram of Carrollton has no relationship label on this certificate; it does not by itself prove that he is her son Charles. Burial is recorded December 9 at Mt. Zion Cemetery. The original says widowed, unlike the index’s married classification.

    Related probate records

    The original twelve-month-support register, Book B, pages 72–73, names Mrs. America Ingram as widow of I. B. Ingram, reports his death on July 6, 1906, and states twelve minor children, without naming them. The administrator, recorded as F. B. Walker, acknowledges notice January 7, 1907. The initials, date, place, business activity, and public role make Fleming Bates Walker the strong-probable identification. The appraisers sign January 9, and the court admits their report as its judgment at the February 4, 1907 term.

    The support is valued at $4,868, including 245 acres, more or less, in lot 371, land district 12, Brooks County, described as the Ingram home place, with livestock, crops, equipment, cash and household/kitchen furniture. The boundary text mentions a branch, but its name has not been securely transcribed here. This is a support award, not the administrator’s deed, a purchase price, or a complete title chain.

    America’s name, her widow status, the 1906 death, and Walker's administration provide a stronger bridge to this household than I. B. initials alone. The award does not name the children or settle the twelve-versus-eleven count. The separate estate sale concerns 190 acres in lot 414, not the lot 371 home place. Neither record identifies lot 523 or proves that these parcels were the same.

Those records make his military-service trail part of the household's later history.
