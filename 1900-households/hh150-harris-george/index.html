    Family Narrative

    George L. Harris appears in the 1900 Barney enumeration as a young farmer with his wife Cora and infant daughter Ruby L. The household reported that George and Cora had been married two years, matching the surviving Sumter County marriage record for G. L. Harris and Cora Tomlison/Tomlinson dated 20 January 1898.

    The later census trail appears to follow the same family from Barney into Quitman, Brooks County. George is enumerated as George L. Harris in 1910, 1920, 1930, 1940, and 1950, generally as a farmer or retired/unable-to-work farmer. The records show two daughters in the household line: Ruby/Rubie L. Harris and Mabel/Maybell I. Harris, later wife of Russell W. Emerson/Emmerson. In 1930 and 1940, Mabel, Russell, and their son Henry Harris Emerson were living with or near George and Cora. Ruby/Rubie moved to Winter Garden, Orange County, Florida, where the 1940 and 1950 censuses list her as an unmarried public-school educator/principal. A Florida county marriage record and the Florida marriage index place her marriage to William Fred Roper in Citrus County, Florida, on 30 December 1951. The Orlando Sentinel published the marriage notice two days later, identifying Rubie Harris as the daughter of Mr. and Mrs. George Lee Harris of Quitman. Later records identify her as Rubie H. Roper of Winter Garden. She died in Orange County, Florida, on 23 January 1998 and was buried at Woodlawn Memorial Park in Gotha, Orange County.

    George's earlier placement is better supported than before but still not fully resolved. The Valdosta Daily Times obituary says George Lee Harris was born in Lowndes County, Georgia, and was a son of the late Mr. and Mrs. John Harris. An 1880 Irwin County household lists George L. Harris, age eight, son of John and Liza Harris; this likely fits the obituary's father-name evidence and approximate age, but it should still be treated cautiously until a death certificate or other direct linking record confirms the mother and reconciles the Lowndes/Irwin/Brooks locality trail. Cora's 1880 Sumter County placement with W. M. and Sarah A. Tomlinson is plausible and supported by the marriage surname, but direct parentage should remain cautious until tied by a record beyond name, age, and locality.

    Connections to Other Barney Families

    • Wife: Cora Tomlinson/Tomlison Harris.
    • Children documented in the census and obituary trail: Ruby/Rubie L. Harris Roper and Mabel/Maybell I. Harris Emerson/Emmerson.
    • Ruby/Rubie L. Harris married William Fred Roper in Citrus County, Florida, on 30 December 1951; the 1 January 1952 Orlando Sentinel notice adds the family context and Winter Garden connection.
    • Grandchild documented in the household and obituary trail: Henry Harris Emerson/Emmerson, later Lt. Henry H. Emerson.
    • Father of George: John Harris, supported by the Valdosta Daily Times obituary; possible mother: Liza Harris, based on the 1880 Irwin County census match.
    • Possible parents of Cora: W. M. Tomlinson and Sarah A. Tomlinson of Sumter County, Georgia, based on the 1880 census match and Cora's marriage surname.

    Open Research Questions

    • George's 25 July 1952 death in Brooks County is supported by the Georgia Death Index and by two newspaper obituaries. A full death certificate is still needed for parent fields, birthplace detail, and informant evidence.
    • The Valdosta Daily Times obituary gives the fuller survivor list: widow Cora Tomlinson Harris; daughters Mrs. Russell W. Emerson of Quitman and Mrs. W. Fred Roper of Winter Garden, Florida; and grandson Lt. Henry H. Emerson. It also says George was born in Lowndes County, was son of the late Mr. and Mrs. John Harris, moved to Brooks County as a child, was a prominent farmer, and was buried in Oak Hill after funeral services at Quitman Baptist Church.
    • Mrs. W. Fred Roper is now identified as Ruby/Rubie L. Harris by the Florida marriage records, the 1952 Orlando Sentinel marriage notice, and Ruby/Rubie's Winter Garden census, death, obituary, burial, and Social Security trail.
    • Rubie's 1998 Orlando Sentinel obituary says she was born in Barney, Georgia, moved to Central Florida in 1925, taught for Orange County schools, lived on Lakeview Avenue in Winter Garden, and was survived by Emerson nephews and a Foster niece. This fits the George Harris household and the Mabel Harris Emerson branch.
    • Cora's death is supported by the Georgia Death Index and FindAGrave marker image, but her full death certificate is still needed for parent fields and informant evidence.
    • The Social Security Applications and Claims Index hit for George Walton Harris is treated as a non-match and should not be used for this household.
    • The 1940 census race entry appears to have been recorded as Black/Negro with later corrections to White. All other listed records in this trail identify the family as White, so the 1940 entry should be noted as a discrepancy rather than used to reclassify the household.

    Research Status

    • Status: Strong census, marriage, death-index, obituary, cemetery-marker, and Ruby/Rubie Roper trail; not source-exhausted.
    • Strengths: 1900 Barney household, 1898 marriage record, 1910-1950 census continuity, Georgia Death Indexes, FindAGrave marker images, the Valdosta/Orlando obituaries, and Ruby/Rubie's Florida marriage, census, death, obituary, burial, public-record, and Social Security trail give a coherent family line for George, Cora, Ruby/Rubie, Mabel, Russell Emerson, and Henry Harris Emerson.
    • Remaining cautions: George's mother, Cora's parentage, and the full death certificates for George and Cora need direct-record confirmation.
