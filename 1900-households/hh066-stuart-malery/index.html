    Original household boundary check (September 20, 2026). The 1900 original, ED 9, sheet 3B, lines 99–100 contains only Malery, 70, and Lucy, 65, in dwelling/family 66. Lucy, age 24, belongs to James Stuart’s separate household 62 at lines 75–79. Her duplicate placement here has been removed; this is not evidence of two wives or three residents in Malery’s household. Lucy in household 66 is recorded as born March 1835, married forty years, with ten children born and two living. That count is not a named list or proof of which children survived.

    Children and Later Trails

    No children reside in the two-row 1900 household, but the 1880 children named below remain open research lines. A child’s absence from this household is not proof of death.

    Family Narrative

    Malery Stuart, also recorded as Malrey Stewart and Malery Stuart, can be placed in Brooks County from at least 1880 through 1900, though the surviving record base remains thin. In 1900, he and Lucy Stuart appeared in Barney as an older Black couple living alone in a rented household. The census gave Malery's birth as May 1830, Lucy's age as 65, and the length of the marriage as about forty years, pointing to a marriage around 1860 if that report was roughly accurate.

    The earlier census places the family in Morven. In 1880, Malrey Stewart, age 49, appeared with wife Lucy, age 50, and five children in the household: Laura, 15; George, 12; Mary A., 11; Stewart A., 8; and Joseph, 3. The original, ED 4, page 93/330A, dwelling 827/family 844, lines 7–13, explicitly records those relationships and Malrey working on a farm. Lucy is reported born in North Carolina, unlike Georgia in 1900; her age 50 in 1880 also differs from the later age progression. These conflicts remain unresolved.

    A George Stewart entry in the 1890 Brooks tax list remains a possible follow-forward lead for the son, not a proved identification. The earlier narrative’s name/place/age comparison does not supply a parent bridge; that original George entry was not re-inspected in this review.

    A Morven tax page in the Brooks 1878–1882 volume explicitly aligns C. W. Ryall, under “Name of Employer,” with Malory Stewart. The uncommon given-name variant, locality and nearby Ryall households in both censuses support a likely connection to this man, but the tax row names no wife or children. The individual tax year has not been established from that leaf; the collection’s generic “1890” citation is not its date.

    A second original tax page has Malory Stuart’s employer column blank. Colen E. McRae appears beside Miller entries farther up that page, not on Malory’s row. This does not disprove employment at another time; the earlier reported McRae connection remains unverified. Neither entry establishes kinship, a pre-emancipation relationship, land ownership or a purchase.

    Later father–son evidence and death

    The 1920 original, ED 46, sheet 8B, lines 81–87 places Malory, 97, widowed, father, in James Stuart’s Barney household with James, Lucy and their children. The 1921 original death certificate for Malory Steward, file 30062 records December 18, 1921, Barney, Brooks County, and names James Steward (Son) as informant. These are direct father–son statements and strongly support linking the older man here with James of household 62.

    The death certificate reports age 100, Georgia birth, and unknown parents; it says “Single” and “Not married,” unlike the 1920 widowed entry. The later ages conflict with the 1900 May 1830 birth entry. The records are not harmonized into a single birth year or marital history. The burial field has not been securely read, so no cemetery or grave is assigned.

    Neither source identifies James’s mother. Lucy here is not automatically his biological mother, and the 1880 child Stewart A. is not automatically James. Searches of first-page 1870 and 1910 census results did not identify a secure earlier or intervening entry; those searches were bounded, not exhaustive.

    Open Research Questions

    • Parentage audit: Keep head-of-household and spouse parentage as unresolved unless a cited direct record names the parents or the entry explains the census chain supporting the identification.
    • The reported marriage about 1860 should be tested against Brooks County or nearby marriage records if any survive for Malery Stuart and Lucy Stuart.
    • The 1880 children Laura, George, Mary A., Stewart A., and Joseph should be followed forward through later census, marriage, death, and cemetery records.
    • The 1890 George Stewart tax candidate needs original-image review and a parent bridge before assignment to the 1880 son.
    • Find the specific source for the reported Colen McRae association. The inspected pages support a C. W. Ryall employer entry and a separate blank-employer entry, not that claim.
    • Malery's name may appear in variant forms such as Malrey, Malary, Mallary, or Mallory; those variants should be checked in census and county records.
    • The 1920 father relationship and 1921 son-informant statement support the James link; resolve the older man’s age conflicts and seek James’s mother without assuming she was Lucy.
    • Malory’s 1921 death certificate is inspected; his burial remains unread and unassigned. Lucy’s death and burial, and both spouses’ probate, land and church trails, remain open.

    Research Status

    This household is better anchored than it was at the start, but it is still thin. The 1880 and 1900 censuses establish Malery and Lucy in Brooks County across two decades, and the inspected tax page adds a qualified C. W. Ryall employer association. The 1920 and 1921 originals supply a direct James–Malory father–son bridge and a qualified December 18, 1921 death identification. The George tax candidate remains unassigned. Conflicting ages, Lucy’s later life, burial, probate, land, church and other child trails remain unresolved.
