    Household #7: Hall, Sion L.¶

    Back to the 1900 Census & Household Index

    In the 1900 Barney census, Sion L. Hall headed a 6-person household. The table below preserves the exact names, relationships, ages, birthplaces, and occupations recorded for the household.

    Household in Local Newspapers¶

    Newspaper accounts from Barney's first years show that Sion L. Hall's public role extended beyond the merchant occupation listed in the census. In February 1899, reports of a stolen horse case named S. L. Hall among the Barney men connected with the pursuit after Perry Peeples recognized the animal and used Barney's telephone connection to help trigger the arrest. A May 1899 court report then carried the case into Brooks Superior Court at Quitman.

    The clearest article for Hall's local role is the November 1899 report of his storehouse burning at Barney. That account says the post office operated inside Hall's store, so the fire destroyed not only goods and accounts but also mail, post office records, and justice-of-the-peace papers. The reported loss was about \$600 with no insurance. Read together with the December 1900 notice of S. L. Hall's election as justice of the peace, these articles show Hall as more than a merchant: his store was one of the places where Barney's business, mail, and local legal affairs met before the town's civic institutions were fully built out.

    Household Snapshot¶

    The census records a 6-person household with 1 spouse or marital partner and 4 children.

    The head's listed work was Merchant; that gives this page its occupational context without repeating the census table below.

    All listed household members were reported as born in Georgia, which keeps the census snapshot local unless later records show otherwise.

    Children and Later Trails¶

    The 1900 census caught Sion L. and Malpha Hall while their household was still young. Alice, Mattie, Willie, and Fannie May were all listed as daughters at home, with Alice the oldest at about nine and Fannie May still a small child. They were growing up in a household tied to Barney's store, post office, justice-of-the-peace work, and the older Hall-Gornto family network.

    Alice Hall is the first daughter with a later newspaper trace. A Valdosta Daily Times notice dated 21 July 1911 is cited for her marriage, showing that she had reached adulthood and entered her own household by the early 1910s. The groom is not named in the extracted notes used for this page, so the marriage is noted here without adding an unverified spouse.

    Mattie Hall's later trail is stronger in the preserved records. The Georgia marriage and death-record trail carries her forward as Mattie Mae Hall Parrish, tying the child listed in Sion and Malpha's 1900 household to a later married identity. A 19 January 1910 Valdosta Daily Times marriage notice gives the local setting for that transition: H. F. Parrish of Valdosta and Miss Mattie Hall of Barney were married at the Barney home of her parents, Mr. and Mrs. S. L. Hall. That gives this page one clear daughter line beyond the Barney childhood snapshot.

    Willie Hall and Fannie May Hall are both named in the 1900 household. No later spouse, death, burial, obituary, or separate household identification has been added for either daughter yet, so they remain here as the younger Hall daughters documented in the 1900 census.

    Historic Family Line¶

    Sion L. Hall belongs to one of the older Hall lines tied to the Morven-Barney area. Huxford places his father, Col. Enoch Hall, in the first generation of local public life: Enoch commanded the 81st Regiment of Lowndes County militia during the 1836 and 1838 Indian War campaigns, served as a Lowndes County Inferior Court justice from 1832 to 1837, represented the county in 1838, and helped oversee the laying out of the Coffee Road.

    The family line reaches back another generation. Huxford identifies Enoch Hall as a son of Sion Hall and Bridget "Beady" Hall, and says Sion Hall moved to the Morven area when the Coffee Road opened in 1823. Sion Hall's home became a public inn on the Coffee Road, hosted the first Lowndes County court, and later passed to Enoch Hall. Huxford describes the Hall place northeast of Morven as the oldest known improved home place in Brooks County with continuous possession back to 1823.

    That context matters for this household because Sion L. Hall was not only a Barney merchant in 1900. He was part of the Hall-Gornto settlement family that connected Barney, Morven, the Coffee Road, early courts, militia service, and local commerce across several generations.

    Source note: this section is based on Folks Huxford, The History of Brooks County, Georgia, 1858-1948, especially the Coffee Road, Morven, and Col. Enoch Hall passages.

    Family Narrative¶

    • Head: Sion L. Hall, age 32 in 1900, born in Georgia, merchant.
    • Wife: Malpha or Malphia Westberry Hall, born in Georgia.
    • Children in the 1900 household: Alice, Mattie, Willie, and other later children supported across the 1910-1940 trail.
    • Identity chain: The cited records support Sion L. Hall from childhood in the Enoch Hall household through the 1900, 1910, 1920, 1930, and 1940 censuses.
    • Career and public role: The same trail supports Sion as a merchant, tie contractor or inspector, farmer, and local justice-of-the-peace figure in Barney.
    • Family line: The record set continues to support Sion as a son of Enoch Hall and as part of the founding Hall-Gornto network in Brooks County.
    • Children and descendants: The current local proof set strongly supports daughters Alice, Mattie, Reva, Thelma, and others through census, marriage, death, and newspaper records, while a few later-life details still rest on compiled follow-forward work.
    • Burial: Positive Hall Cemetery memorials for Sion and Malpha support the burial location and strengthen the broader family reconstruction.

    Connections to Other Barney Families¶

    • HH#33 Bolin Hall: Strong family connection. The preserved research trail treats Bolin Hall as Sion's half-brother.
    • HH#14 and HH#15 Gornto households: Strong maternal-family context. The bridge is Hannah Elizabeth Gornto Hall (1847-1933), Sion L. Hall's mother. Hannah was a daughter of Joel Gornto and Mary Blair and a sister of William Blair Gornto Sr. of HH#15. She married Col. Enoch Hall, the son of early settler Sion Hall and Bridget "Beady" Hall. Through Hannah, Sion L. Hall was tied directly into the Gornto family network represented by HH#15, and William Blair Gornto Jr. of HH#14 was his Gornto-side cousin. Mary Blair's proposed descent from William Blair and his reported wife Mary Joyce remains secondary-supported rather than original-record verified.
