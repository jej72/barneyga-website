    Civil War soldiers

    This page covers people connected to Barney who were identified in the Brooks County veteran register or found during a separate review of men in the 1900 census who were old enough to have served. Those two source groups are not equivalent: an age-based candidate is not presumed to have been a soldier.

    Documented veterans

    Each linked name has an individual dossier summarizing the service evidence and any remaining conflicts.

    Name Rank Unit Service evidence
    Jonathan Alderman Private Company K, Georgia Reserves A Colquitt County pension packet confirms his Confederate reserve service. The handwritten regiment number appears to be 4, but that reading remains provisional.
    John Berryhill Sr. Soldier Company C, 2nd Georgia State Troops; later Company G, 49th Georgia Infantry A compiled soldier profile identifies his Georgia service and matches the established civil record for the Barney-area voter.
    Francis M. Cannon Soldier Company H, 41st Georgia Volunteers His Brooks County pension packet states that he served for three years and repeats the same unit in the 1906 and 1907 papers.
    R. T. Collins Veteran Unit not yet identified The Brooks County veteran register identifies Collins as a veteran, and census records plus an affidavit in William H. Pace's pension file establish the local identity. Research has not yet tied him to a military unit.
    William B. Gornto Soldier Company A, 20th Georgia Battalion Cavalry His Brooks County pension and compiled soldier profile agree on his Confederate cavalry service.
    Malachi Franklin Jones Private Company K, 50th Georgia Infantry Jones was captured at Sailor's Creek on April 6, 1865, probably in the Hillsman House sector. The exact capture wording still awaits the complete compiled service record and original prisoner roll.
    William H. Pace Sr. Soldier Company E, 29th Georgia Battalion Cavalry His Brooks County pension identifies service beginning in September 1863. A separate pension index independently identifies his 1912 Brooks County application.
    Enoch H. Pike Soldier Company A, 2nd Georgia Cavalry; later 20th Georgia Battalion Cavalry Pike's original pension application and William B. Gornto's affidavit identify Company A, 2nd Georgia Cavalry. The compiled-service index uses the later battalion designation.
    William R. Ryall Soldier Company B, 1st Battalion Florida Special Cavalry Ryall, a same-company witness, and a War Department abstract confirm Florida cavalry service beginning about February 1864. His papers differ on whether his final release occurred at Madison or by parole at Thomasville.
    William M. Russell Soldier Company G, 12th Georgia Infantry Volunteers Russell and two witnesses identify his May 1864 enlistment and unit. The exact handwritten location of his April 1865 surrender or discharge remains unclear.
    Joseph J. Rogers Soldier Company I, 26th Georgia Infantry A soldier profile reports that Rogers enlisted on May 1, 1862, and was paroled at Thomasville on May 19, 1865. A separate Brooks muster also names a Joseph Rogers in the Piscola Volunteers; the relationship between the entries remains under study.
    George W. Wilkes Veteran Unit not yet identified The Brooks County veteran register identifies Wilkes as a veteran. A same-name Chatham Artillery record has not been connected to him by age, residence, spouse, pension, or another identifying fact.
    Bolin G. Hall Private Company F, 12th Georgia Infantry A War Department headstone application connects this service to the local man. The application and cemetery memorial differ by one day on his death date.
    Blackman Hawley Culbreth Corporal; later private Company F, 24th North Carolina Infantry; Company B, 2nd Battalion Local Defense Culbreth enlisted at Fayetteville on June 1, 1861, at age sixteen. He was promoted to corporal, later reduced to private, and discharged for minority on December 9, 1862. His uncommon full name, Cumberland County origin, age, and later civil record connect the soldier to the Barney educator.
    Joseph J. Oglesby Lieutenant; later military-shop foreman Company D, 2nd Georgia Infantry A July 1914 Confederate Veteran memorial says Oglesby volunteered in Company D, was elected lieutenant, and later supervised firearms production at a Macon machine shop. Huxford independently identifies him as a Confederate veteran. His original muster and shop-payroll records remain uninspected.
    E. D. Ponder Veteran Unit not yet identified An exact-name Georgia Confederate pension index in Brooks and Wilkinson counties confirms veteran status. Ponder's 1880 Quitman household, Brooks County tax trail, and service as county coroner connect the pension entry to the local man. The pension images and compiled service record have not yet been inspected.
    Henry A. Lashley Soldier Company F, Lee Battalion, under Capt. L. L. Strozier Contemporary obituaries report that Lashley enlisted on April 15, 1863, was wounded at Atlanta, and left active service in August 1864. His Florida pension calls the organization Company F, 25th Georgia Battalion Infantry. The War Department found H. E. rather than Henry A. Lashley in that unit, so the exact compiled-service-file link remains unresolved even though his veteran status is well documented.
    Stephen William Bourquin First lieutenant Company H, 25th Georgia Infantry The Confederate Officers Card Index and a compiled soldier profile identify Bourquin's rank and unit. His uncommon name and civil record connect the soldier to the Barney resident.
    Charles Nelson Isom Veteran Unit not yet identified The civil record follows Isom from Berrien and Lowndes counties to Barney and later Plant City, Florida. A 1930 newspaper photograph calls him a “veteran of 1861,” and the 1930 census independently records Civil War service. No pension or unit-linked service record has yet been identified.
    William M. Hall Soldier Captain H. Morgan's company, 18th Georgia Regiment A 1907 Georgia indigent-pension application and the household chain through his wife, Serena Hires or Hiers, connect the service record to the Barney resident. His reported birth date differs from the census age pattern.
    Henry Sapp Soldier Company D, 18th Georgia Infantry The 1911 Brooks County pension application of his widow, Susan Sapp, identifies his Confederate unit.
    Charles Pinkney Weeks Private Company H, 50th Georgia Infantry A Confederate service index, soldier profile, and 1867 Reconstruction oath identify Weeks and connect him to the local civil record. Records place his birth between about 1834 and 1838.

    Method

    The project compares voter registers, censuses, pensions, compiled service records, military indexes, newspapers, cemetery material, and family records. A service claim must connect the soldier to the Barney-area person through identifying facts such as age, residence, family, occupation, or a direct pension or newspaper statement.

    “Research underway” means the current evidence is not strong enough to publish a service conclusion. It does not prove that the person did or did not serve.
