    Dr. Theophilus Williams Hunter

    Back to the Railroad Organizers index

    Identity confidence: Verified.

    Theophilus Williams Hunter was a Quitman physician, farmer, major landowner, school-board member, and South Georgia Railroad subscription organizer. He was born in Georgia on September 21, 1852 and died in Atlanta on December 13, 1919. He usually appeared in public records and newspapers as Dr. T. W. Hunter. His original death certificate supplies the full name, while Jefferson Medical College and the Journal of the American Medical Association independently identify him as Theophilus W. Hunter.[1-5]

    Birth, parents, and childhood

    Hunter was a son of Abraham Hunter and Jane Bryan or Bryant. His death certificate uses Abraham and Jane Bryant. The 1860 Brooks County census lists Abram Hunter, age fifty-one, Jane, thirty, and children John M., Theophilus W., Abram B., and Virgil C. Theophilus was seven and all members of the household were Georgia-born.[1-2]

    The date September 21, 1852 is supported by his photographed marker, the September 1852 entry in the 1900 census, and the age and birth day on his death certificate. Georgia is the only birthplace that can be stated without qualification. Brooks County and Quitman did not exist in 1852. Huxford's later description of Hunter as Brooks County-born may refer to the part of Lowndes County that became Brooks in 1858, but no contemporary birth record or exact land location was found. The derivative claim that he was born in Quitman is anachronistic.[1-3][6]

    The parents' surname evidence also needs to remain visible. The death certificate writes Jane's maiden name as Bryant. Family and local material use Bryan. The records identify the same woman, but they do not justify silently choosing one spelling.[1]

    Schooling and medical education

    The 1870 census places eighteen-year-old Theophilus Hunter and nineteen-year-old John Hunter at school near Jefferson in Jackson County. Their names, ages, Georgia births, and later return to the same Brooks County family make this a strong-probable match to the Hunter brothers. The schedule does not name the school.[7]

    Hunter's 1919 obituary says that he graduated at the state university before receiving a medical diploma in Philadelphia. No university record was found that names his degree or attendance dates. His university education remains reported but unverified.[17]

    His medical education is much firmer. Jefferson Medical College's institutional alumni roll lists "Hunter, Theophilus W., Ga., '76." Huxford's transcription of the Brooks County physicians' register gives a Jefferson Medical College diploma date of March 11, 1876. JAMA's January 1920 professional death notice again identifies Theophilus W. Hunter of Quitman as a Jefferson graduate of 1876.[4-6]

    Medicine and farming

    The 1880 census found Hunter back in his parents' Tallokas District household. He was twenty-seven and his occupation was doctor. The schedule appears to give him a middle initial M, and Ancestry follows that reading. The single conflicting initial is outweighed by the marriage record, other censuses, medical-school records, and death certificate that use W or Williams.[8]

    The 1900 Quitman census called Hunter a physician. Contemporary newspapers continued to use the title Dr. through his death. The surviving evidence does not establish a specialty, hospital appointment, public-health office, military medical service, or medical-society office. Polk's 1896 medical directory omitted him from its Quitman list, but that negative cannot overcome the county register, census, Jefferson roll, JAMA notice, and repeated contemporary identification.[4-6][10]

    Medicine was only one part of Hunter's working life. An 1893 political roster described him as a farmer. The 1910 census recorded "own income," and his death certificate gave farming as his occupation. A 1911 agricultural-train report placed him among Quitman's large farmers.[1][11][20]

    Marriage and children

    The original marriage book resolves a repeated date conflict. The license for Theophilus W. Hunter and Fannie C. Rountree was issued on November 14, 1878. The minister certified that he married them on November 17. Later family material copied the license date as the wedding date, but the original return establishes the ceremony date.[9]

    The 1900 census lists four children, all then living:

    • Walter Rountree Hunter, born in September 1881, later a bank clerk and the husband of Emma De V. McCall;
    • John M. Hunter, born in October 1884;
    • Mary E. Hunter, born in June 1887; and
    • Mattie Louise Hunter, born in August 1889, later the wife of Thomas Oliver Marshall.[10-12]

    The 1910 manuscript says that Fannie had borne four children and that three were living. Ancestry transcribes the last figure as two, but the original image clearly reads three. Walter, Mary, and Mattie were in the household, while John had died in 1904.[11]

    John M. Hunter was killed in a street duel with Mercer Baggs at Bainbridge on February 27, 1904. Contemporary reports identify him directly as Dr. Hunter's son. At the commitment hearing, Dr. Hunter and Walter Hunter appeared as the victim's father and brother. These reports keep the event with the correct generation and distinguish John from his surviving brother.[18]

    Newspaper reports place Mattie at Agnes Scott in 1906 and 1908. Her 1916 wedding report says her father gave her in marriage to Thomas O. Marshall. Fannie's 1938 death certificate and obituary identify her as Dr. Hunter's widow and confirm the surviving family chain.[12][19-20]

    Land, timber, and the 1901 sale

    The 1890 Quitman District tax digest records Theophilus W. Hunter with 723 acres valued at $2,100. It also lists extensive Hunter family holdings on the same page. The tax entry is a dated assessment, not a complete deed history.[13]

    In October 1901, Oglesby & Brother and E. C. Bridges bought Hunter's property about six miles northwest of Quitman. A Savannah Morning News report reprinted from the Quitman Free Press described about 4,500 acres and consideration above $35,000. The transfer included substantial virgin timber, productive farm land, mules, wagons, farm equipment, and supplies.[14]

    A shorter report named only Oglesby Brothers and said the buyers planned a mill and tramroad to the timber tract. The fuller buyer list includes E. C. Bridges. The proposed tramroad belonged to the purchasers' timber operation and does not prove a railroad investment or operating role for Hunter.[14]

    No deed image was located. The exact conveyance date, recorded acreage, land lots, deed book and page, and division of the purchase among the buyers remain unresolved. The newspapers verify the reported scale and economics, not the legal description.

    Public education and community activity

    Huxford says the March 1894 town election placed Hunter on Quitman's school board. Contemporary reporting in March 1896 says he was already serving and was renominated with the old board. In March 1900 he appeared on the old ticket but did not finish among the five nominees. A March 1902 report again named him to a slate whose nomination was described as equivalent to election. The evidence supports service before 1896, defeat in 1900, and return in 1902, but not uninterrupted tenure.[6][15]

    An 1893 political roster listed Hunter among Brooks County supporters of Clement A. Evans. That is evidence of a stated political preference, not elective office or party leadership. No inspected record establishes municipal or county office beyond the school board, a church office, fraternal membership, or medical-association office.[16]

    The Baptist pastor W. M. Anderson conducted Hunter's funeral. The minister's role does not by itself prove that Hunter was a Baptist member.[17]

    South Georgia Railroad

    Hunter helped raise subscriptions for the South Georgia Railroad. Huxford says that on January 29, 1896 he joined Mitchell Brice, Fleming Bates Walker, Silas M. Young, and a man printed as James W. Hitch on the county-at-large committee. The identity evidence makes John William Hitch the strong-probable committee member. The committee reported the required money raised five days later.[6]

    His role was subscription organizing, not incorporation or governance. Hunter was not among the twenty-six charter petitioners in Huxford's list and was not among the first directors or officers in the contemporary organization report. No stock ledger, subscription amount, share count, railroad office, employment record, or construction role was found. The committee service is strong probable because it rests on Huxford's detailed retrospective account.[6][21]

    Illness, death, funeral, and burial

    Hunter became ill in December 1919 and spent about ten days under treatment in Atlanta. His death certificate says he died at St. Joseph's Infirmary on December 13. It records arteriosclerosis and cerebral hemorrhage, with diabetes mellitus and chronic nephritis also written in the cause section. Son W. R. Hunter supplied the personal information.[1]

    The Atlanta Constitution called him a prominent Quitman physician and named his wife Fannie and surviving children Walter, Mary, and Mrs. Thomas Marshall. The Atlanta Journal reported that his body reached Quitman on Sunday morning and that the funeral took place Sunday afternoon, conducted by W. M. Anderson.[17]

    The death certificate orders burial or removal to Quitman. Hunter's photographed marker reads:

    T. W. HUNTER M.D.
    SEPT. 21, 1852
    DEC. 13, 1919

    Find a Grave places the photographed marker in West End Cemetery. Fannie's original 1938 death certificate independently names West End Cemetery for her burial. These records make Quitman burial verified and West End Cemetery strong probable for Hunter, although no cemetery register was located.[1][3][19]

    Record conflicts and limits

    • The full name is Theophilus Williams Hunter. The death certificate writes it in full; Jefferson and JAMA use Theophilus W. The forms Theopilus, Theophelus, and Theopheley are index or compilation variants.
    • The 1880 schedule appears to use middle initial M. The original death certificate and several independent W records control.
    • Jane's maiden surname appears as Bryant and Bryan. Both spellings are preserved.
    • The marriage license was issued November 14, 1878; the ceremony occurred November 17.
    • The 1900 census says four children born and four living. The 1910 manuscript says four born and three living, consistent with John's 1904 death.
    • Hunter was born in Georgia before Brooks County or Quitman existed. Exact birthplace is unresolved.
    • University study is reported in the obituary, but no direct university record was obtained.
    • No probate file, cemetery register, medical specialty, 1901 deed image, or railroad stock record was located in the accessible search.
