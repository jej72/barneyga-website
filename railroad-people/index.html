    The people who organized the South Georgia Railroad

    Back to the South Georgia Railroad narrative

    The railroad emerged through several kinds of work: proposing the financing, raising subscriptions, signing the charter petition, governing the corporation, and managing its first operations. The same person sometimes filled more than one role, but the roles were not interchangeable. These dossiers state what the record supports for each participant and leave gaps unresolved when the evidence runs out.

    Initial promoters and financing

    • James Wood Oglesby Sr.: Oglesby Brothers financing proposal; founding director; first president
    • Zenas Wise Oglesby Sr.: Oglesby Brothers financing proposal; founding director; first superintendent

    • John W. Hopson: Quitman committee; incorporator; founding director; first vice president

    Why the roster names James W. Hitch and two McCalls

    Huxford names James W. Hitch on the January subscription committee and in the county-commissioner roster. His own index assigns the commissioner page to John W. Hitch, and his errata corrects the railroad director from James to John. Contemporary reports identify the commissioner, Morven businessman, and director only as J. W. Hitch. The full record chain makes John the strong-probable committee member too, but that last identification cannot be verified until the original January report or another primary record is found. The site therefore has one full dossier, John William Hitch, rather than separate James and John biographies.

    The McCalls are not a printing problem. Dr. James H. McCall, the charter incorporator, and John Goldwire McCall, the founding director, were brothers with different public careers.
