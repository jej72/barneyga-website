    John Tillman

    Back to the Railroad Organizers index

    John Tillman (April 4, 1845 to August 6, 1899) was a Quitman merchant, hardware and farm-supply dealer, planter, county commissioner, Baptist lay leader, Confederate veteran, and the first treasurer of the South Georgia Railroad. He was not among the charter petitioners or first directors identified in the surviving evidence. The first board elected him treasurer on March 23, 1896, and by April 1899 he was serving as secretary and treasurer.[1-2][16-19]

    The name requires care. Judge Joseph Tillman, John’s sons Charles Thomas and Joseph Bruce Tillman, and several earlier or later men named John Tillman had separate careers. The railroad treasurer is securely identified because his contemporary obituary joins the railroad office, Quitman hardware business, Baptist membership, and former county chairmanship in one account.[14-15]

    Family and early years

    Tillman’s marker gives April 4, 1845 as his birth date. Folks Huxford places his birth in Tattnall County and identifies his parents as Joseph Isaiah Tillman and Casandra C. Everett Tillman. John was reportedly one month old when his father died. His mother later moved the family to Thomas County, where her brother Josiah Everett lived.[1-2]

    Huxford names John’s full siblings as Eliza Everett, Casandra, and Joseph Isaiah Tillman, with James M. and Henry Tillman as paternal half-brothers. This is a coherent compiled family account, but no original birth, probate, or census image inspected for this dossier independently proves the whole household.[1]

    Huxford says John entered Mercer University at Penfield at age fifteen and belonged to the Phi Delta Literary Society. The Civil War interrupted his studies, and he did not return after the war. No Mercer matriculation register, contemporary catalog entry, or society roll naming him was located, so the Mercer account remains strong probable rather than verified.[1]

    No original 1860, 1870, or 1880 census image was located for this dossier. Household composition, census occupation wording, property values, and residence on each enumeration date therefore remain unverified by census.

    Confederate service

    The safest military identification is John Tillman of Quitman in the Piscola Volunteers. Huxford’s company roster places that unit as Company I, 26th Georgia Infantry. It records Tillman’s enlistment on July 23, 1861 and reenlistment on May 1, 1862.[1]

    Huxford’s separate family sketch gives August 2, 1861 as the enlistment date and says Tillman served without a wound, was paroled in April 1865, and then held the rank of second sergeant. Those details were not independently closed with the compiled service file, parole, or pension packet. The roster and biography agree that he served with the Piscola Volunteers, but they conflict on the enlistment date and only the later sketch supplies his ending rank.[1]

    His Find a Grave biography labels the unit Company C, 29th Georgia Cavalry. That conflicts with Huxford’s roster and should not be treated as settled without the original military records. Confederate service with the Piscola Volunteers is strong probable. The exact regiment throughout the war, the August date, second-sergeant rank, and April 1865 parole remain unresolved at the original-record level.[1-2]

    Marriages and children

    Tillman first married Mary Lawson Wyche. Huxford gives December 23, 1868 as the wedding date and identifies her parents as John S. and Hannah MacIntyre Wyche of Cherry Lake, Florida. Mary’s memorial links John as her husband, gives 1844 to 1871, and includes a marker photograph, but no original marriage image was found.[1][3]

    An original Brooks County bond dated July 5, 1873 records Tillman as the court-appointed trustee for Mary Lawson Tillman and her children under a deed from John S. Wyche. It proves that fiduciary appointment, not that Tillman practiced law.[26]

    Their first son, John Tillman Jr., was born November 22, 1869 and died July 28, 1870. Charles Thomas Tillman was born June 5, 1871. Mary died on November 2, 1871, shortly after Charles’s birth. The relationships and dates are strong probable from the compiled history and cemetery network.[1][3]

    An original Thomas County marriage-book image verifies Tillman’s second marriage. The license for John Tillman and C. M. Gaulden was issued November 28, 1873. Minister C. D. Campbell married them on November 29, and the record was entered February 27, 1874. The record proves Thomas County, not specifically Thomasville.[4]

    Huxford identifies the bride as Charlie Gaulden, daughter of Rev. Charles S. Gaulden and Charlotte LeSueur Gaulden. Her memorial uses Charlie Mary Gaulden Tillman and gives April 7, 1847 to March 2, 1925.[1][5]

    John and Charlie’s children were:

    • Ela Groover Tillman, who married James L. Key of Atlanta;
    • Joseph Bruce Tillman, who married Carrie Denmark; and
    • Marie Tillman, who married Herbert Rhodes.[1]

    Charles Thomas and Joseph Bruce later became prominent in Quitman business. Charles also held later railroad offices. Those careers belong to the sons and should not be assigned to John.[1][25]

    Merchant and hardware dealer

    In 1866 Tillman joined J. B. Creech and his brother-in-law M. J. Culpepper in Culpepper, Creech & Company, general merchants. Huxford calls him the managing partner. The partnership dissolved January 1, 1871, and Tillman continued the business under his own name.[1]

    Contemporary advertising shows the range of that business. A January 1873 advertisement offered plows, steel farm tools, cotton seed, cotton planters, and guano distributors. In 1877 his store advertised dry goods, boots, shoes, hats, hardware, crockery, plows, and farming implements.[7-8]

    Huxford says Tillman sold the non-hardware stock to W. T. Gaulden & Company in March 1883. He then concentrated on hardware, building supplies, and farm equipment. Huxford’s 1889 and 1897 business lists identify his hardware store, and a February 1890 report says he was extending the building and installing a glass and iron front.[1][10]

    The 1885 bird’s-eye map of Quitman lists "John Tillman, Hardware" in its business references. The map is useful evidence of the store’s presence, though it is not a business ledger or property survey.[9]

    An 1896 national feature described Tillman as one of three wealthy Quitman agricultural-implement dealers. It said he owned a plantation and quoted him on the importance of a practical cotton-picking machine. The article proves plantation ownership and the implement trade, but gives no acreage, legal description, crop schedule, or labor record.[12]

    The November 1897 Quitman fire threatened Tillman’s building, but the contemporary reports place it among the structures saved. His August 1899 obituary called his operation the largest hardware business in Quitman. That was the newspaper’s contemporary assessment, not an audited market ranking.[13-15]

    After John’s death, Joseph Bruce bought the other heirs’ interests and continued the hardware company. Charles Thomas developed a separate record in railroads, lumber, banking, education, and civic work. A 1910 profile discussed both sons and the old family business, but it does not prove that John held their later offices.[1][25]

    Manufacturing, hotel, agriculture, and local development

    Tillman had corporate treasury experience before the railroad. Huxford identifies him as treasurer of the Brooks County Manufacturing Association when it reorganized Quitman’s cotton factory in 1875. The association and the later South Georgia Railroad were separate companies.[1]

    He was one of the charter members of the Quitman Hotel Company in May 1889. The company developed the Hotel Marie. Tillman was not listed among its first directors or executive officers, so charter membership should not be expanded into management.[1]

    In February 1890 he joined Clayton Groover and R. C. McIntosh on a committee raising Quitman’s requested $15,000 subscription for a cotton compress. The report does not state Tillman’s personal investment or prove that the project became affiliated with the railroad.[11]

    The surviving record supports a merchant-planter with interests in manufacturing and community development. It does not supply a complete deed chain, farm acreage, probate inventory, bank account, or personal investment schedule. No evidence found identifies him as a bank officer or director.[1][12]

    Town and county service

    Huxford’s municipal tables place Tillman among Quitman’s town commissioners in 1870, councilmen in 1875 and 1876, and councilmen again in 1882. The 1876 result is independently visible in the local newspaper’s election report. The complete minute books were not inspected, so the year-by-year municipal record is strong probable.[1][6]

    The November 1880 grand jury recommended Tillman for the proposed Brooks County Board of Commissioners. The General Assembly created the board in 1881 and named him to it. He served from 1881 through 1894.[1]

    Tillman became chairman in 1887 after the service of James O. Morton and Clayton R. Denmark. Huxford says he held the chair until resigning in 1894 and spent considerable time supervising the courthouse-remodeling contract. The 1899 obituary independently remembers him as a former chairman, but no complete set of board minutes was reviewed for this dossier.[1][14-15]

    No evidence found places Tillman in the Georgia House, Georgia Senate, a state constitutional convention, or statewide office. Judge Joseph Tillman and other public figures of the same surname must not be merged into his record.

    Baptist and civic work

    Tillman was a Quitman Baptist deacon by September 1878, when he was assigned to open a Mercer Baptist Association discussion about churches meeting their obligations to pastors. In October 1886 he was named among Quitman’s delegates to the association.[20-21]

    Huxford says he served for years as senior deacon and Sunday school superintendent and sometimes represented the association at the Georgia Baptist Convention and the state convention at the Southern Baptist Convention. The deacon and superintendent claims fit the contemporary record. The higher-convention service was not closed with dated delegate lists.[1]

    His civic activity ranged beyond business and officeholding. In 1873 he joined a local committee supporting an inland waterway proposal. After a Black school was burned in 1885, Tillman spoke at a biracial public meeting that condemned the attack and supported a plan for a replacement school on ten acres within Quitman. The report does not say he donated the land or signed the later petition. His home also hosted a literary club meeting in April 1886.[22-24]

    These episodes establish participation in local civic life. They do not support a claim that Tillman held a school-board office, personally funded the school, or led the waterway project.

    South Georgia Railroad

    John Tillman was not one of the twenty-six charter petitioners named by Huxford. He also was not among the nine first directors named in two contemporary reports. His name appears separately in the officer slate elected by those directors.[1][16-17]

    South Georgia Railroad stockholders met at Quitman City Hall on March 23, 1896, accepted the charter, adopted bylaws, and elected the board. The directors then chose James W. Oglesby as president, John W. Hopson as vice president, John Tillman as treasurer, and Zenas W. Oglesby as superintendent. The Savannah and Atlanta reports independently verify Tillman as the first treasurer.[16-17]

    In April 1899 the directors reelected the existing officers. The report calls Tillman secretary and treasurer, showing that his title had expanded by then. Huxford says he remained treasurer until his death and had become a stockholder when the company was organized.[1][18]

    The stockholder claim is strong probable, not verified. No stock certificate, subscription entry, stock ledger, payment record, share count, or voting percentage was found. Election as treasurer does not by itself prove ownership.[1]

    Tillman died on Sunday, August 6, 1899. Four days later the directors held a called meeting and appointed his eldest surviving son, Charles Thomas Tillman, as treasurer. This verifies that John still held the office at death. Continuous service between the dated elections is strong probable because no complete annual minute series was located.[14-15][19]

    No evidence found names John Tillman as a South Georgia Railroad director, president, vice president, superintendent, general manager, or charter petitioner. Those are distinct roles, several of which later belonged to his son Charles or to other organizers.[1][16-19]

    Death and burial

    Tillman died in Quitman on August 6, 1899 after an illness of several months. The newspaper reported dropsy, a historical term for fluid accumulation, as the cause. It should not be converted into a modern diagnosis without medical records.[14-15]

    The obituary called him fifty-three, but his marker dates make him fifty-four. The date-specific marker and Huxford agree, so the obituary’s age is contradicted.[1-2][14-15]

    The funeral was held at Quitman Baptist Church, with Rev. W. B. Bennet and Rev. R. D. Harris of Thomasville officiating. The local dispatch said Quitman’s business houses closed during the services. Tillman was buried in West End Cemetery, where his photographed marker reads "BORN APRIL 4, 1845" and "DIED AUGUST 6, 1899".[2][14-15]

    Record conflicts and limits

    • Birth and death: the marker and Huxford agree on April 4, 1845 and August 6, 1899. The obituary’s age fifty-three conflicts with those dates.
    • Census: no original 1860, 1870, or 1880 image was located. Household and property details are not census-verified.
    • Mercer: attendance and Phi Delta membership come from Huxford. No contemporary Mercer record naming him was located.
    • Military: Piscola Volunteers service is well supported. Huxford’s roster and family sketch conflict on the enlistment date, while the memorial gives a different regiment. Original service and pension files remain unchecked.
    • First marriage: Mary Lawson Wyche and the family are well supported by Huxford and linked memorials, but the exact marriage date lacks an inspected original record.
    • Second marriage: C. M. Gaulden, Thomas County, November 29, 1873 is verified by the original marriage-book image.
    • Business: the merchant, hardware, farm-supply, and plantation record is supported. No complete ownership, acreage, employee, or probate account was assembled.
    • Public office: town and county roles are supported. No state legislative, judicial, or banking office was found.
    • Railroad: first treasurer, 1899 secretary-treasurer, and service at death are verified. Stockholding is strong probable. No director, president, or charter-petitioner role is supported.

    Research note

    This dossier was completed August 22, 2026. Research covered the existing South Georgia Railroad collection, Huxford’s county history and military roster, an original marriage-book image, an original trustee bond, photographed cemetery markers, historic business advertising, the 1885 Quitman map, Georgia Historic Newspapers, Newspapers.com material, Find a Grave, Mercer-related catalog searches, and prior Quitman and Tillman family research. Census originals, the complete Confederate service file, the widow’s pension packet, the first marriage image, the railroad stock ledger, and complete company minutes were not located or inspected. Claims dependent on those records remain graded rather than filled by inference.
