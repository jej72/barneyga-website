    Historical Timeline

    Pre-Railroad Era

    1814-1820 - The Treaty of Fort Jackson and later Creek cessions open the region to Georgia settlement. Georgia creates Irwin County, surveys the land into districts and lots, and distributes much of it through the 1820 land lottery.
    1821-1822 - James Rountree, Lawrence Folsom, Drew Vickers, and Alfred Belote move their families into what became Lowndes County, just before the Coffee Road era.
    1822-1823 - Coffee Road is authorized and opened, becoming the main route into the region for later settlers.
    1824-1826 - Washington Joyce settles on Little River; Sion Hall's Coffee Road place and Hamilton W. Sharpe's store become early landmarks near present-day Morven.
    23 December 1825 - Georgia creates Lowndes and Thomas counties. Land later placed in Brooks County is divided between them.
    1828-1829 - Later accounts place the beginning of Mount Zion's church and camp-meeting tradition in this period. The exact founding year remains disputed.
    1830s - Later local history says William "Uncle Billy" Folsom settled on land about two miles north of present-day Barney.
    11 December 1858 - Georgia creates Brooks County from parts of Lowndes and Thomas counties. Public business is initially conducted at Thomas Folsom's home while Quitman is selected as the county seat.
    1889 - Lowndes County considers separate public roads from Hahira to Miller Bridge and Folsom Bridge. Appointed commissioners examine and lay out both routes.
    1890 - Foster post-office location paper documents Thomas A. Rozier's proposed office; Isom also applies for a nearby Brooks County post office. McNeill & Rozier begin opening the Folsom Bridge route from Hahira.
    1892 - McEachern post-office location paper documents another nearby proposed office; Rozier, Connolly & Company establishes a turpentine still at Foster. Local reports call the Folsom Bridge route McNeill Road by December.
    1892-1894 - Valdosta Times "Foster Notes" columns document Foster's naval-stores work, including the July 7, 1892 Brooks County Alliance meeting at the Gornto school house west of Foster, plus Hahira hauling, doctors, social life, and neighborhood ties.
    1894-1897 - The "Foster Notes" column drops out after March 24, 1894, but Foster remains in the known newspaper record until a July 13, 1897 Valdosta Times reference; that same year, the Foster-to-Barney post-office change and new railroad station mark the settlement's shift toward Barney.

    Railroad Arrival and Town Founding

    1896 - South Georgia Railroad organized, with the Oglesbys and local Quitman, Morven, and Brooks County investors forming the company; Huxford names Fleming Bates Walker on the county-at-large subscription committee and among the charter petition's incorporators.
    26 July 1897 - Thomas A. Rozier is appointed postmaster at Barney in the corrected U.S. postmaster appointment ledger; Huxford says the Roziers built the first residence associated with the new railroad town, while local history says a metal commissary building was moved from Foster by mules and rolled on logs.
    October 1897 - The Valdosta Times reports Oglesby & Bro.'s Heartpine and Barney mills running full time on a large passenger-car-sill order for Ohio Falls Company, Pullman, and Barney & Smith.
    13 October 1898 - Georgia Archives' militia district index records Brooks County Militia District 1571, Barney, as created on this date.
    1899 - Mitchell Brice's seven-mile industrial tram is operating west from Barney to his Tallokas-area mills and turpentine still. A separate report describes an Oglesby timber branch planned east or southeast from Barney, although its completion remains unproved.

    Early Growth

    1899 - Huxford says Barney Methodist Church is organized with trustees John Berryhill, T. A. Rozier, R. L. Hodges, C. W. Ryall, and E. C. Bridges; newspaper evidence also places school activity at Barney by this year.
    1900 - Barney Baptist Church established; Militia District 1571 census enumeration counts 992 residents.
    1901 - Huxford says the first public school building is completed in May; before completion, W. R. Smith teaches in the Methodist Church, and later newspaper evidence names Prof. B. H. Culbreth as principal.
    21 July 1903 - The Valdosta Times reports a new Reedy Creek bridge on the Barney road and planned work at Wells Mill Creek, with Brooks and Lowndes County commissioners sharing the cost.
    4 August 1903 - The Valdosta Times tax-return report lists Barney district at $140,730 in 1903 taxable values, an increase of $17,426.
    6 August 1903 - Georgia Act No. 292 incorporates the town of Barney, setting the corporate limits at one half mile in every direction from the South Georgia Railway depot and creating a mayor-and-five-councilmen municipal government.
    29 September 1903 - The Valdosta Times, reprinting the Quitman Free Press, reports that the South Georgia Railroad bridge across Little River above Barney has washed away, with trains running to Barney while through schedules and mail service are disrupted.
    24 October 1903 - The Atlanta Journal reports that Tobe G. Robinson has been appointed postmaster at Barney, Brooks County, replacing Thomas A. "Roseir," the newspaper's spelling for what is likely Thomas A. Rozier.
    26-27 December 1903 - Atlanta and Savannah newspapers report from Quitman that Sheriff Connolly and a posse were sent toward Barney after Perry Peeples killed Ed Cook and a threatened racial conflict was feared; the follow-up report says the posse found things quiet at Barney.
    January and June 1904 - Contemporary notices identify Sion L. Hall as mayor of Barney. With the charter's first election scheduled for December 1903, Hall is strongly supported as the town's first mayor.
    12-15 April 1904 - Newspapers report that Curtis Webb killed Tobe Robinson Jr. at Barney after a horse-trade dispute. Webb surrendered and was jailed at Quitman; no later case outcome has been located.
    18 April 1904 - The corrected U.S. postmaster appointment ledger records Blackmon H. Culbreth appointed postmaster at Barney.
    30 April 1904 - Brooks County temporary letters of administration name Tobias G. Robinson as deceased and appoint Mrs. Laura J. Robinson to collect and care for his estate.
    15 November 1904 - Rural free delivery begins at Barney. The 1908 Brooks County map later shows routes 1 and 2 extending into the surrounding countryside.
    10 May 1905 - The Atlanta Journal reports rural-carrier appointments for Barney route 2, naming Walter E. Westberry carrier and Moses Westberry substitute.
    11 May 1905 - The corrected U.S. postmaster appointment ledger records Andrew W. Voight appointed postmaster at Barney.
    9 June 1905 - The Moultrie Observer reports that Will Womack shot and seriously wounded his wife at H. D. Woolard's turpentine place near Barney, then killed himself. Her later condition is unknown.
    24 September 1905 - The Atlanta Journal reports Citizens Bank organized at Adel and says the bank will have a branch bank at Barney.
    15 December 1905 - Barney's annual municipal election selects Mayor W. E. Hiers and councilmen W. B. Greeson, W. E. Westbury, J. M. McMullan, Turner Folsom, and Steve Collins, according to a transcript of the 16 December 1905 Valdosta Times in the James E. Jones collection.
    22 December 1905 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports that Perry Peeples was acquitted at Quitman after charges brought by some Barney citizens and the town of Barney failed at trial.
    30 December 1905 - The corrected U.S. postmaster appointment ledger records Moses W. Wesberry appointed postmaster at Barney.
    1905-1906 - Newspaper coverage places Barney & Smith in the Milltown/Naylor/Valdosta timber-and-railroad discussion, including sawmill operations, proposed rail extension work, and possible South Georgia car shops.
    18 February 1906 - The Atlanta Journal prints [Fleming Bates Walker's](railroad-people/fleming-bates-walker.md) dispatch from Barney district saying he had polled fifteen men at his store in the governor's race and all supported Hoke Smith. The newspaper identified him as “F. B. Walker.”
    20 February 1906 - The corrected U.S. postmaster appointment ledger records Thomas A. Rozier appointed postmaster at Barney again.
    2 April 1906 - The Atlanta Journal lists Gilbert Wayne Nicholson of Barney, Georgia, among Atlanta College of Physicians and Surgeons graduates in medicine.
    25 June 1906 - The Atlanta Georgian lists a Bank of Barney representative for a meeting of banks associated with W. S. Witham, confirming that the bank was operating by this date.
    3 September 1906 - A brief report says a Black man known as “Big Kid” was killed at Barney and Pete Crawley was arrested. The victim's name and the case outcome remain unknown.
    28 September 1906 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports a fire at the Walker & Moody general-merchandise store at Barney, with damage estimated around $1,500, mostly from smoke and water.
    4 October 1906 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports that surveys for three possible Milltown-to-Valdosta railroad routes have been completed for N. E. Whitehurst, manager of the Barney-Smith railway interests.
    6 October 1906 - The Brice estate sells several miles of rail from the Tallokas-to-Barney tram for reuse south of Quitman, showing that the tram was being dismantled or had already ceased operation.
    24 January 1907 - A southbound freight train strikes a coal car at Barney. Three cars and the caboose derail; no one is injured, but traffic is delayed.
    4 August 1907 - The Macon Telegraph reports that Revs. Adams and Allaben were conducting a meeting at Barney and that about a dozen people had united with the Methodist church.
    9 August 1907 - The Valdosta Daily Times names B. Walker and W. R. Blease on the Barney district committee for the Farmers' Institute picnic at Quitman.
    16 March 1908 - The Valdosta Daily Times lists W. R. Bleas of Barney on the Brooks County Democratic Executive Committee.
    24 April 1908 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports that P. Feingold's dry-goods store at Barney burned after only about two weeks in business.
    December 1908 - A newspaper profile describes Barney's bank, hotel, gin, warehouse, turpentine plant, school, churches, blacksmith shops, and expanding brick business district. Its business-volume claims were promotional estimates.
    February 1909 - The Bankers Magazine reports that the Bank of Barney changed its title to Barney Banking Company.
    31 May 1909 - Barney's baseball team defeats Morven 14 to 8.
    5 July 1909 - With no telegraph office in Barney, the South Georgia Railroad depot relays a paid Western Union message to Quitman over the railroad's private telephone line.
    13 August 1909 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports that the gins at Morven and Barney were running full time during the early sea-island cotton season.
    13 May 1910 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports that Gordon Chambers, who lived near Barney, had been kicked by a mule and that his family in Valdosta received word by telegram.
    20-21 May 1910 - Hoke Smith gives an education address at Barney, introduced by Dr. S. S. Gaulden, during a school-grounds picnic attended by many out-of-town people.
    3 June 1910 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports the death of James Walker of Barney, a teenager and former depot agent there.
    6 August 1910 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports a shooting at Barney in which J. W. Watkins was killed and two others were wounded.
    3 and 24 September 1910 - Valdosta and Quitman farm advertisements market Barney District land using roads, rail access, churches, schoolhouse, business houses, and rising Brooks County land values as selling points.
    21 November 1910 - The Valdosta Daily Times reports the Barney marriage of Elias Chancey and Alberta Simms at the home of Mr. and Mrs. D. F. Parker.
    1910 census - The surviving schedules document individual Barney households and occupations, but no dependable precinct-wide population total has yet been established from the full set of pages.
    November-December 1911 - Barney residents E. H. Beck, T. A. Rozier, and Fleming Bates Walker join organizers seeking a charter for the Georgia, Alabama and Western Railroad, proposed from Milltown through Barney to the Alabama line.
    25 January-June 1912 - Georgia charters the Georgia, Alabama and Western. Organizers pursue subscriptions, rights of way, preliminary surveys, and a larger Brunswick-to-Dothan route, but no verified operating railroad results.
    1915 - Boll weevil infestation begins damaging the sea-island cotton industry.
    17 February 1916 - The corrected U.S. postmaster appointment ledger records Paris Culpepper appointed postmaster at Barney.
    1920 - Population reaches historical peak of 1,651 residents.

    Development Period

    1923 - Lloyd Greer designed school opened, "most modern rural school building in the state."
    1923-1925 - Brooks and Cook counties build the concrete Rock Bridge over Little River. A separate concrete slough bridge fails during the January 1925 flood, while the main river bridge survives.
    1926 - School recognized as "best model school in the state."
    1920s - Georgia's banking crisis restricts rural credit and access to capital.
    1920s-1930s - Turpentine production declines as the surrounding pine forests are cut over.
    August 1928 - Floodwater washes away the Brooks County approaches to Rock Bridge, but contemporary reports say the main bridge remains open.

    Peak Period

    1930 - Population stable at 1,640 despite early Depression effects.
    1930s - Community reaches peak development with state championship athletics and continued educational recognition.

    Decline Period

    1930s - Automobile travel reduces dependence on railroad transportation.
    1940 - Population declines to 1,249 during Great Depression economic impacts.
    1941-1945 - Most able-bodied men leave for World War II military service.
    1947 - An Atlanta Journal investigation calls the temporary wooden slough crossing beside the old Rock Bridge concrete wreckage dangerously unsafe.
    1949-1950 - State Route 76 is rebuilt and paved through the area. A new 1,473-foot Little River bridge opens on a relocated alignment, bypassing old Rock Bridge.
    1950 - Population drops to 810, showing continued post-war decline.
    1995 - Georgia General Assembly officially revokes town charter along with many other communities.
