    James Groover¶

    James Groover appears as a household head in Thomas County in 1830 and in the Grooversville district in 1840.[1][2] Huxford identified him as a son of John Groover and Hannah Lastinger, born in 1798, and said that he married Elizabeth Denmark in Bulloch County in 1820. Those family details fit the later James and Elizabeth household, but the original marriage record and a direct parent-naming record have not been inspected.[3][4]

    Arrival and later households¶

    The date of James's move into the future Brooks County area is unresolved. Huxford's early-settler roster gives 1823. The Groover family history in the same book says James, his father John, and his uncle Solomon came in 1826, citing a Liberty Baptist Church record book. The 1830 census confirms James's residence by that year, but it cannot settle the earlier conflict. Until the church book or another contemporary record is found, 1826 is best treated as a secondary-supported family tradition rather than a verified arrival date.[4]

    Two James Groover households appear among the preserved 1850 Thomas County candidates. The James and Elizabeth household is the stronger match to the reported 1798 settler, but the census images alone do not establish that identity.[3][5]

    Connection to Barney¶

    James lived in the Dixie, Piscola Creek, and Grooversville part of the future county. No inspected record places him at the later Barney townsite.

    His family nevertheless connects to a later Barney Jones line through a collateral branch. Huxford identified James's father John and Charles Groover as brothers. Compiled Groover and Rieser family work identifies Charles's daughter as Sarah Reiser Groover. Walter C. Jones's 1913 biographical sketch names Sarah “Reissier” Groover as the mother of M. Franklin Jones, whose household is documented in Barney in 1900.[4][6][7]

    On that reconstruction, Sarah and James were first cousins. The Jones line from Sarah through M. Franklin Jones and Edgar Jelks Jones is well supported, while Sarah's parentage and the older Groover sibling link remain dependent on compiled family histories. This is a documented family connection to Barney, but it does not place James himself in the town.

    Evidence status¶

    • Verified: a James Groover household in Thomas County in 1830.
    • Supported: James Groover in the Grooversville district in 1840.
    • Strong probable: the reported 1798 identity and the later James and Elizabeth household.
    • Secondary-supported: the 1826 move and the reported marriage to Elizabeth Denmark.
    • Strong secondary: the collateral connection through James's reported first cousin Sarah Reiser Groover to the Barney Jones family.
    • Unresolved: the 1823 arrival claim, the two 1850 namesakes, and any direct Barney connection.
