    John Hendry of Liberty, Lowndes, and Hamilton Counties

    Back to the Early History Profiles index

    Research scope and conclusion

    This report concerns the elder John Hendry who lived in Liberty County, Georgia, before the Lowndes County settlement era; appeared beside William Hendry in early Lowndes records; helped occupy one of the first tents at Mount Zion; and died after moving to Hamilton County, Florida.

    The evidence supports a strong-probable identity chain from Liberty County in 1820 to Lowndes County in 1830, 1840, and 1850. Original Hamilton County probate records then prove that a John Hendry senior was dead by February 16, 1859 and that a younger John Hendry administered his estate.[1-6,12-13] The Florida identification as the former Lowndes settler is strong probable rather than absolute because no single record states both places.

    Several familiar details remain family tradition. The exact December 1, 1778 birth, December 13, 1808 marriage to Catherine McFail, nine-child list, December 1858 death, and Hendry Cemetery burial come from later family accounts or a modern memorial, not an original vital, Bible, church, or cemetery record.[19-20] The evidence strongly supports Catherine as his wife, John Jr. as his son, and William Hendry as his brother, but no located original record directly states those relationships.[4,7,9-13,19]

    The record also requires careful namesake control. The younger John's 1840 census age range and the family account place his birth in the 1800s, so he was old enough for militia service in 1832. He had a separate tax row by 1834 and headed a separate census household by 1840. An unsuffixed 1832 militia appointment and an 1885 Salem Church reference cannot be assigned safely to the elder.[3,9-10,16-17,19]

    Birth and Liberty County identity

    The only original record located online that directly states the elder John's birthplace is the 1850 census. It records North Carolina and an age of seventy-two, implying birth about 1777-1778.[4] It does not name a county or give a calendar date.

    The Wilson-Huxford family account says John was born December 1, 1778 in New Hanover County, North Carolina, the oldest son of Robert Hendry and Ann Lee. Find a Grave memorial 41683478 repeats the date and parentage.[19-20] No birth, baptism, Bible, or parental probate record was found online. The state is verified; the exact date, county, and parents remain family-history claims.

    An 1820 Liberty County census page lists John Hendry immediately after William Hendry. John's household contained three males under ten, one male aged ten through fifteen, and one male aged forty-five or older. No woman or enslaved person was recorded, and three people were engaged in agriculture.[1]

    This household is a strong-probable match for the later Lowndes settler because John and William reappear together in Lowndes County and later family history identifies them as brothers. It does not fit the compiled biography cleanly. A man born in December 1778 would have been about forty-one, not forty-five or older. The absence of an adult woman and young daughter also conflicts with the reported Catherine and child list. The schedule may contain an age error, some family members may have been elsewhere, or the entry may concern another John Hendry. The conflict cannot be resolved from the census image.

    Lowndes County households

    1830

    The 1830 Lowndes census lists John immediately after William Hendry. John's household contained ten free white people and no enslaved people:[2]

    • one male under five;
    • two males aged five through nine;
    • one male aged ten through fourteen;
    • two males aged fifteen through nineteen;
    • one male aged twenty through twenty-nine;
    • one male aged fifty through fifty-nine;
    • one female aged ten through fourteen; and
    • one female aged thirty through thirty-nine.

    The oldest man's range implies birth between 1770 and 1780. The household structure closely resembles the later family's reported sequence of children, but the schedule names only the head. It cannot prove the identity or relationship of each resident.

    1840 and the two Johns

    Two John Hendry households appear in the 1840 Lowndes census.[3]

    The elder household on page 238 contained a man aged sixty through sixty-nine, a woman aged fifty through fifty-nine, and five younger males. Four people were engaged in agriculture. No enslaved people were recorded.

    The separate younger household on page 247 contained a man aged thirty through thirty-nine, a woman aged twenty through twenty-nine, and five girls under fifteen. It also recorded no enslaved people. The age and family structure exclude this household from the elder's census chain.

    The 1840 tax digest confirms the distinction more clearly. It labels one entry "John Hendry Sen" and a later entry "John Hendry Jun" on the same page.[10]

    1850

    The 1850 Lowndes population schedule records:[4]

    • John Hendry, 72, born North Carolina, farmer, $40 in real estate;
    • Catharine Hendry, 61, born North Carolina;
    • Ellen C. Martin, 22, born Georgia; and
    • George W. Martin, 18, born Georgia, laborer.

    The census did not state relationships. Catherine is the strong-probable wife because she is the only older woman in the household, later appears with the younger John in Florida, and is identified as the elder John's wife in the family account. Ellen and George Martin should be described only as household members unless another record establishes their relationship.

    The 1850 agricultural schedule separately indexes "John Hendry Sr" on original page 227, line 11. The image is difficult to read column by column, but it independently confirms the senior man's farming operation.[5]

    Catherine and the reported children

    Wilson and Huxford call John's wife Catherine McFail and say they married in Liberty County on December 13, 1808. They give her birth as April 18, 1789 in South Carolina and say she died about 1889 near Fort Green, Florida.[19] The accessible Georgia marriage collection begins in 1828, so it cannot test the reported 1808 marriage. No original marriage return, Bible entry, death record, or cemetery record was located online.

    The 1850 and 1860 censuses support Catherine's identity but disagree on details. The 1850 schedule calls her Catharine, age sixty-one, born in North Carolina.[4] In 1860 Catherine Hendry, age sixty-five and born in South Carolina, lived in Hamilton County, Florida, in the household of the younger John Hendry.[7] The schedules do not state marital or parent-child relationships. Their combined evidence makes Catherine the elder John's strong-probable wife and supports the family migration, while leaving her exact birth and death details unresolved.

    The family account names nine children: John, James Norton, Robert, Judith, Archibald, William McFail, Charles Wesley, Andrew Jackson, and George Washington.[19] The 1830 household is compatible with eight children born before the reported 1832 birth of George, but compatibility is not proof.[2]

    The 1859 estate bond bears the names John Hendry, James N. Hendry, and Charles W. Hendry. The probate order identifies John as administrator of the senior John's estate.[12-13] These are strong kinship connections, but the documents do not call the men sons or heirs. John Jr. is a strong-probable son; James N. and Charles W. are strong-probable close kin. The full child list remains family tradition until each relationship is supported separately.

    John Jr. as the essential identity control

    The younger John appears independently in Lowndes records. A separate "John Hendry Jr" tax row is present in 1834, and the 1840 census and tax digest distinguish his household from the senior man's.[3,9-10]

    In 1850, John Hendry, age forty-one and born in Georgia, headed another Lowndes household. Elizabeth, Amanda, Mary, Nancy, John, Archibald, Priscilla, and Judith shared it. The schedule did not state relationships, so they cannot all be labeled children from census order alone.[4]

    In 1860, a fifty-year-old Georgia-born John Hendry lived in Hamilton County, Florida, with Margaret, Archibald, Penelope, Alfred A., Florida A. J., Priscilla R., and Catherine Hendry.[7] His age fits the younger Lowndes man, not the elder. Catherine's presence helps connect the Lowndes and Hamilton County families, but it does not by itself prove every relationship in the household.

    This younger man is also the likely estate administrator in 1859. The probate court explicitly called the deceased "John Hendry senior", while naming the administrator simply John Hendry.[13]

    William Hendry relationship

    Wilson and Huxford identify John and William Hendry as brothers, sons of Robert Hendry and Ann Lee.[19] Original records repeatedly associate the two men:

    • John immediately follows William in the 1820 Liberty census;[1]
    • John immediately follows William in the 1830 Lowndes census;[2]
    • the 1834 tax digest places their elder entries together;[9]
    • Hamilton W. Sharpe names both among Mount Zion's first tenters.[14]

    This repeated association strongly supports the brotherhood claim. No located deed, probate, Bible, or contemporary statement expressly calls them brothers. The relationship is therefore strong probable, not directly proved.

    Mount Zion

    Hamilton W. Sharpe's April 12, 1884 history says the first Mount Zion camp meeting occurred in fall 1829. Sharpe named the seven original tent occupants as William Blair, William Hendry, John Hendry, Rev. Joshua Platt, H. W. Sharpe, John Pike, and Lawrence Folsom.[14] Sharpe participated in the event, although he wrote about fifty-five years later.

    The original newspaper matters because Huxford altered two details. Sharpe printed fall 1829 and named "William Hardy" on the three-man site committee. Huxford changed the date to 1828 and the committee surname to Hendry. The original paper controls those details. It directly supports John as a first tenter, not as a site-committee member.

    John was not a Mount Zion trustee in either located legal list. Huxford's transcription of the November 8, 1831 deed names William Blair, William Hendry, Lawrence Folsom, James J. Joyce, and William G. Hall.[15] Section 10 of the 1833 incorporation act names William Blair, John Jordan, William Hendry, James J. Jordan, and Randal Folsom. John Hendry appears in neither list.

    Later accounts provide retrospective corroboration. Norman Campbell named William, John, Eli, and James Hendry among the early founders in 1903. Centennial and reprint articles in 1926 and 1941 repeated the local Mount Zion tradition.[21] These later reports do not add proof of kinship or office.

    Salem Church and militia namesakes

    M. M. Folsom wrote in 1885 that "Jesse Giles, John Hendry and other of those eloquent expounders of right and truth" had labored in Salem Church's vineyard.[16] The article gives no date, age, suffix, or office. The elder had an adult same-name son, and Rev. John McFail Hendry was another later local churchman. The passage proves that a John Hendry was remembered at Salem, but it does not identify which one or establish that the elder was a preacher.

    Huxford's officer table lists "John Hendry, Feb. 29, 1832" as second lieutenant in the 790th District militia.[17] The entry has no suffix. John Jr., reportedly born in 1810, was old enough to serve and appears separately in the 1834 tax digest.[9,19] Without the commission, executive-minute entry, or another identifier, the appointment cannot be assigned securely to the elder.

    War of 1812 claim

    The Wilson-Huxford account says John served near Darien during the War of 1812 and that William served with his brother John.[19] Ancestry's service index contains two soldiers of the right name: one private in Pray's 2nd Regiment, Georgia Militia, and another in Nixon's New Hanover Regiment, North Carolina Militia.[18] Neither index entry gives age, residence, spouse, or kin.

    The Georgia soldier is geographically plausible, but no inspected muster roll, payroll, pension file, or bounty-land record ties him to Catherine, Liberty County, Lowndes County, or Hamilton County. Find a Grave instead assigns John to Quarterman's company without attaching an original record.[20] The elder's War of 1812 service is possible, not proved.

    Tax, land, and farming

    The likely 1830 Captain Burnett's District tax row for John reports one poll, 565 acres of pine land, and 125 acres of oak-and-hickory land. It has no enslaved-person count.[8] Because the row is unsuffixed and John Jr. was nearing adulthood, it is a strong-probable elder record. The handwritten parcel columns are not clear enough to support an exact land-lot claim without a second record.

    The 1834 Captain Strickland's District digest lists an unsuffixed John immediately after William Hendry. The row contains one poll, with no land acreage or enslaved-person count entered. A separate "John Hendry Jr" appears later in the same digest, making the unsuffixed entry a strong elder identification.[9]

    The 1840 tax digest directly uses senior and junior labels. The senior entry includes Lowndes land, while the junior entry is separate.[10] The 1850 population and agricultural schedules identify the elder as a farmer.[4-5]

    The Georgia Archives plat index contains two Liberty County entries under the exact name John Hendry: 200 acres in 1801 and 150 acres in 1818.[22] The place and dates make them plausible leads, but the index gives no age, wife, parent, or later residence. Neither parcel can be assigned to this John without the original plat, grant, or conveyance chain.

    No located original or contemporary online record identifies the elder as a grand juror, justice, sheriff, clerk, legislator, road overseer, bridge contractor, deed trustee, or county commissioner. That search result sets the current evidence boundary; it does not prove that he never held an unrecorded or undigitized role.

    Enslaved people

    The 1820, 1830, and 1840 federal census households recorded no enslaved people for the elder John.[1-3]

    The 1850 slave schedule is different. It explicitly names "John Hendry Senr" as the owner of five people:[6]

    • a Black woman whose manuscript age reads thirty-four, although Ancestry indexes thirty-five;
    • a seven-year-old girl marked mulatto;
    • a four-year-old Black boy;
    • a three-year-old Black boy; and
    • a nine-month-old Black girl.

    The original image controls the woman's age. The schedule did not record any of their names or relationships. It proves that the elder John enslaved five people in 1850 but cannot identify them across later records.

    Move to Florida, death, and estate

    The family account says John moved near Jennings, Hamilton County, Florida, about 1855.[19] No original deed or removal record proving that year was found. The probate index and manuscripts prove that a John Hendry senior was a resident of Hamilton County by death, and the younger John and Catherine were there in 1860.[7,11-13]

    On February 16, 1859, John Hendry obtained letters of administration on the estate of "John Hendry deceased late of said county". John, James N. Hendry, and Charles W. Hendry signed an $8,000 bond.[12] The preserved image is a mixed spread: the left page concerns Mary T. Henry, while the right page concerns John Hendry's estate. Reading only the indexed decedent or only one side of the spread would merge two different estates.

    A March 1, 1859 order removes the remaining senior-junior ambiguity. It is headed "In the matter of the estate of John Hendry senior late of said County deceased" and grants administrator John Hendry leave to sell the personal property after notice.[13]

    These records prove that a John Hendry senior of Hamilton County died before February 16, 1859 and that a younger John administered the estate. They do not give a death date, cause, wife, heirs, or relationships among the bond signers. The identification as the former Lowndes settler is strong probable, supported by the complete family migration pattern rather than by a direct prior-residence statement.

    Exact death and burial limits

    Wilson-Huxford says John died in December 1858 in Hamilton County and was buried in Hendry Cemetery near Jennings.[19] Find a Grave memorial 41683478 repeats December 1858 and the cemetery.[20] The memorial shows no photographs, marker inscription, cemetery register, or contemporary burial source.

    The original probate proves only that death occurred before February 16, 1859.[12] No contemporary death notice, funeral record, cemetery register, or photographed marker was found in the documented online searches. December 1858 and the Hendry Cemetery burial are strong derivative traditions, not independently proved facts. The exact day and grave location remain unresolved.

    Chronology

    • About 1777-1778: born in North Carolina according to the 1850 census; exact December 1, 1778 New Hanover claim remains unproved.[4,19]
    • 1808: reported marriage to Catherine McFail in Liberty County; no original marriage return located.[19]
    • 1820: strong-probable Liberty County household immediately after William Hendry; household conflicts with parts of the later family account.[1]
    • About 1825: reported move to Lowndes County; exact year unproved.[19]
    • Fall 1829: named by Hamilton W. Sharpe as one of Mount Zion's seven first tenters.[14]
    • 1830: Lowndes household of ten, beside William, with no enslaved people.[2]
    • 1834: elder and John Jr. separated in the tax digest.[9]
    • 1840: elder and younger households separately enumerated; elder household had no enslaved people.[3,10]
    • 1850: elder was a North Carolina-born Lowndes farmer; the slave schedule recorded five unnamed enslaved people.[4-6]
    • About 1855: reported move near Jennings, Florida; exact year unproved.[19]
    • Before February 16, 1859: death proved by Hamilton County administration bond.[12]
    • March 1, 1859: probate order explicitly called him John Hendry senior and authorized the administrator to sell personal property.[13]
    • 1860: strong-probable widow Catherine lived with the younger John in Hamilton County.[7]

    Search coverage and remaining targets

    No relevant contemporary obituary, death notice, estate advertisement, jury presentment, court notice, deed notice, or road notice for the elder was found in the documented newspaper and local-record searches. Florida newspaper coverage for the reported 1858 death is thin, so the conclusion is "not found online", not "none existed".

    The main unresolved targets are:

    1. an original birth, baptism, Bible, or parental probate record;
    2. the alleged 1808 Liberty County marriage return;
    3. deeds or grants connecting the Liberty and Lowndes properties;
    4. an original Mount Zion membership or camp-meeting record;
    5. the 1832 militia commission or executive-minute entry;
    6. a military file that identifies the War of 1812 soldier by residence or family;
    7. the complete Hamilton County estate file, inventory, sale, distribution, and heirs;
    8. Catherine's later census, death, and burial records; and
    9. a cemetery register, marker photograph, deed, or contemporary burial notice.

    The findings describe the accessible online evidence checked through August 19, 2026. They do not imply that undigitized courthouse, church, cemetery, military, or family records do not exist.
