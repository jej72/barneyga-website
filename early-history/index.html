    Early History Profiles

    Back to the Early Settlers overview

    These 26 profiles collect the evidence for people who shaped the settlement, roads, government, churches, ferries, and public life of the region before Barney became a railroad town. Each profile separates original records from later tradition and leaves disputed details unresolved.

    Georgia government and the Coffee Road

    • Governor John Clark: Georgia governor who appointed John Coffee and Thomas S. Swain to supervise the state road in December 1822
    • General John Coffee: co-superintendent who directed the road work carried out in 1823
    • Thomas S. Swain: co-superintendent, surveyor, ferry operator, and Telfair County public official

    First settlers and pioneer families

    • Alfred Belote: early Lowndes settler, District 12 landholder, and member of the traditional four-family settlement account
    • Jose Bryant: reported Okapilco and Mule Creek settler whose identity and relationship to Joe and John Bryant remain unresolved
    • Drewry “Drew” Vickers: Montgomery and Lowndes County farmer, landholder, enslaver, and member of the four-family settlement tradition
    • James Rountree: early settler, Pulaski-to-Lowndes migrant, and victim of an 1834 killing on the Tallahassee road
    • Lawrence Folsom: documented Irwin County resident and landowner by November 1823, Lowndes county-site commissioner, Mount Zion trustee, and Folsom family patriarch
    • Sion Hall: Coffee Road resident whose home became Lowndes County’s temporary court and election place
    • Washington Joyce: Little River settler and ferryman associated with the later Miller Bridge crossing
    • Amos Emanuel: Lowndes County resident named in the unresolved 1827 proposal for a ferry where Coffee Road crossed Little River
    • Daniel McCranie Sr.: Coffee Road settler and probable commissioner in Lowndes County’s organizing act

    Early county government, roads, courts, militia, and mail

    • Enoch Hall: farmer, mill-associated landholder, Inferior Court justice, legislator, and militia commander
    • Francis Rountree: landholder whose home became the county’s statutory court and election place in 1826
    • Hamilton W. Sharpe: merchant, postmaster, justice, legislator, Methodist lay preacher, planter, and enslaver
    • John J. Underwood: county-site commissioner, Franklinville resident, militia officer, court official, and attorney
    • William Blair: county-site commissioner, Inferior Court justice, state senator, and Mount Zion trustee
    • Henry Blair: first county clerk, sheriff, legislator, militia colonel, farmer, and Methodist preacher
    • William Smith: Franklinville clerk and postmaster who later kept Tranquil Hall at Troupville and Valdosta
    • John Pike: Mount Zion neighbor, militia battalion commander, paymaster, and probable Brushy Creek company captain

    Mount Zion and the early local community

    • James J. Joyce: Mount Zion trustee, Methodist minister, tax collector, court official, landholder, and enslaver
    • William Folsom: early Irwin and Lowndes resident, farmer, Methodist, estate holder, and probable courthouse commissioner
    • John Hendry: Liberty-to-Lowndes migrant, farmer, early Mount Zion tenter, and probable brother of William Hendry
    • William Hendry: Mount Zion trustee and grand juror whose estate held District 12 land near Mule Creek
    • Rev. Joshua Platt: early Mount Zion tenter and Methodist local preacher who later moved to Florida
