    Early History Profiles¶

    These 95 profiles separate people with a demonstrated Barney-area connection from settlers whose evidence belongs to the wider future-Brooks story.

    Roster status describes the historical evidence. Barney relevance describes the connection to Barney: direct influence, a family or settlement bridge, broader county context, or none yet proved. A family bridge does not by itself prove residence at the later townsite.

    Coffee Road figures¶

    These officials directed or authorized the 1822–1823 state road project. They are included as infrastructure context, not as Barney settlers.

    • Governor John Clark — Status: context profile. Authorized the road and appointed its supervisors.
    • General John Coffee — Status: context profile. Surveyed the route and directed the road work carried out in 1823.
    • Thomas S. Swain — Status: context profile. Co-superintendent, surveyor, ferry operator, and public official.

    Barney and immediate-area settlers¶

    These people have documented influence on the later Barney area through roads, land, local institutions, residence, or a supported family and settlement bridge.

    • George Alderman — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: direct area connection. Documented regionally by 1830 and connected to the north-of-Barney Alderman land cluster.
    • Cornelius English — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: secondary-supported descendant bridge. Original records place him locally by 1830; compiled genealogy connects him through Matthew and John English to Andrew James English of Barney.
    • Ebenezer Folsom — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: family bridge. Documented through an 1840–1860 household chain and strong-probable father of Israel Lee Folsom.
    • Lawrence Folsom — Status: verified · Barney relevance: family and institutional bridge. Irwin landowner by 1823, county-site commissioner, Mount Zion trustee, and Folsom patriarch.
    • Priscilla Jane Folsom — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: family bridge. Documented from 1850 through 1900 and as a Brooks landholder; Rogers parentage remains unresolved.
    • William Folsom — Status: verified · Barney relevance: family and institutional bridge. Early resident, farmer, Methodist, estate holder, and probable courthouse commissioner.
    • Joel Gornto — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: immediate-area district connection. His 1836 militia service and 1860 estate are supported by original records.
    • James Groover — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: collateral family bridge. Reported first cousin of Sarah Reiser Groover, mother of Barney resident M. Franklin Jones.
    • John Groover Sr. — Status: traditional/secondary-supported · Barney relevance: collateral family bridge. His reported brother Charles begins the line through Sarah Reiser Groover to the Barney Jones family.
    • Enoch Hall — Status: verified · Barney relevance: land, mill, and family bridge. Farmer, mill-associated landholder, judge, legislator, and militia commander.
    • Sion Hall — Status: verified · Barney relevance: direct settlement influence. Coffee Road resident whose home became Lowndes County’s temporary court and election place.
    • James E. Hendry — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: secondary-supported family bridge. Compiled family history makes him Norman Campbell Hendry’s paternal uncle; Norman’s railroad connection is strong probable, but the James-to-Neal sibling link lacks an inspected parent-naming original.
    • James C. Hodges — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: strong-probable descendant bridge. Robertson or Robinson L. Hodges appeared in James's 1850 and 1860 households and later headed Barney household #8.
    • Nathan Hodges — Status: qualified supplemental · Barney relevance: secondary-supported collateral bridge. Compiled genealogy makes Nathan the brother of Joseph Hodges, ancestor of the principal Barney Hodges branch; no original parent-naming record verifies the upper generation.
    • Rebecca Yates Hiers — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: direct residence and family bridge. Brooks landholder and 1900 Barney resident; strong-probable daughter of William Yates.
    • James J. Joyce — Status: verified · Barney relevance: immediate-area institutional influence. Mount Zion trustee, minister, court official, landholder, and enslaver.
    • John Lawson — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: probable immediate-area land connection. His Barney-district land remains secondary-supported.
    • Angus Morrison — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: direct place connection. A 1937 newspaper identifies the old Angus Morrison home at Barney; the complete family descent remains secondary-supported.
    • James Rountree — Status: verified · Barney relevance: settlement-corridor influence. Early settler and Pulaski-to-Lowndes migrant killed on the Tallahassee road in 1834.
    • Drewry “Drew” Vickers — Status: verified · Barney relevance: settlement and family bridge. Farmer, landholder, enslaver, and member of the four-family settlement tradition.
    • William E. Dean — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: strong-probable family bridge. Robert Dean appeared in William's 1860 household and later headed Barney household #95; no parent-naming record proves the relationship.
    • Ely Webb — Status: traditional/secondary-supported · Barney relevance: family bridge. Probable father of Barney resident Malinda Webb Chambers; his own local residence remains unproved.
    • William Yates — Status: supplemental · Barney relevance: land and family bridge. Northern Brooks landowner and mill namesake linked on strong-probable evidence to Rebecca Yates Hiers.

    Broader future-Brooks settlers¶

    These people are independently documented in territory that became Brooks County, or have a strong evidence-based placement there, but no direct influence on Barney itself has been proved.

    • William Alderman — Status: supplemental. Documented in Lowndes by 1833; the reported 1828 arrival remains unproved.
    • Matthew Allbritton — Status: supplemental. Verified in Lowndes in 1830 and strongly associated with future-Brooks Baptist congregations.
    • Fleming Bates — Status: supplemental. The original 1830 census and a District 12 deed locator establish his local presence; no direct Barney influence is proved.
    • Alfred Belote — Status: verified. Early Lowndes settler, District 12 landholder, and member of the four-family settlement account.
    • Henry Blair — Status: verified. First county clerk, sheriff, legislator, militia colonel, farmer, and minister.
    • William Blair — Status: verified. County-site commissioner, judge, state senator, and Mount Zion trustee.
    • Alexander Campbell — Status: supplemental. Indexed in Lowndes from 1830 through 1850.
    • Thomas Carlton — Status: supplemental. Household evidence supports regional presence, but his family and exact tract remain unresolved.
    • Henry Dean — Status: supplemental. Census transcriptions and District 12 deed locators place him in future-Brooks territory; no direct Barney influence is proved.
    • John Dean — Status: supplemental. Household and Tallokas office evidence support local residence; the Jane Allbritton marriage remains secondary-supported.
    • Edward Clinton Dukes — Status: supplemental. Original 1840 and 1850 schedules anchor his household; no descendant bridge to the later Barney Dukes families is proved.
    • Zachariah Fletcher — Status: supplemental. Verified in the future-Brooks 660th District by 1836; no descendant bridge to Barney is proved.
    • Israel Folsom — Status: supplemental. Pioneer household head kept separate from Israel Lee Folsom; their uncle-nephew link is secondary-supported.
    • John Folsom — Status: supplemental. Verified as an 1840 Lowndes household head; the Kelley John identity remains secondary-supported.
    • Pennywell Folsom — Status: supplemental. 1830 household head killed at Brushy Creek in 1836.
    • Thomas Folsom — Status: supplemental. 1830 household head and militiaman whose home served early Brooks public business.
    • Nathan Gornto — Status: verified. Tallokas settler, Nankin justice, and co-administrator of Joel Gornto’s estate.
    • John Hall — Status: supplemental. Verified in the 660th District in 1836; unresolved Hall identities prevent a family bridge.
    • William G. Hall — Status: verified. Landholder, Mount Zion trustee, and 660th District captain.
    • John Hendry — Status: verified. Liberty-to-Lowndes migrant, farmer, and early Mount Zion tenter.
    • William Hendry — Status: verified. Mount Zion trustee and grand juror with District 12 land near Mule Creek.
    • William Holloway Jr. — Status: supplemental. Bethel service and a District 12 deed locator support the profile; overlapping Sr./Jr. records remain qualified.
    • William G. Hunter — Status: supplemental. Tallokas captain, Bethlehem pastor, landholder, and courthouse committee member.
    • Jared Johnson — Status: supplemental. Documented by an 1840 household and District 12 land locators.
    • William Jones — Status: supplemental. Original records place him locally by 1828–1830, but the documented Barney line descends through Thomas Jones and no relationship to that family is proved.
    • Washington Joyce — Status: verified. Little River settler and ferryman associated with the later Miller Bridge crossing.
    • Ashley Lawson — Status: supplemental. Verified in the 660th District in 1836.
    • Daniel McCranie Sr. — Status: verified. Coffee Road settler and probable county-organizing commissioner.
    • James McLeod — Status: supplemental. Indexed as a Lowndes household head in 1830; militia and church roles remain secondary-supported.
    • James McMullen Jr. — Status: supplemental. An original 1830 household and District 12 land records support the profile, while a younger James McMullen remains separate.
    • William McMullen — Status: supplemental candidate. Census and militia evidence place McMullen men locally, but the original 1830 row and competing 1840 identities remain unresolved.
    • James Newton — Status: supplemental. Original 1850 household and secondary-supported Bethel clerkship.
    • John O’Steen — Status: supplemental. Published officer records place him as captain of the 659th, or Nankin, District in 1835; no direct Barney influence is proved.
    • John Bryan — Status: verified supplemental. Original records document him from 1830 through 1850; Jose or Joe Bryant remains a separate unresolved identity.
    • John Pike — Status: verified. Mount Zion neighbor, militia commander, and paymaster.
    • Rev. Joshua Platt — Status: verified. Early Mount Zion tenter and Methodist local preacher.
    • James T. Robeson — Status: supplemental. The original 1850 census and later court service establish continuity into Brooks County; no direct Barney influence is proved.
    • Thomas F. Rogers — Status: supplemental. Verified in the future-Brooks area by the 1844 tax digest.
    • Uriah Rogers — Status: supplemental. Verified in the future-Brooks area by the 1844 tax digest.
    • Francis Rountree — Status: verified. His home became the county’s statutory court and election place in 1826.
    • Henry Rowell — Status: supplemental. Verified in the 659th District militia return in 1836.
    • James Rowell — Status: supplemental. Verified as owner of District 12, Lot 393 in 1828.
    • Ezekiel Selph — Status: supplemental. The older Ezekiel was a Lowndes household head by 1830 and is strongly associated with District 12, Lot 466; no direct Barney influence is proved.
    • Hamilton W. Sharpe — Status: verified. Merchant, postmaster, justice, legislator, lay preacher, planter, and enslaver.
    • Ivy Simmons — Status: supplemental. Verified regionally by 1830 and associated with future-Brooks District 12 land by 1844; his exact migration date remains unresolved.
    • Archibald Strickland — Status: supplemental/secondary-supported. Secondary-supported at Bethel in 1826 and as father of Simpson and Simeon.
    • Simeon Strickland — Status: supplemental. Documented with Simpson in 1826; their brotherhood remains secondary-supported.
    • Simpson Strickland — Status: supplemental. Verified with Simeon in an 1826 deed.
    • Melus or Milus Thigpen — Status: supplemental. Verified by an 1826 District 12 deed.
    • John J. Underwood — Status: verified. County-site commissioner, Franklinville resident, militia officer, court official, and attorney.
    • Jeremiah Wilson Sr. — Status: supplemental. Documented on future-Brooks land and later as the surveyor who laid out Quitman.

    Traditional settlers and unresolved candidates¶

    These people remain part of the research record, but their exact identity, locality, continuity, or future-Brooks placement is not yet secure enough for the verified or supplemental groups.

    • Dennis Adams — Status: traditional/unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. An 1829 deed to his heirs conflicts with the traditional 1836 arrival and indicates a date error or generational conflation.
    • Cyprian Brinson — Status: supplemental Lowndes settler, geography unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. Census and District 15 evidence establish Lowndes activity, but no record places him in future-Brooks territory or connects him to Mills or Miles Brinson.
    • Mills or Miles M. Brinson — Status: supplemental Lowndes settler, geography unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. The 1850 household and an 1841 District 15 church deed are documented, but no record places him in future-Brooks territory.
    • Jacob Bryan — Status: early Lowndes officer, locality unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. His reported 1826 county office does not establish residence in the territory that became Brooks County.
    • Jose Bryant — Status: unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. Reported Okapilco and Mule Creek settler whose identity remains unresolved.
    • James Burnett — Status: traditional candidate · Barney relevance: none proved. James S. Burnett, the 1829 militia officer, cannot yet be identified as James H. Burnett, the later household candidate.
    • Amos Emanuel — Status: unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. Named in an unresolved 1827 Little River ferry proposal.
    • Elijah Folsom — Status: traditional candidate · Barney relevance: none proved. His local household, land, and family identity remain unresolved.
    • William Hancock — Status: verified regional settler; sheriff identity strong probable · Barney relevance: none proved. The original 1830 Lowndes census names him, but no record locates his home in future Brooks County or at Barney.
    • Abraham Hunter — Status: traditional candidate · Barney relevance: none proved. His reported 1823 Okapilco settlement conflicts with the surviving chronology.
    • Willis King — Status: unresolved regional settler · Barney relevance: none proved. Present in Irwin by 1820; the claimed 1823 date appears to describe a later relocation.
    • Joseph Kinsley and John Kinsey — Status: Joseph unresolved; John independently supported · Barney relevance: none proved. The traditional roster names Joseph, while the 1829 office and land records name John; no record equates them.
    • William Newton — Status: supplemental candidate · Barney relevance: none proved. Later Lowndes records cannot yet be tied safely to either 1830 Thomas County namesake.
    • David Platt — Status: verified regional settler · Barney relevance: none proved. Verified in Lowndes in 1830; a future-Brooks residence has not been established.
    • Elias Roberts — Status: traditional candidate · Barney relevance: none proved. Verified in Thomas County in 1830, but his reported Tallokas connection and continuity into future Brooks County remain unresolved.
    • William Smith — Status: verified regional official · Barney relevance: none proved. Franklinville clerk and postmaster who later kept Tranquil Hall at Troupville and Valdosta.
    • Canneth or Kenneth Swain — Status: verified Thomas County resident; future-Brooks locality unresolved · Barney relevance: none proved. Estate notices show that Canneth or Caneth died before November 1832; Kenneth and the family relationships remain unresolved.
    • Morgan G. Swain — Status: verified regional settler · Barney relevance: none proved. Documented in later Lowndes and Troupville records, with no proved future-Brooks parcel.
