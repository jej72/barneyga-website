    Henry Blair of Lowndes County, Georgia, and Hamilton County, Florida

    Back to the Early History Profiles index

    Research scope and conclusion

    This dossier concerns the Henry Blair who took part in the first decade of Lowndes County government, commanded county militia in the Creek conflict of 1836, and later lived in Hamilton County, Florida. The online evidence is strong enough to join those Georgia and Florida lives into one identity.

    The decisive link appeared while Henry was still alive. In September 1891 a Hamilton County item described him as an eighty-eight-year-old local Methodist preacher and said he had helped organize Lowndes County, served as its first court clerk and second sheriff, and taken the county's 1830 census.[1] Censuses then trace him from Lowndes in 1840 to Hamilton County in 1850, 1860, 1870, and 1880.[2-6]

    Henry was born in Georgia about 1803-1805. He was in Lowndes by 1830, served as sheriff and representative, and commanded the 81st Regiment of Georgia Militia in the field at Cow Creek in August 1836. He still resided in Lowndes in 1846, but had moved to Hamilton County by 1850. There he farmed, preached, and patented federal land.[4,5,18]

    Two important questions remain open. The records do not prove whether Henry was William Blair's son or brother, and they do not establish Henry's exact death date or burial place. Later sources disagree on the first question and offer only unsourced approximations for the second.[21-24]

    Identity and birth

    The September 1891 profile is a direct identity bridge, not a modern reconstruction. Reprinted from the Jasper News, it said:

    Henry Blair, a local Methodist preacher who lives in the western portion of this county, is 88 years old. He was in the organization of Lowndes county, Ga., was the first clerk of the court of that county, the second sheriff, and took the census there in 1830.

    It added that he was partially deaf but otherwise hale.[1] A second newspaper reprinted the item later that month.[25]

    The reported age implies birth about 1803. Census ages imply about 1805 in 1850, 1804 in 1860, 1807 in 1870, and 1804 in 1880. Every named census gives Georgia as his birthplace; the 1880 schedule says his father was born in South Carolina and his mother in Georgia.[3-6]

    The best statement is therefore born in Georgia about 1803-1805. The frequently repeated exact year 1803 is compatible with the evidence but not established by a birth, Bible, or baptismal record. A 1950 article's Screven County birthplace is a useful family-history lead, not original proof.[22]

    Relationship to William and Mary Blair

    Henry was closely tied to the older William Blair. When William died in 1840, Henry applied for administration. Henry and a younger William Blair later acted together as administrators, selling the elder man's personal property and land for creditors and heirs.[14]

    None of those legal notices calls Henry a son, brother, or heir. Later accounts conflict:

    • a handwritten addendum to a 1933 Georgia Archives family letter calls Henry the elder William's brother;[21]
    • a 1950 county-history article calls Henry William's son;[22]
    • user trees commonly identify William Blair and Mary Joyce as Henry's parents, but they cite no direct parental record.[23]

    Henry also served as executor of Mary Blair's estate in 1846. Her cattle, hogs, sheep, and household and kitchen property were sold at Henry's Lowndes residence. The notice does not identify Mary as his mother, sister, wife, or other relative.[17]

    The age pattern makes the son tradition plausible, and Henry's estate roles show trust and proximity. Neither is kinship proof. Henry's relationship to William and Mary Blair remains unresolved, with son-versus-brother explicitly disputed.

    First Lowndes offices

    Lowndes County was created in December 1825. A Georgia Historical Society marker says the first county officers commissioned May 29, 1826 included Henry Blair as Superior Court clerk.[8] Huxford's county-officer roster instead gives Henry's commission date as May 9, 1826 and his term as 1826-1830.[30] Hamilton W. Sharpe, an early resident writing in 1884, likewise remembered Henry as the clerk selected at the county's first election and said Henry was still living.[9]

    The exact 1826 commission image was not found online, and the two later sources disagree by twenty days. The precise commission date therefore remains unresolved. Henry's status as first clerk is nevertheless strong and was also repeated in the 1891 profile published during his lifetime.[1]

    The claim that Henry took the 1830 Lowndes census also comes directly from that 1891 profile.[1] The full 1830 Georgia population roll was inspected online, but a signed appointment or return identifying him as assistant marshal was not located. The statement is reported in a lifetime profile but officially unproved; it should not be presented as a verified federal appointment.

    Sheriff and legislator

    Contemporary statewide sheriff lists published in 1832 name "Lowndes, Henry Blair". Henry's own sale notices then identify him as sheriff, including the levy of John J. Underwood's Franklinville property and a countywide sale of academy land.[10,11]

    These records verify Henry's service in 1832-1833. A later office chronology extends his sheriff term through 1834, but the endpoints have not been independently proved.[22]

    Henry also represented Lowndes County in the Georgia House in 1833. Contemporary election returns name Sharpe and Blair for Lowndes, while legislative reports identify "Mr. Blair, of Lowndes" as reporting a bill to extend Georgia jurisdiction over territory disputed with Florida.[30] A December report also names him among House members who signed a protest after the majority ended debate.[12] These records verify his membership and legislative activity, but the search did not establish that his jurisdiction bill passed.

    Militia command and Cow Creek

    The original 1836 Lowndes general-militia return names Henry as colonel commanding the 81st Regiment. Enoch Hall served as lieutenant colonel; D. H. Howell as quartermaster; John Pike as paymaster; and I. S. Burnett as adjutant.[13]

    That return describes the standing county militia. It is not an active-service roster. Henry's own report to Governor William Schley supplies the field evidence.[15]

    Dated August 30, 1836, Henry's report says a Native party was sighted in upper Lowndes on August 24. He joined an initial pursuit, sent for Captain Levi J. Knight, and commanded about sixty or sixty-five men in an August 27 fight at Cow Creek swamp near the Ware-Lowndes line. He reported one wounded man and one killed horse among his force. He claimed two Native people killed in the field, two possibly carried into the swamp, and six prisoners, one of whom died the next day. The five survivors were sent to the Thomasville jail. He signed as "Colonel Commanding 81st Regiment, G. M."[15]

    The document verifies Henry's command and what he reported. Its estimates of the Native party's size and losses are his wartime account, not independent casualty findings. A short contemporary newspaper summary confirms the basic engagement and command structure.[16]

    Household, enslavement, and Lowndes property

    The 1840 census places Henry in Lowndes as a male aged thirty through thirty-nine. His household contained six free white people and twelve enslaved people; six people were recorded in agriculture.[2] The enslaved residents were counted by age and sex but not named: six males and six females across the schedule's age categories.

    Georgia tax indexes place Henry in Lowndes in 1830, 1834, 1835, 1838, 1840, and 1844. The district labels move from Captain Pike's to Captain Godwin's, Captain Grantham's, and finally Captain Blair's.[7] Those entries establish continuity but do not, without full image transcription, prove every acreage or tax column.

    Newspaper levies identify Henry's land interests:

    • In 1844, Land Lot 360 in the 12th District, 490 acres of well-improved pine land, was levied under a Lowndes Inferior Court execution. Henry pointed out the property.[20]
    • In 1848, a mortgage execution covered Lot 332, 222 acres of Lot 307, and 75 acres of Lot 333, all in the 12th District, totaling about 787 acres.[20]

    The notices verify ownership or a mortgaged interest. They do not establish Henry's exact house site. The 1846 Mary Blair sale is the clearest residence statement because it places the sale at Henry's Lowndes home.[17]

    Indexed searches of the Hamilton County 1850 and 1860 slave schedules returned no qualifying Henry Blair entry.[26] That is a documented negative search, not proof that he held no enslaved people after moving; schedules and indexes can omit or misread names.

    Move to Hamilton County

    The migration can be narrowed to the mid-1840s. Henry appeared on the 1844 Lowndes tax digest and lived in Lowndes during the August 1846 Mary Blair sale.[7,17] In the 1850 Hamilton County census, his older children were Georgia-born while four-year-old Henry was born in Florida.[3]

    The safest window is after August 1846 and by the 1850 census. The 1848 Lowndes mortgage sale proves continuing Georgia land interests, not continuing residence.[20]

    Wife and children

    Henry's wife appears on original census images under several spellings:

    • "Luranah", age 38, in 1850;
    • "Laurina", age 48, in 1860;
    • "Lurany", age 55, in 1870;
    • "Lurany", age 68 and explicitly Henry's wife, in 1880.[3-6]

    The name, age progression, and household continuity strongly support one woman despite normal spelling variation. A compiled genealogy calls her Lorena Beasley, and a user-created memorial uses the same name and places her at Hickory Grove Methodist Church Cemetery in Madison County, Florida.[23,24] No original marriage record or maiden-name record was found. The defensible wording is Luranah/Laurina/Lurany, traditionally Lorena Beasley.

    The 1850 and 1860 schedules place James, William, Franklin, Vianah/Vienna, and younger Henry in the household with broadly compatible names and approximate ages despite normal census variation. Because those censuses did not state relationships, they are strong probable children, not directly proved from the schedules alone.[3,4]

    The 1880 census provides direct relationship evidence. It identifies Robert S. Blair, age twenty-eight, as Henry's son and William H. Blair, age sixteen, as his grandson.[6] The seven-year-old William in the 1870 household is therefore strong probable to be that grandson rather than a late-born son.[5]

    Florida work, land, and post office

    The 1850 enumerator wrote Henry's occupation as "M.P. Minister", and the 1891 profile called him a local Methodist preacher.[1,3] Together they verify a long-lived preaching role.

    A 1926 Mount Zion centennial article went further, saying Henry and Hamilton W. Sharpe were local preachers who established the church at the old camp ground.[27] That claim conflicts with Sharpe's 1884 recollection and the 1831 Mount Zion deed evidence, both of which name William Blair, not Henry, on the site committee, first-tenter group, or trustee list.[9,28] Henry may have preached there, but his status as a Mount Zion co-founder is unresolved and vulnerable to William/Henry confusion.

    In Florida, a federal patent issued to Henry on October 1, 1860 for land in Township 2 North, Range 12 East, Section 30, Hamilton County.[18] A Henry Blair was appointed postmaster at Bellville on July 7, 1869.[19] The appointment record does not distinguish the elder Henry from his adult son of the same name, so assigning the office to the elder is possible but unproved. The 1860 census valued the elder Henry's real estate at $600 and personal estate at $1,120; the 1870 census recorded $300 real and $150 personal estate.[4,5]

    Death and burial

    Henry was alive in September 1891, when the Jasper item described him as partially deaf but otherwise healthy.[1] A later family-history book says he died "circa 1891"; a 1950 article instead gives 1893.[22,23] Neither displayed source cites a death record, obituary, probate file, or grave marker.

    Targeted searches of Georgia Historic Newspapers, authenticated Newspapers.com, Ancestry death and probate collections, Find a Grave, Hamilton County variants, and cemetery leads did not locate a qualifying obituary, death record, will, probate proceeding, burial register, photographed marker, or Henry Blair memorial.[29] Apparent Valdosta hits in 1893 were search false positives.

    Lorena Blair's user-created memorial cannot supply Henry's burial place, and the presence of later family members at Blair Cemetery near Jennings does not place him there.[24] Henry's exact death date and burial place remain unresolved.

    Chronology

    • About 1803-1805: born in Georgia.[1,3-6]
    • May 1826: later sources agree Henry became first clerk but conflict between May 9 and May 29.[8,30]
    • 1830: Lowndes tax entry; later lifetime profile says he took the county census.[1,7]
    • 1832-1833: verified Lowndes sheriff.[10,11]
    • 1833: verified Lowndes representative in the Georgia House.[12]
    • 1836: colonel of the 81st Regiment; commanded the Cow Creek operation on August 27 and reported it on August 30.[13,15]
    • 1840: heads Lowndes household of eighteen, including twelve enslaved people; begins administration of William Blair estate.[2,14]
    • 1844: Lowndes tax entry and levy on improved Land Lot 360.[7,20]
    • July-August 1846: executor of Mary Blair estate; sale at Henry's Lowndes residence.[17]
    • 1848: mortgage levy on Lowndes Lots 332, 307, and 333.[20]
    • 1850: Hamilton County household; occupation "M.P. Minister".[3]
    • October 1, 1860: federal Hamilton County land patent.[18]
    • July 7, 1869: a Henry Blair was appointed Bellville postmaster; elder-versus-son identity unresolved.[19]
    • 1880: Hamilton census explicitly identifies Lurany as wife, Robert S. as son, and William H. as grandson.[6]
    • September 1891: alive in western Hamilton County, age reported as eighty-eight.[1,25]
    • After September 1891: death date and burial unresolved.[22-24,29]

    Search coverage and remaining record targets

    The best unresolved targets are:

    1. Henry's 1826 clerk commission and federal 1830 assistant-marshal appointment or signed census return;
    2. William and Mary Blair probate packets, wills, bonds, distributions, or family Bible evidence;
    3. the mortgage/deed chain for Lowndes Lots 360, 332, 307, and 333;
    4. a marriage record or direct record establishing Luranah's maiden name;
    5. Hamilton County probate, deed, tax, Methodist, and cemetery material after September 1891;
    6. manually browsed Hamilton/Jasper/Jennings newspapers for late 1891 through 1894.
