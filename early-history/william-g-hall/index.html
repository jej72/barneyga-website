    William G. Hall¶

    William G. Hall can be followed in Lowndes County records from 1830 through 1844. Four original tax images, an original-based transcription of the 1831 Mount Zion deed, and the preserved 1836 militia return identify the same full-name local figure. These records do not verify the traditional 1824–1825 arrival date or place his home in the later town of Barney.

    Tax and land record¶

    Original Lowndes County tax images place William G. Hall in Captain Pike's District in 1830 and Captain Godwin's District in 1835. The 1840 return places him in Captain Vann's District with District 12, Land Lot 132. In 1844 he appears in Captain Burnett's District with Lots 132 and 133.

    The repeated full name and Lot 132 provide a coherent record chain. The changing district labels do not prove that Hall moved, and the tax pages do not identify the location of his house. Lots 132 and 133 have not been tied to the future Barney townsite.

    No William G. Hall transaction appears in the reviewed abstraction of reconstructed Lowndes Deed Book A. That is a source gap, not proof that no deed existed. The 1858 courthouse fire destroyed most early deed books, and many instruments were never returned for re-recording.

    Mount Zion trustee¶

    Folks Huxford printed a transcription of the November 8, 1831 Mount Zion deed and said he copied it from the original. Sion and Enoch Hall conveyed about six acres for Methodist worship to trustees William Blair, William Hendry, Lawrence Folsom, James J. Joyce, and William G. Hall. The deed was proved October 3, 1832 and recorded in Lowndes Deed Book A, pages 654–655, on February 26, 1833.[5]

    The courthouse copy was later lost and was not re-recorded. Huxford's transcription is strong original-based evidence, but no manuscript image has been located. The deed proves William's trusteeship. It does not state that he was related to Sion or Enoch Hall or that he lived on the church tract.

    Militia office¶

    The 1836 Lowndes general-militia return names William G. Hall, Captain, at the head of the 660th District list.[6] The return enumerates men subject to militia duty. It is not a roster of soldiers who entered active Creek War service, so the document verifies Hall's office and district association rather than combat service.

    Identity limits¶

    A possible 1850 Lowndes census candidate appears only as William Hall, age 37, with no real-estate value reported. That entry does not fit comfortably with the established landholder who held Lots 132 and 133 through 1844. Without a deed, tax, probate, or family bridge, the 1850 candidate remains unassigned.

    No reviewed record establishes William's spouse, children, parents, death, burial, or close relationship to other Hall families in the area. Shared surname and proximity are not enough.

    A later Lowndes deed transcription reports that “heirs of John Hall” conveyed 265 acres in District 12, Lot 132 to Rebecca Hall in 1877. That instrument cannot be assigned safely to either John Hall household. William G. Hall had held Lot 132 in 1840 and Lots 132–133 in 1844, and the accessible online tax and title records do not show when the property left his ownership or first became associated with a John Hall. The original Book L, page 37 deed and a complete title chain are still needed.

    Connection to Barney¶

    William G. Hall belongs to the wider Mount Zion and future-Brooks County story. His trusteeship, militia office, and District 12 land show a substantial regional presence, but no reviewed record places his home in Barney or proves a direct role in the later town.

    Evidence status¶

    • Verified: Lowndes County presence from 1830 through 1844; District 12 land in Lots 132 and 133; captain of the 660th District in 1836.
    • Strong original-based evidence: Mount Zion trustee in Huxford's transcription of the 1831 deed.
    • Unverified tradition: arrival in 1824–1825.
    • Unresolved: the 1850 census candidate, family relationships, death, burial, exact home site, and any direct Barney connection.
    • Title-chain gap: the later Lot 132 heirs deed cannot be interpreted until William G. Hall's ownership and the named heirs are traced in the original records.
