    Ivy Simmons

    Back to the Early History Profiles index

    Evidence assessment

    Supplemental early settler; locator-anchored in future-Brooks territory. The original 1830 census schedule names Ivy Simmons in Lowndes County. Reconstructed Lowndes deed entries associate Ivey/Ivy Simmons with District 12, Lot 173 in 1844 and Lots 523 and 307 in 1847–1854; those parcels later lay in Brooks County. His reported 1826 arrival should not be repeated as fact.

    Identity and chronology

    The original 1830 Lowndes schedule, stamped page 88, contains a legible Ivy Simmons household. The original 1840 schedule is preserved and indexed to Ivy Simmons on manuscript page 237, though a complete header-aligned household transcription has not been accepted. FamilySearch also indexes an Ivey Simmons, born about 1798 in North Carolina, in Lowndes County in 1850. These records support one adult Lowndes identity across 1830–1850, but the full household continuity still needs image-level comparison.

    Huxford gives two incompatible migration statements. His dated roster says Simmons came to future Brooks territory in 1826, while a later family sketch says he lived in Montgomery County and moved to the Lowndes area that became Brooks County about 1840. Neither date is independently proved. The narrow public conclusion is that Simmons was in Lowndes County by 1830 and held future-Brooks land by 1844.

    Public and military role

    Huxford's militia-officer compilation identifies Ivy Simmons as the first major of the 158th Battalion, commissioned January 29, 1830. This is strong secondary evidence, not an inspected commission. The 1830 household independently confirms an adult Ivy Simmons in the county that year.

    Land and future-Brooks geography

    The preserved Lowndes deed reconstruction supplies three reproducible land anchors:

    • James McMullen to Ivey Simmons, February 5, 1844, District 12, Lot 173, 3,500 dollars, Deed Book C, p. 408.
    • James M. Wilkinson, sheriff, to Ivey Simmons, January 6, 1847, District 12, Lot 523, Deed Book D, pp. 92–93.
    • Ivy Simmons to Leary Stanley, May 17, 1854, 220 acres in the southeast section of District 12, Lot 307, Deed Book E, pp. 479–480.

    An 1848 sheriff's-sale notice also advertised interests in District 12, Lots 332, 307, and 333 under a mortgage execution in favor of Ivy Simmons against Henry Blair. This verifies a substantial legal and land association in the future-Brooks district, but not residence on each parcel.

    Family claims

    Huxford names Piety Joyce, daughter of Henry Joyce of Montgomery County, as Ivy's wife. Compiled Joyce genealogies place Piety in the same sibling group as Mary Joyce Blair, Washington Joyce, and James Jackson or James J. Joyce. That would make Ivy Simmons a brother-in-law of William Blair and connect the Simmons family collaterally to the Blair–Gornto line that later reached Barney. The sibling group remains secondary-supported; no inspected probate, Bible, deed, or church record names all of Henry Joyce's children.

    The same compilation identifies Mary Simmons, wife of Benjamin Johnson Jr., and Rachel Simmons, later wife of Dr. Henry Briggs, as Ivy's daughters. These links are also secondary-supported because the located census, deed, and newspaper records do not state the relationships. A later descendant path through Mary and granddaughter Ivy Johnson is likewise derivative until each generational link is independently proved.

    Death and burial

    FamilySearch's Find a Grave index gives 1798–1855 and Providence Community Church Cemetery near Morven. This is a cemetery-index lead, not inspection of a marker image or death record. No probate file was located in the preserved local evidence.

    Conflicts and limits

    • Arrival: 1826 and about 1840 conflict; neither is verified.
    • Major's commission: strong secondary evidence only; original commission uninspected.
    • Piety Joyce and daughters Mary/Rachel: explicit compiled relationships, coherent but not parent- or marriage-record verified.
    • Barney connection: future-Brooks land association is strongly supported by reproducible deed locators, but the underlying instruments remain uninspected and no direct residence at the later Barney townsite or direct institutional effect on Barney is proved.
    • Census detail: the names and county are secure for 1830; complete demographic transcription for 1830 and 1840 remains unfinished.

    Public-ready summary

    Ivy Simmons was a Lowndes County household head by 1830. A reconstructed deed entry associates him with District 12 land in territory later included in Brooks County by 1844. A county-history compilation identifies him as the first major of the 158th Battalion and names Piety Joyce as his wife, but the underlying commission, deed, and a relationship-naming marriage or probate record have not been inspected. Conflicting county-history statements place his arrival in either 1826 or about 1840, so no exact migration date should be assigned.
