    Site Updates

    This page records substantial additions and corrections to the website. Minor wording, formatting, and maintenance changes are not listed.

    October 2, 2026

    William and Elizabeth Folsom original deeds

    Expanded original-record profile

    The William Folsom dossier now incorporates four administrator's deeds proving at least 2,080 acres in his estate. The deeds identify District 9 Land Lots 492, 519, and 521, 120 acres of Lot 491, and District 12 Lot 323, together with the purchasers and auction prices. A separate executor's deed records Elizabeth Folsom's conveyance of District 9 Lots 493 and 520 to her daughter Edith Rountree. The Early Settlers and Folsom family pages now explain how these land transfers and Elizabeth's will connect the Folsom, Ryall, Rountree, and Perry families north of Barney. William's acquisition deeds, complete acreage, and the original 1867 road record remain unresolved.

    October 1, 2026

    Wider early-settler verification

    Research update

    The Early Settlers section now reports the results of an independent review of eighty additional roster names. A digitized 1836 Lowndes County general-militia return supplies narrow district and rank evidence for fourteen named men, while federal official registers place Hamilton W. Sharpe at Sharpe's Store by 1829. The update also states what the review did not prove: no newly inspected census image verified a named household from the wider roster, and traditional arrival dates remain attributed to their retrospective sources.

    The review preserved identity safeguards for same-name people and separate generations. It did not promote possible matches, surname associations, or reel-level census references into verified biographies.

    Expanded early-settler descendant crosswalk

    New family connections

    The Families & Connections section now documents 22 residents of the 1900 Barney census through 29 graded paths to eight early settlers. New pages establish three qualified George Alderman lines and two Joel Gornto lines. The Swain and Strickland proposals remain under research: an 1858 guardianship record supports Thomas E. B. Swain as Stephen Swain's father, but the Morgan G. Swain generation remains unproved, and no reviewed record connects Stephen Smith Strickland to the earlier regional Strickland families.

    September 30, 2026

    Folsom settler-descendant link

    New family connection

    Jeremiah Walton Folsom's dossier now traces his reported line to early settler Lawrence Folsom and links to the Lawrence and William Folsom dossiers. The page separates the census-supported father-son relationship from the earlier generations supplied by compiled genealogy, for which no parent-naming original has been reviewed at every step.

    FamilySearch review of eleven contemporary-report identities

    Research correction

    An authenticated FamilySearch review verified Rev. J. W. Folsom as Jeremiah Walton Folsom through Brooks County census and voter records. It also established the broader civil record of county coroner E. D. Ponder and found his exact-name Confederate pension index. Jesse Chambers received an independent Brooks County voter-registration bridge. Eight other identities remain possible because no record securely joined them to the Barney reports.

    Contemporary-report dossiers and military review

    New research

    The 1900 census page now links individual dossiers for all 15 people whose identities are verified or strong probable. Possible and unresolved identities remain unlinked. A military-record audit also corrected Joseph J. Oglesby's status: near-contemporary and county-history accounts establish his Confederate service, although his detailed company, rank, and Macon shop assignment still await original military records.

    Original-record research on unresolved contemporary-report names

    Research correction

    Original-record searches strengthen two identities. The 1901 inquest juror J. W. Kendrick is probably Jason W. Kendrick of Brooks County, and the 1899 teacher Minnie McKinnon is probably Minnie A. McKinnon of Hahira. Eighteen names remain unresolved after census, marriage, tax, court, school, business, newspaper, and military checks. The revised table records the strongest available finding without assigning same-name records that lack a Barney connection.

    Joyce and Culbreth second-round research

    Research correction

    New census, cemetery, and military records strengthen Janie Joyce as a daughter of John Wesley P. Joyce and verify Minnie Adelia Culbreth as Blackman Hawley Culbreth's daughter. They also correct Blackman's birth year to 1847 and establish his Confederate service in North Carolina units.

    Hall cluster second-round research

    Research correction

    A second review separated the Hall references into at least two families. Mrs. J. B. Hall is now verified as Lula S. Collingsworth Hall. The 1899 merchant belongs to the older household-1 J. F. Hall family, while the railroad conductor remains unresolved. Frank Henry Hall is the leading candidate for the early merchant Frank Hall, but direct Barney business evidence has not been found. Mrs. Frank Hall and the 1901 juror George P. Hall remain unresolved. No Hall military service was proved.

    Contemporary reports reconciliation

    Research correction

    The 44 people named in contemporary reports but previously left unmatched were reviewed individually. The revised census page now distinguishes verified, strong probable, possible, and unresolved identities and explains which people lived outside the Barney district or died before the census. Later military follow-up established Confederate service for Blackman Hawley Culbreth and Joseph J. Oglesby.

    September 27, 2026

    Hope and Jack Croft dossiers

    New research

    The Hope Croft dossier brings together the 1887 Stonewall Academy reports, the community resolution requiring the Croft family to leave, census evidence for Hope's likely identity as Abba or Abner Croft, his probable adult record as George A. Croft, and the younger namesake killed near Barney in 1922. The proposed connection to the 1885–1886 Morven-area fires remains unproved.

    The revised Jacob Henry “Jack” Croft dossier covers his family, Confederate service and amputation, Brooks County farm, Land Lot 227 conveyance, removal from the community, second marriage, later life, and estate. It also corrects the geography: Croft's tract belonged to the Morven district during his residence, before the McNeil & Rozier company village developed there.

    Initial website release

    Site update

    The first public edition was published at barneyga.com. Research currently extends through 1910, with later material included where it helps explain Barney's roads, bridges, railroads, and community sites.

    The launch edition included:

    • New research: expanded accounts of early settlement, Mount Zion, county formation, early roads, Folsom Bridge, and the communities along Little River.
    • New research: separate histories of the South Georgia Railway, the Tallokas-to-Barney tram, the Valdosta, Moultrie and Western, and the proposed Georgia, Alabama and Western Railroad.
    • New research: reorganized coverage of Barney before incorporation, the 1903 charter, early municipal government, commerce, postal service, community life, and public order.
    • Correction: the earlier 1910 census totals were withdrawn after a systematic extraction error was identified. Household information is used only where it has been checked, and a page-by-page reconstruction is underway.
    • New images: historical maps, photographs, restored images, and clearly labeled AI-generated reconstructions were added throughout the site.
