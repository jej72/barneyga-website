    Site Updates

    This page records substantial additions and corrections to the website. Minor wording, formatting, and maintenance changes are not listed.

    October 12, 2026

    Charles Dorothus Paxson dossier

    Expanded the Charles Dorothus Paxson dossier with his family background, marriage, sawmill work, Barney land transactions, 1900 household, later move to Florida, and death. The page also explains that the Valdosta Times printed “C. D. Parson” in Barney's 1903 nominated council slate. Paxson is the strong-probable intended man, but the printed wording and the absence of a located election return are preserved.

    John Berryhill and Dr. A. S. Humphreys dossiers

    Expanded the John J. Berryhill Sr. dossier from a military summary into a full account of his family, Confederate service, farm, 1903 accident, church role, municipal nomination, death, and probate. A new Dr. Alexander S. Humphreys Jr. dossier follows his family, marriage, medical practice in Florida and Barney, work during the 1899 smallpox outbreak, municipal nomination, and later residence in Morven. The pages preserve the unresolved expansion of Humphreys's middle initial and the conflicting death dates found for both men.

    Barney's first municipal slate

    The Government and Early Officers page presents Barney's December 1903 nominated slate: S. L. Hall for mayor and John Berryhill, T. A. Rozier, F. B. Walker, “C. D. Parson,” and A. S. Humphreys for council. The printed Parson is now strongly identified as C. D. Paxson, a documented Barney sawmill operator and landholder. The newspaper proves the nominations, not the council election result or completed terms.

    October 11, 2026

    South Georgia Railway poor-farm and damage claims

    The South Georgia Railway page now documents the 1900 road-drainage complaint, the county poor farm's reported $500 loss, the strong-probable identification of keeper Sidney T. Belcher, and W. A. Edmondson's separate 1901 damages case. The page keeps the three matters separate because the poor-farm location and any county settlement remain unknown.

    Priscilla and Thomas Rogers: possible sibling relationship

    The Rogers Family page now states the Ancestry-tree hypothesis explicitly: if James Rogers fathered both Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom and Thomas F. Rogers, they were siblings or half-siblings. The page names the competing maternal assignments—Nancy Ann Giddens and Nancy Wallace—and keeps the resulting relationships to Aaron J. Rogers, Arthur Richmond Rogers, and I. L. Folsom conditional because no inspected original record names Priscilla and Thomas together as James's children.

    Rogers collateral family branch

    The Rogers Family page now examines the secondary-supported James Rogers–Thomas F. Rogers line and the stronger Aaron J. Rogers–Arthur Richmond Rogers chain. Arthur's LL321 deeds connect the branch to Barney at strong-probable confidence. The proposed kinship to Uriah and Joseph Rogers remains conditional because the older parent-child links have not been established by an original record.

    Rogers family connections

    Added an evidence-graded Rogers Family page connecting Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks to Joseph J. Rogers's Foster and Barney household. The page presents Mary Ann Rogers Folsom as Joseph's strong-probable older sister, traces the Hires, Moody, Westberry, and Folsom connections, and keeps Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom's parentage and relationship to Uriah's branch unresolved.

    Marietta Folsom Westberry family connections

    The Folsom Family page now traces Marietta Elizabeth Folsom Westberry's relationship to pre-Barney landowner William Folsom and Barney merchant Israel Lee “I. L.” Folsom. Census records establish the later generations, while the older sibling sets remain identified as secondary-supported from the 1938 Genealogy of the Folsom Family.

    Thomas Alligood Rozier burial

    The Thomas Alligood Rozier dossier now identifies his burial place as Camp Ground Cemetery at Mount Zion Church near Morven. The Georgia death certificate supplies Morven as the intended burial place, and Find a Grave memorial 111534073 supplies the full cemetery name.

    Rozier and Savannah Naval Stores dossiers

    Expanded the Thomas Alligood Rozier dossier and added a source-graded Savannah Naval Stores Company dossier. The pages document Rozier's separate roles as local operator, factoring-network customer or borrower, and strong-probable company stockholder; they also trace the company's verified ownership of LL320 and LL325 while keeping the debt-settlement explanation explicitly unproved.

    E. F. Niblack bankruptcy records

    The Stores, trades, and the developing town page now gives the complete located chronology of E. F. Niblack's 1901 bankruptcy: the petition, $1,615.96 in liabilities, $755 in assets, November 16 adjudication, and December 2 creditors' meeting. It also distinguishes those proved proceedings from the still-unlocated final disposition and records that Niblack's full name, merchandise line, creditors, and storefront remain unknown.

    Edenfield & McMillan store location

    The Stores, trades, and the developing town page now distinguishes E. F. Niblack's unrelated 1901 bankruptcy from the 1903 Moran and Edenfield & McMillan fire. The page also identifies W. C. Edenfield's adjoining LL278 town lots 10 and 11 in the depot-side commercial strip as the strong-probable location of Edenfield & McMillan's two buildings.

    John G. Scruggs store location

    The Stores, trades, and the developing town page now places John G. Scruggs's 1903 general store, at strong-probable confidence, on the approximately one-acre tract at the northeast corner of Ninth Street and the South Georgia Railway right-of-way. The deed description closely matches B. H. Legg's 1898 tract. Scruggs's Barney store is distinguished from his sawmill, gin, and other milling operations near Scruggs Pond.

    Spivey & Nesmith turpentine still

    The Commerce and Industry page now identifies the still marked at the south end of First Avenue on the 1908 Brooks County map as strong probable to be the Spivey & Nesmith turpentine still. Deeds locate their still tract in LL320 in 1908, and a 1910 security deed describes a 25-barrel still and its associated buildings and equipment. The map does not name the operator, so the identification remains below verified.

    Barney business directory, 1903

    The Stores, trades, and the developing town page reproduces Barney's six-entry roster from the 1903 Georgia, Alabama, Florida Business and Professional Directory. The image and searchable transcription identify W. C. Edenfield, Edenfield & McMullen, M. A. Hall, T. A. Rozier, John G. Scruggs, and F. B. Walker & Company.

    October 10, 2026

    Barney business directory, 1909–1910

    The Commerce and Industry page now reproduces the complete twenty-entry Barney roster from Young & Co.'s Business and Professional Directory of the Cities and Towns of Georgia, 1909–1910. The directory lists the bank, merchants, physicians, dentist, druggist, barber, blacksmiths, sawmill, market, and turpentine firms, while the accompanying note distinguishes a Barney business address from proved property ownership or the physical location of an outlying operation.

    Bank of Barney property and early operations

    The Commerce and Industry page now traces the bank parcel carved from F. B. Walker's storehouse lot in 1905 through its 1908 transfer to Barney Banking Company. The expanded account gives the lot dimensions and boundary calls, identifies W. D. Manley as proprietor, adds the directory's $28,090 deposit figure, and uses the 1910 census to document Benjamin F. Long as the bank's cashier. It also explains the period meaning of that office and documents Clyde Newman's 1911 resignation while preserving the unresolved institutional relationship among Citizens Bank, Bank of Adel, Bank of Barney, and Barney Banking Company.

    James I. Moody's store and Walker family history

    The Commerce and Industry section now documents James I. Moody's 1907 purchase of a storehouse at Main Street and First Avenue and his later purchase of the adjoining residential lot to the west. It compares the deeds and the later cross-street location of F. B. Walker's brick stores with family history that Moody first worked for his uncle Walker, while noting that the 1900 census still placed nineteen-year-old Moody in his family's Fodie household and does not establish when he began working or living in Barney.

    Barney Hotel photograph

    The Barney Hotel section now includes the historical photograph preserved in the Brooks County Museum and Cultural Center history binder. The outdated general 1910 census subsection was removed because the site now has a dedicated 1910 Census and Household Index; the hotel-specific evidence from Mary Walker's household remains in the hotel section.

    The Barney Hotel through 1910

    The First Municipal Years section now connects D. H. Purvis's documented January 1906 purchase of a half-acre at Eighth Street and First Avenue with the Brooks County Museum binder's account that he built the Barney Hotel that year. The expanded section also brings together the 1908 map and newspaper evidence and Mary Walker's 1910 First Avenue boarding household while keeping the deed, later local history, and census evidence distinct.

    F. B. Walker storehouses and bank block

    The Stores, trades, and the developing town page follows F. B. Walker's early LL321 commercial property from his 1898 acquisitions to an 1899 storehouse at old Fifth Avenue and Ninth Street—later First Avenue and Main Street. Later deeds place the Bank of Barney within Walker's storehouse lot and prove that two Walker brick stores stood immediately south of the bank by 1915, sharing its south wall.

    October 9, 2026

    Railway Age construction and engineering record

    The South Georgia Railroad page now includes a contemporary construction chronology from The Railway Age: six operating miles in July 1896, grading nearly complete by October, track at Morven in early November, and twenty-three miles added during 1896. The same journal identifies Joel Mable as chief engineer, records the 1897 Quitman traffic-office change, and documents proposed Nashville and Deadman's Bay extensions. The extension notices are presented as plans, not completed routes; the July 1896 article's printed “Whitman” error is also preserved and explained.

    Ancel Lawrence land and family dossier

    Added an evidence-graded profile of Ancel Lawrence, an important early Black landowner and parcel seller. The dossier brings together his 1900 and 1910 households, verified interests in both halves of LL321, early Barney-area conveyances, and the unresolved station-title and Walker-to-Lawrence identity questions.

    Foster and LL320 purchase chronology

    The Origins page now distinguishes the two October 1890 acquisitions. J. H. Croft conveyed McNiel & Rosier the Foster tract in LL227 on October 9. Henry G. Turner conveyed James T. McNiel and Thomas A. Rozier all of LL320 and LL325 on October 18. Rozier, Conoly & Company did not make either original purchase; the Conoly brothers entered the Foster business in November 1891.

    LL321 parent-tract clarification

    The Origins page no longer says that Ancel Lawrence subdivided the west half of LL321 into town lots. Deed Book K, page 721, proves his 1893 purchase of a 245-acre tract described as the west half, but it does not connect the later platted parcels to that tract. A 1914 deed also places 200 acres of Lawrence's land in the east half. The title by which he acquired that additional interest remains unresolved.

    The four land lots that became Barney

    Research update

    The Origins: Foster to Barney page now follows the separate title histories of Land Lots 278, 279, 320, and 321. New deed evidence places the name “Barney” in local legal use by November 20, 1896, proves that Judge Hansell authorized Joel Mable's numbered town plan on December 19, and identifies the railroad right-of-way, station parcel, and whole-lot purchase that joined four ownership systems around the new line.

    The revision also closes the former LL320 title gap. Rozier, Conoly & Company conveyed all of LL320 and LL325 to Savannah Naval Stores Company in 1894, and the company sold LL320 to Sallie A. Rozier in December 1896.

    Station parcel land-lot correction

    Correction

    The Origins page no longer assigns Ancel Lawrence's December 26, 1896, station parcel to Land Lot 321. Deed Book N, page 534, leaves the land-lot number blank in its operative description; “LL321” appears only as a later marginal or index notation. The deed bounds the parcel north by Hodges land, east by Berryhill land, and south by church property, but those calls do not yet establish its land lot. The deed conveys only an undivided half interest, and the other half remains unresolved.
