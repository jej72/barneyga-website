    Site Updates

    This page records substantial additions and corrections to the website. Minor wording, formatting, and maintenance changes are not listed.

    October 4, 2026

    Eighth early-settler cohort

    Five new evidence-graded profiles

    New profiles cover John O’Steen, James T. Robeson, Ivy Simmons, Ezekiel Selph, and Elias Roberts. O’Steen, Robeson, Simmons, and Selph are classified as broader future-Brooks settlers. Roberts remains a traditional candidate because no contemporary record yet places him securely in the future county.

    The profiles preserve the unresolved points, including the separate John H. O’Steen identity, conflicting Simmons migration dates, two generations of Ezekiel Selph, and the unproved Tallokas connection for Elias Roberts.

    Early History profiles reorganized by Barney relevance

    The 67 Early History profiles are now divided into three evidence-based groups: 20 people with direct Barney-area influence or a documented family or settlement bridge, 36 broader future-Brooks settlers, and 11 traditional or unresolved candidates.

    Every entry now separates roster status from Barney relevance. This preserves the wider county history without implying that every regional settler lived at Barney or influenced the town directly.

    Seventh early-settler cohort

    Five new evidence-graded profiles

    New profiles cover James Newton, James Rowell, Jeremiah Wilson Sr., Henry Rowell, and William Newton. An original 1828 deed verifies James Rowell's ownership of District 12, Lot 393. The original 1836 militia return places Henry Rowell in the 659th District, while the 1850 census anchors the later James and William Newton households. Jeremiah Wilson's District 12 deeds and later survey work establish his future-Brooks record.

    The profiles preserve the unresolved points. Neither of the two 1830 Thomas County William Newton households can yet be assigned safely to the later Lowndes man. James and Henry Rowell are not treated as relatives, Cyntha Rowell's relationship to Henry remains unknown, and the traditional Newton and Wilson arrival dates remain secondary rather than verified.

    Sixth early-settler cohort

    Five new evidence-graded profiles

    New profiles cover Simpson Strickland, Melus or Milus Thigpen, James McLeod, Alexander Campbell, and John Lawson. An original 1826 deed verifies Simpson and Thigpen in Lowndes County and places Simpson with Simeon Strickland. A fresh census search supplies a 1830 Lowndes locator for James McLeod, while a contemporary-record transcription and an 1840 census locator strengthen John Lawson's civil identity. Alexander Campbell's indexed census trail extends from 1830 through 1850.

    The profiles keep later family and institutional claims qualified. Simpson's kinship to Simeon and Archibald, Thigpen's church and family history, McLeod's commission and Bethlehem roles, Campbell's wife and daughter relationships, and Lawson's family and Barney-district land remain secondary-supported until parent-naming, deed, probate, church, or other original records are inspected.

    Hall family identity and parentage update

    Four evidence-based profile revisions

    The John Hall profile now separates John Hall Sr. from John Hall Jr. The older man is documented as Morven postmaster and at District 12, Lot 270; the younger man's 1840–1870 household chain makes him William M. Hall's strong-probable father. Francis Pearson Hall's 1938 death certificate identifies John Jr.'s later wife as Elizabeth Easter and verifies her as Francis's mother. Her age and the earlier household structure show that she was William's probable stepmother, while William's biological mother remains unresolved.

    William M. Hall's Barney household page now incorporates the original 1870 marriage return, Serena Hires Hall's duplicate 1870 enumeration beside her parents, and all three pages of William's 1907 pension packet. The pension confirms his service history, residence, occupation, poverty, and disabilities but names no parents. The William G. Hall page now explains why his 1840s ownership of Lots 132–133 prevents the later “heirs of John Hall” deed from being assigned safely without the original instrument and title chain. Sion Hall's page treats John Sr. as his secondary-supported probable brother, based on a later obituary rather than original parentage proof.

    Fifth early-settler cohort and Yates–Hiers update

    Six new evidence-graded profiles

    New profiles cover Jared Johnson, William Hancock, William G. Hunter, Ashley Lawson, William Yates, and Rebecca Yates Hiers. The Johnson page corrects a former 1830 census attribution: the household head was Jared Singleton. Hancock’s first-sheriff identification remains strong secondary evidence because the original election and census records have not been inspected. Hunter and Lawson are anchored in the future-Brooks area by land, militia, church, and public records with their unresolved family claims kept qualified.

    Original deeds correct the Yates–Hiers land record. Owen Yates sold David Hiers about 580 acres in 1864. David’s later conveyance to Rebecca was one $2,500 deed dated October 27, 1875—not four deeds dated October 7—and covered about 545 acres. William Yates’s 1874 inventory was valued at $12,073.80. Estate vouchers document purchases for Mrs. and Miss Hiers and a $1,496.96 debt to David Hiers, but no heir list or parent-naming statement identifies Rebecca as William’s daughter. That relationship remains strong probable, while Rebecca’s 1900 residence with son Sim Hires in Barney and her daughter Nancy’s marriage to Joseph J. Rogers are documented.

    October 3, 2026

    Joshua and David Platt family evidence

    Two evidence-based profile revisions

    A 2006 compiled genealogy identifies David Platt as a son of Rev. Joshua Platt and Amy Newell. David's 1818 Bulloch County marriage and the two men's appearances on the same 1820 Bulloch census page and later in Lowndes County support the chronology and migration. No inspected original record names David's parents, so both profiles grade the relationship as secondary-supported rather than verified.

    Priscilla Folsom and Rogers evidence update

    One new profile and five evidence-based revisions

    A new Priscilla Jane Folsom profile follows her from the 1850 Lowndes household through her 1900 widowed household in Quitman. An 1861 deed-index entry verifies her full married name and independent Brooks County landholding. The page identifies her as Israel Lee Folsom’s verified mother while keeping Rogers as a secondary-supported maiden surname and her exact birth and death dates unresolved.

    The Ebenezer and Israel Lee Folsom pages now reflect that distinction. The Uriah Rogers, Thomas F. Rogers, and Joseph J. Rogers pages incorporate the inspected deeds, estate records, marriage, and guardianship evidence without treating land associations as kinship. No inspected record connects Priscilla or Joseph’s branch to the James–Thomas–Isaac Rogers group.

    Rogers, Hall, Platt, and Strickland cohort

    Five new evidence-graded profiles

    New profiles cover Uriah Rogers, John Hall, David Platt, Simeon Strickland, and Archibald Strickland. Original records place Uriah in the future-Brooks area by 1844, John in the 660th District in 1836, and David in Lowndes County in 1830. David’s precise future-Brooks location remains unproved.

    The Strickland profiles incorporate the authenticated deed and census follow-up. An 1826 Lowndes deed verifies that Simpson and Simeon Strickland witnessed the same transaction, while a later affidavit records Simpson’s knowledge of Simeon’s signature. A secondary local history identifies them as brothers and sons of Archibald and Luander Strickland; Archibald’s 1820 household fits that reconstruction, but no inspected original record names the relationship. No canonical Barney census household is presently connected to Simpson or Simeon.

    Folsom household cohort and Thomas F. Rogers

    Seven new evidence-graded profiles

    New profiles cover John, Elijah, Pennywell, pioneer Israel, Thomas, and Ebenezer Folsom, together with Thomas F. Rogers. The update corrects Pennywell’s death to July 15, 1836, removes the unsupported claim that he was Lawrence Folsom’s son, and identifies his reported sibling relationship to Elizabeth Folsom as secondary-supported. Pioneer Israel remains separate from Israel Lee Folsom of Barney. Ebenezer’s household chain supplies the collateral bridge between them.

    The original 1844 Lowndes tax digest verifies Thomas F. Rogers with 163 acres in District 12, Land Lot 199. James Rogers returned another 163 acres in the same lot, and Uriah Rogers appears on the following line. The cluster and Thomas’s 1859 deed to Ebenezer Folsom strengthen a Rogers association, but they do not establish Priscilla Jane Folsom’s parents or exact relationship to the three men.

    The John and James Groover profiles now explain the family's collateral connection to Barney. Compiled Groover family evidence places Sarah Reiser Groover as a daughter of Charles Groover, the reported brother of John Sr.; a 1913 biographical sketch names Sarah as the mother of M. Franklin Jones. The Jones chain into Barney is well documented, while Sarah's parentage and the older Groover sibling link remain identified as strong secondary evidence.

    Five new early-settler profiles examine John Groover Sr., James Groover, Canneth or Kenneth Swain, Morgan G. Swain, and Matthew Allbritton. The original 1830 census verifies the Groover and Allbritton county residences described in their profiles. Conflicting arrival dates, same-name households, the Canneth/Kenneth identity, Morgan's parentage and early land, and every claimed direct connection to Barney remain qualified where the surviving records do not settle them.

    Seventeen early-settler profiles reviewed

    Three five-person reviews and a two-subject follow-up cover George Alderman, Nathan Gornto, Drewry Vickers, Sion Hall, William Blair, William Alderman, Ely Webb, Joel Gornto, Alfred Belote, Willis King, Daniel McCranie Sr., Francis Rountree, Washington Joyce, Abraham Hunter, William Smith, the Jose/Joe Bryant–John Bryan identity question, and William G. Hall. The revised profiles separate original records from later tradition and state plainly when an arrival date, family relationship, land claim, or direct connection to Barney remains unproved.

    The latest additions document William Alderman in Lowndes County by 1833, Joel Gornto’s 1836 militia office and 1860 probate, and Alfred Belote’s census and land-tax trail. They also treat Ely Webb’s relationship to Malinda Webb Chambers as strong probable and Willis King’s reported 1823 move as a relocation theory rather than his first arrival in the county.

    The third review confirms that Daniel McCranie Sr., Francis Rountree, Washington Joyce, and William Smith already have publication-ready profiles. A new Abraham Hunter profile follows his probable move from Screven County to Lowndes County after 1830 and explains why the surviving chronology conflicts with the traditional 1823 settlement date.

    The follow-up revises the Jose Bryant page without merging Jose or Joe with John Bryan. It adds John’s reconstructed District 12 deed locators while explaining why the apparent 1824 instrument is not yet verified residence evidence. A new William G. Hall profile documents his 1830–1844 tax trail, Mount Zion trusteeship, and 1836 militia office. Hall’s reported 1824–1825 arrival and any direct residence in Barney remain unproved.

    October 2, 2026

    Amos Emanuel and the proposed Little River ferry

    New profile

    A new Amos Emanuel profile brings together the 1827 ferry bill, Senate vote, census trail, Lowndes County tax entry, 1838 militia record, and later Ware County household. The page does not call him a verified ferry operator: the bill is absent from the published session laws, no record proves that the ferry opened, and Emanuel's land has not been tied to Miller Bridge, Folsom Bridge, or another crossing. It also identifies “Amos Enoch Emanuel” and “Martha Elizabeth Jones” as unverified names found in public family trees.

    William and Elizabeth Folsom original deeds

    Expanded original-record profile

    The William Folsom dossier now incorporates four administrator's deeds proving at least 2,080 acres in his estate. The deeds identify District 9 Land Lots 492, 519, and 521, 120 acres of Lot 491, and District 12 Lot 323, together with the purchasers and auction prices. A separate executor's deed records Elizabeth Folsom's conveyance of District 9 Lots 493 and 520 to her daughter Edith Rountree. The Early Settlers and Folsom family pages now explain how these land transfers and Elizabeth's will connect the Folsom, Ryall, Rountree, and Perry families north of Barney. William's acquisition deeds, complete acreage, and the original 1867 road record remain unresolved.

    October 1, 2026

    Wider early-settler verification

    Research update

    The Early Settlers section now reports the results of an independent review of eighty additional roster names. A digitized 1836 Lowndes County general-militia return supplies narrow district and rank evidence for fourteen named men, while federal official registers place Hamilton W. Sharpe at Sharpe's Store by 1829. The update also states what the review did not prove: no newly inspected census image verified a named household from the wider roster, and traditional arrival dates remain attributed to their retrospective sources.

    The review preserved identity safeguards for same-name people and separate generations. It did not promote possible matches, surname associations, or reel-level census references into verified biographies.

    Expanded early-settler descendant crosswalk

    New family connections

    The Families & Connections section now documents 22 residents of the 1900 Barney census through 29 graded paths to eight early settlers. New pages establish three qualified George Alderman lines and two Joel Gornto lines. The Swain and Strickland proposals remain under research: an 1858 guardianship record supports Thomas E. B. Swain as Stephen Swain's father, but the Morgan G. Swain generation remains unproved, and no reviewed record connects Stephen Smith Strickland to the earlier regional Strickland families.

    September 30, 2026

    Folsom settler-descendant link

    New family connection

    Jeremiah Walton Folsom's dossier now traces his reported line to early settler Lawrence Folsom and links to the Lawrence and William Folsom dossiers. The page separates the census-supported father-son relationship from the earlier generations supplied by compiled genealogy, for which no parent-naming original has been reviewed at every step.

    FamilySearch review of eleven contemporary-report identities

    Research correction

    An authenticated FamilySearch review verified Rev. J. W. Folsom as Jeremiah Walton Folsom through Brooks County census and voter records. It also established the broader civil record of county coroner E. D. Ponder and found his exact-name Confederate pension index. Jesse Chambers received an independent Brooks County voter-registration bridge. Eight other identities remain possible because no record securely joined them to the Barney reports.

    Contemporary-report dossiers and military review

    New research

    The 1900 census page now links individual dossiers for all 15 people whose identities are verified or strong probable. Possible and unresolved identities remain unlinked. A military-record audit also corrected Joseph J. Oglesby's status: near-contemporary and county-history accounts establish his Confederate service, although his detailed company, rank, and Macon shop assignment still await original military records.

    Original-record research on unresolved contemporary-report names

    Research correction

    Original-record searches strengthen two identities. The 1901 inquest juror J. W. Kendrick is probably Jason W. Kendrick of Brooks County, and the 1899 teacher Minnie McKinnon is probably Minnie A. McKinnon of Hahira. Eighteen names remain unresolved after census, marriage, tax, court, school, business, newspaper, and military checks. The revised table records the strongest available finding without assigning same-name records that lack a Barney connection.

    Joyce and Culbreth second-round research

    Research correction

    New census, cemetery, and military records strengthen Janie Joyce as a daughter of John Wesley P. Joyce and verify Minnie Adelia Culbreth as Blackman Hawley Culbreth's daughter. They also correct Blackman's birth year to 1847 and establish his Confederate service in North Carolina units.

    Hall cluster second-round research

    Research correction

    A second review separated the Hall references into at least two families. Mrs. J. B. Hall is now verified as Lula S. Collingsworth Hall. The 1899 merchant belongs to the older household-1 J. F. Hall family, while the railroad conductor remains unresolved. Frank Henry Hall is the leading candidate for the early merchant Frank Hall, but direct Barney business evidence has not been found. Mrs. Frank Hall and the 1901 juror George P. Hall remain unresolved. No Hall military service was proved.

    Contemporary reports reconciliation

    Research correction

    The 44 people named in contemporary reports but previously left unmatched were reviewed individually. The revised census page now distinguishes verified, strong probable, possible, and unresolved identities and explains which people lived outside the Barney district or died before the census. Later military follow-up established Confederate service for Blackman Hawley Culbreth and Joseph J. Oglesby.

    September 27, 2026

    Hope and Jack Croft dossiers

    New research

    The Hope Croft dossier brings together the 1887 Stonewall Academy reports, the community resolution requiring the Croft family to leave, census evidence for Hope's likely identity as Abba or Abner Croft, his probable adult record as George A. Croft, and the younger namesake killed near Barney in 1922. The proposed connection to the 1885–1886 Morven-area fires remains unproved.

    The revised Jacob Henry “Jack” Croft dossier covers his family, Confederate service and amputation, Brooks County farm, Land Lot 227 conveyance, removal from the community, second marriage, later life, and estate. It also corrects the geography: Croft's tract belonged to the Morven district during his residence, before the McNeil & Rozier company village developed there.

    Initial website release

    Site update

    The first public edition was published at barneyga.com. Research currently extends through 1910, with later material included where it helps explain Barney's roads, bridges, railroads, and community sites.

    The launch edition included:

    • New research: expanded accounts of early settlement, Mount Zion, county formation, early roads, Folsom Bridge, and the communities along Little River.
    • New research: separate histories of the South Georgia Railway, the Tallokas-to-Barney tram, the Valdosta, Moultrie and Western, and the proposed Georgia, Alabama and Western Railroad.
    • New research: reorganized coverage of Barney before incorporation, the 1903 charter, early municipal government, commerce, postal service, community life, and public order.
    • Correction: the earlier 1910 census totals were withdrawn after a systematic extraction error was identified. Household information is used only where it has been checked, and a page-by-page reconstruction is underway.
    • New images: historical maps, photographs, restored images, and clearly labeled AI-generated reconstructions were added throughout the site.
