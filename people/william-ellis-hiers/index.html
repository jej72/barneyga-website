    William Ellis Hiers¶

    William Ellis Hiers (October 4, 1889–March 15, 1966) is the strong-probable identity of the W. E. Hiers elected mayor of Barney on December 15, 1911. The election report gives only the mayor's initials, but the combined census, newspaper, draft-registration, and family evidence points to William Ellis Hiers. No competing adult W. E. Hiers has been found in the relevant Barney records.

    Identity and family¶

    The 1900 census records ten-year-old Ellis Hiers in the Brooks County household of William T. and Fannie Hiers. His siblings included Hubert, Thomas, Stanley, and Susie. The census gives his birth as October 1889. A World War I draft card later expands his name to William Ellis Hires and gives his birth date as October 4, 1889. The spellings Hiers and Hires were both used in records for this family.

    William T. Hiers farmed in the Morven district and was among the petitioners for the Barney Supply Company charter in 1909. That record connects the family to Barney's mercantile economy before the 1911 election.

    The wider Hiers family¶

    The census record places William T. Hiers with his parents, William and Susan Hiers, in 1870 and explicitly identifies him as their son in 1880. The elder William appears in 1850 in the Lowndes County household of Daniel and Nancy Hiers. Separate records place David Hiers, husband of Rebecca Yates, in the same larger family. David and Rebecca were the parents of Sim or Simeon Hires and Nancy Jane Hires Rogers.

    These records support William Ellis Hiers as a first cousin once removed of both Sim Hires and Nancy Jane Hires Rogers. The relationship is strong probable rather than verified because the inspected records do not directly name both the elder William and David as sons of Daniel and Nancy. The 1850 households, later census continuity, Daniel Hiers estate activity, and compiled family records support that sibling relationship.

    Other Barney Hiers households belong to a second collateral branch. The 1860 and 1870 censuses place John A. Hiers in Phillip Hiers's household, and the 1880 census calls John a nephew of the elder William Hiers. Those records support Phillip and William as brothers. On that evidence, William Ellis Hiers was a first cousin once removed of John A. Hiers and Jacob Hunter Hiers. Mitchell Hiers also appears to belong to Phillip's branch, although his parentage remains less firmly documented; if that placement is correct, William Ellis was Mitchell's first cousin once removed and Gordon Hiers's second cousin. These extended relationships remain strong probable rather than verified.

    Barney and the 1911 election¶

    The 1910 census records “Ellis Hires,” age twenty, boarding with James H. Culpepper on Main Street in Barney. He worked as a dry-goods salesman. On December 19, 1911, the Atlanta Semi-Weekly Journal reported that Barney voters had elected W. E. Hiers mayor four days earlier. The same report named five councilmen and Marshal A. T. Moody.

    Barney's charter provided for annual municipal elections, so the 1911 result ordinarily describes a one-year office. It does not prove that Hiers remained mayor through 1913. By September 1913, a newspaper described Ellis Hiers as living in Jacksonville and visiting relatives near Barney. An October notice named W. E. Hiers among those attending the opening of Barney High School. The second notice shows that he maintained local ties after moving away; it does not establish that he was still mayor or had moved back to Barney.

    The 1913 notices, together with the 1910 census, make William Ellis Hiers the only documented local candidate who fits the mayor's initials, age, residence, and chronology. The identification remains strong probable, not verified. No located election return, oath, or contemporary personal notice explicitly expands “Mayor W. E. Hiers” to “William Ellis Hiers.” See The next documented administration.

    Later life¶

    By the World War I registration period, Hiers lived in Atlanta and was married. The 1920 census records William E. Hiers and wife Ophelia as boarders there; he worked as a postal clerk. The 1930 and 1940 censuses describe him as a mail clerk, and the 1950 census records him as a hotel clerk.

    An obituary published March 16, 1966, identifies William Ellis Hiers as a Brooks County native and the husband of Ophelia Hollingsworth. It names brothers whose names agree with the earlier Hiers household. He died March 15, 1966, and was buried at Greenwood Cemetery in Atlanta.
