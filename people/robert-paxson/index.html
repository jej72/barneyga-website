    Robert Paxson

    Back to the contemporary-reports table

    Identity grade: Strong probable

    Robert Paxson is strongly identified as the 1899 smallpox victim associated with Barney's milling economy and as a brother of Charles Dorothus Paxson.

    Barney connection

    A sequence of August and September 1899 reports uses Paxson, Paxon, and Paxton. The Atlanta report calls the patient R. Paxton and names C. D. Paxton as his brother. The broader Macon sequence supplies Robert.

    Census and civil record

    Robert died before the 1900 census. Charles Dorothus Paxson headed Barney household 197, providing the local family bridge. Robert's own age and complete civil record have not been recovered.

    Military review

    No service was proved. Robert's age is unknown, so same-name military records cannot be graded reliably.

    Evidence limits

    The identification rests on the unusual surname, brother C. D., event sequence, and Barney mill context. A death, probate, cemetery, or family record explicitly joining Robert and Charles would make it verified.

    Sources

    1. Atlanta Constitution, August 18, 1899
    2. Charles D. Paxson household dossier
