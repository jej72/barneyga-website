    Sion L. Hall

    Merchant · justice of the peace · Barney's strongly supported first mayor

    Census records place Sion L. Hall in the Hall family from childhood. The 1900 census identifies him as a Barney merchant, newspapers connect his store with the post office and justice-court papers, and two 1904 notices identify him as mayor immediately after Barney's first scheduled municipal election.

    Identity and family

    Sion L. Hall was born in Georgia on February 11, 1868, according to his cemetery memorial. The 1870 census places two-year-old Sion—indexed as “Lion”—in the household of Enoch Hall and Hannah Elizabeth Gornto Hall. The 1880 census explicitly records him as their son. This makes the younger Sion distinct from his grandfather, the early Coffee Road settler Sion Hall.

    Hall married Malpha or Malphia Westberry. In the 1900 Barney census, he was thirty-two and a merchant; Malpha was twenty-six. Their household included daughters Alice, Mattie, Willie, and Fannie May. The 1910 household added Ruth, Reva, Thelma, and a son recorded as S. L. Hall. Later records follow Mattie as Mattie Mae Hall Parrish and preserve additional daughter lines, but several child-by-child trails still need tighter original-record review. See the complete household dossier.

    Merchant, justice, and the 1899 fire

    Hall's public role began before Barney incorporated. Huxford's commission list begins the 1571st Barney District with S. L. Hall on February 16, 1899, making him the first recorded justice of the peace for the district. A newspaper account later that year independently confirms the office.

    On November 8, 1899, the Savannah Morning News reported that Hall's Barney storehouse had burned. The post office operated inside the building, and the fire destroyed merchandise, mail, postal records, and Hall's justice-of-the-peace papers. The loss was reported at about $600, with no insurance. The evidence does not show that Hall was the federal postmaster; it shows that his store housed the post office and that his legal papers were kept there.

    In December 1900, a local election notice again named S. L. Hall as justice of the peace. Together, the commission list, fire report, and election notice show that Hall's store joined retail trade, postal service, and local legal business before Barney had a municipal government.

    First mayor of Barney

    Georgia incorporated Barney on August 6, 1903, and scheduled its first municipal election for the first Wednesday in December. The Valdosta Times published Hall as the nominated mayoral candidate on December 5, together with five council nominees.

    No election return or oath of office has been located. Two contemporary notices nevertheless establish Hall in office. On January 8, 1904, The Adel News called him “Mayor S. L. Hall, of Barney.” On June 24, the paper again referred to “Mayor Hall of Barney.” Because both notices follow immediately after the charter's first scheduled election, Sion L. Hall is strongly supported as Barney's inaugural mayor. W. E. Hiers, elected in December 1911, is the next documented mayor; missing returns prevent treating him as necessarily Hall's immediate successor.

    Later life

    The census trail follows Hall and Malpha through 1910, 1920, 1930, and 1940. Later enumerations describe him in work connected with railroad ties or inspection and farming, showing a career broader than the merchant label recorded in 1900. A 1910 marriage notice places daughter Mattie's wedding to H. F. Parrish at the Barney home of Mr. and Mrs. S. L. Hall.

    Hall's memorial gives his death year as 1942 and places him in Hall Cemetery near Morven beside Malpha. The memorial supplies useful family and burial evidence, but an original death certificate or obituary has not been inspected for this dossier; the exact death date therefore remains unresolved here.

    Family and settler connection

    Path: Sion Hall → Enoch Hall → Sion L. Hall
    Grade: Secondary-supported across the full three-generation line.
    Evidence: The 1870 and 1880 censuses establish the younger Sion in Enoch and Hannah Hall's household, with the 1880 schedule naming him as a son. Huxford and nineteenth-century accounts identify Enoch as the elder Sion Hall's son.
    Open issue: No reviewed original probate or Bible record names all three generations together.

    Evidence summary

    • Verified: son of Enoch and Hannah Hall; Barney merchant in 1900; justice of the peace by 1899; identified as Barney's mayor in two 1904 newspapers.
    • Strong probable: Barney's first mayor, based on the charter's election date and the immediate 1904 notices.
    • Secondary-supported: grandson of the elder Sion Hall across the complete three-generation line.
    • Unresolved: the missing first-election return, the exact limits of Hall's mayoral term, the location of his store, and his exact death date.
