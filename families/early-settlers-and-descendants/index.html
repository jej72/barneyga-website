    Early settlers and their descendants

    This crosswalk publishes the supported results of the 1900 Barney census audit and its later expansion. It includes 22 residents and 29 separately graded paths. A person may appear under more than one settler line.

    Evidence grades

    • Census- or vital-record supported: recent parent-child steps are named or placed in compatible households by census, marriage, death, or comparable records.
    • Secondary-supported: a compiled genealogy supplies one or more early generations while local records independently establish the people involved.
    • Strong probable: the relationship is well supported, but one part of the settler claim still lacks direct settlement-era proof.

    Each generation is judged separately. Marriage, surname, proximity, or a shared household does not by itself establish descent.

    George Alderman

    George Washington Alderman

    Path: George Alderman → Jonathan Howell Alderman → George Washington Alderman
    Grade: Strong Probable
    Evidence: George Washington Alderman's death certificate names J. H. Alderman as father; the 1870 and 1880 censuses place him in Jonathan's household. The 1880 and 1850 censuses anchor Jonathan as George Alderman's son. Huxford names George among the 1827 settlers.
    Open issue: No reviewed original probate names the full chain; the 1827 arrival year is from later county history.

    James Carlton Alderman

    Path: George Alderman → William Alderman → James Carlton Alderman
    Grade: Strong Probable
    Evidence: James Carlton Alderman's death certificate names William Alderman as father, and the 1860 census places James in William's household. The 1850 family reconstruction and Aldermans in America identify William as George Alderman's son. Huxford names George among the 1827 settlers.
    Open issue: The George > William step should be strengthened with probate, guardianship, or a parent-naming deed; George's exact arrival year remains retrospective.

    Jonathan Howell Alderman

    Path: George Alderman → Jonathan Howell Alderman
    Grade: Strong Probable
    Evidence: The 1880 census places George and Nancy Alderman in Jonathan H. Alderman's household as his parents; the 1850 census places Jonathan in George's household. Huxford's arrival roster names George Alderman among the 1827 settlers and the broader antebellum roster documents the family in the future Brooks geography.
    Open issue: An original probate or parent-naming deed for George > Jonathan would raise the lineage step to original-record supported; George's exact arrival year remains retrospective.

    Scope

    The audit reviewed 385 unique people: 201 heads, 162 spouses, and 22 boarders. No lodgers were enumerated. The complete person-level audit remains an internal research control. Absence from this published crosswalk means only that no qualifying path has yet been documented; it is not proof that no relationship existed.
