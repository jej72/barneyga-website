    The Folsom Family¶

    The Folsom surname appears in several early Lowndes County and Barney-area families. The surviving evidence does not support treating every Folsom as part of one proved line. This page keeps the documented branches separate and explains where the proposed links remain unproved.

    Elijah Folsom: a traditional candidate¶

    Elijah Folsom remains a traditional early-settler candidate. Folks Huxford wrote that he came to the territory of present Brooks County in 1824 or 1825, but no contemporary record inspected for this audit identifies Elijah's household, land, or residence there.

    Elizabeth Knowles Folsom's 1938 genealogy gives the following reconstruction:

    William Folsom + Sally Armstrong → Lawrence Folsom + Rachel Vickers → Elijah Folsom + Nancy Morrison → Green, Martha, Jackson, Elizabeth, Macon, and Elijah.

    That is the strongest compiled theory, not a proved lineage. No inspected parent-, spouse-, or child-naming original establishes the Elijah links. The proposed six children remain unresolved individually, and the reported move to Florida lacks a county, date, and original record.

    Elijah identity controls¶

    At least three people must remain separate unless new records connect them:

    1. the proposed early-settler Elijah;
    2. the younger Elijah whom the 1938 genealogy calls his unmarried son; and
    3. the much later Elijah identified as the father of Ella Dee Folsom, born in 1902.

    The claimed 1832 Georgia Land Lottery draw for Carroll County Lot 255 is not used as evidence. No page, draw category, residence, stable identifier, or identity bridge to the proposed pioneer has been recovered.

    The Lawrence and William branch¶

    Lawrence Folsom is independently documented by an 1823 Irwin County deed and a later Lowndes County record chain. Those records establish Lawrence's presence; they do not prove that Elijah was his son.

    The separate William Folsom and Elizabeth Folsom branch has a stronger original-record trail. Elizabeth's will identifies Edith Rountree as her daughter and names Rachel Ann Ryall's four children. Estate deeds document land passing through the Ryall, Rountree, Perry, Joyce, Pounds, Ingram, and Jones families. Those descendants reached Barney, but no inspected record joins their branch to Elijah generation by generation.

    Known 1900 Barney descendants¶

The earlier Lawrence-to-William step remains supported by compiled genealogy and circumstantial records rather than a parent-naming original.

    The Priscilla Jane and Israel Lee branch¶

    The best documented Barney surname branch runs through Priscilla Jane Folsom and Israel Lee Folsom. The 1880 census directly identifies Priscilla as Israel Lee's mother. Household continuity makes Priscilla the strong-probable wife of Ebenezer Folsom, although no original marriage record has been located.

    The proposed relationship between the older Ebenezer and pioneer Israel Folsom remains secondary-supported. No reviewed record joins this branch to Elijah.

    The Barney census also contains Folsom households that cannot yet be placed safely. The 1920 schedules include F. B. Folsom and Israel Lee's household, while several 1930 groups remain unreconciled. Shared surname and proximity are not enough to assign them to Elijah or another pioneer line.

    Other early Folsoms¶

    Pennywell, pioneer Israel, John, Thomas, Ebenezer, and several men named William appear in early regional records. They remain separate identity controls unless a relationship-naming record connects them. The proposed Lawrence-and-Rachel sibling group—William, Randal, John, Elijah, Maston, Cynthia, Elizabeth, and James—is secondary-supported by compiled genealogy rather than proved by inspected originals.

    Jesse Folsom does not supply a South Georgia bridge. The identifiable Jesse in the 1938 genealogy was born in New Hampshire in 1817 and lived there. No distinct South Georgia Jesse was found in the documented local searches.

    Evidence grades¶

    • Traditional / unresolved: Elijah's reported 1824–1825 arrival in future Brooks County.
    • Secondary-supported / possible: Lawrence and Rachel as Elijah's parents; Nancy Morrison as his wife; the reported move to Florida.
    • Secondary-supported as a compiled group; unresolved individually: Green, Martha, Jackson, Elizabeth, Macon, and the younger Elijah as their children.
    • Unsupported; rejected as current evidence: the unlocated 1832 Carroll County Lot 255 assertion.
    • Unresolved: any generation-by-generation connection between Elijah and a documented Barney Folsom branch.

    What would settle the open questions¶

    The most useful missing records are Lawrence and Rachel's probate or distribution papers naming children; an original record naming Elijah with Nancy or one of the six proposed children; a county-specific Florida record; and child-specific Alabama, Georgia, or military records that identify parents or close associates. The complete 1832 lottery entry would also be needed before the Lot 255 claim could be reconsidered.
