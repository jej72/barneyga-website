    The Rogers Family¶

    The Rogers families around Foster and Barney cannot all be joined from surname and proximity alone. The best-documented local branch begins with Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks and continues through their son Joseph J. Rogers. A second child, Mary Ann Rogers Folsom, is strongly probable from the 1850 household and later census chain. A collateral line through James Rogers, Thomas F. Rogers, Aaron J. Rogers, and Arthur Richmond Rogers is supported by Ancestry trees and several original records, but the older parent-child links remain unproved. The parentage of Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom also remains unresolved.

    Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks¶

    The 1850 Lowndes County census places Uriah and Elizabeth Rogers in one household with Mary A. Rogers, age eighteen, and Joseph Rogers, age eleven. The census did not state family relationships. Joseph's 1922 Georgia death certificate later names his parents as Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks, verifying his place in this family.[1][2]

    Mary's place is less direct. She next appears as Mary Ann Folsom, born about 1832–1833 in Georgia, with Lawrence A. Folsom in the 1860, 1870, and 1880 Berrien County censuses. Her age, birthplace, name, and disappearance from the Rogers household after marriage make her identification as Uriah and Elizabeth's daughter—and Joseph's older sister—strong probable. No inspected probate, Bible, obituary, or death record names her parents directly.[1][3]

    Joseph and Nancy Hires Rogers¶

    Joseph J. Rogers married Nancy J. Hires in Brooks County on October 21, 1866. The original marriage record fixes the couple in Brooks County immediately after the Civil War. Nancy's 1932 death certificate names her parents as David Hires and Rebecca Yates, joining the Rogers household to the local Hires family.[4][5]

    Joseph and Nancy headed a farming household in Brooks County in 1870 and 1880. By 1900 they were enumerated at Barney with children Ophelia, Effie, Everett, Miles, Dove, and Willie. Joseph's 1890 tax return placed him in the Morven district with 340 acres in Land Lot 270. The 1908 Brooks County map marked “J. J. Rogers Res.” near the south end of Land Lot 274, close to the Foster neighborhood. In 1910 Joseph still owned his Barney farm free of mortgage.[6]

    Family tradition gives Joseph's middle name as Jasper. The census, marriage, tax, death, pension, and cemetery records inspected for this research use Joseph J., J. J., or Joe and do not expand the initial. “Joseph Jasper Rogers” is therefore retained as a family-reported name rather than an original-record fact.[6]

    Nancy Hires and the Hiers branches

    Nancy Jane Hires Rogers and Sim or Simeon Hires belonged to David Hires and Rebecca Yates’s family. The later census and family reconstruction makes William Ellis Hiers, the strong-probable identity of Barney’s 1911 mayor, their first cousin once removed. William Ellis was a son of William T. Hiers and a grandson of the elder William and Susan Hiers.

    The cousin relationship is strong probable: it depends on the elder William and David being brothers. The inspected records do not directly name both as sons of Daniel and Nancy Hiers. The Hiers dossier sets out that link and the separate collateral branch through Phillip Hiers.

    Barney family connections¶

    Dove Rogers and James I. Moody¶

    The 1900 census identifies Dove Rogers as a daughter of Joseph and Nancy. By 1910 she was the wife of Barney merchant James I. Moody. Nancy Rogers died at the Moody home in Barney in 1932.[7]

    Walter H. Rogers¶

    The 1880 census places Walton H. Rogers, age nine, in Joseph and Nancy’s household. His proposed identification with Walter H. Rogers remains unresolved. Walter’s separate Barney household appears in the 1900 census, and he married Lela E. Merchant in 1905. His family remained in the Barney area through the first half of the twentieth century.[8]

    Mary Ann Rogers Folsom and the Westberry line¶

    If the strong-probable sibling identification is correct, Mary Ann Rogers Folsom was Joseph's older sister. Mary married Lawrence A. Folsom. Their daughter Marietta Elizabeth Folsom married General Jackson Westberry on July 30, 1884. Marietta was therefore Joseph's niece, and her son Emerson Westberry was his great-nephew. His identification as councilman W. E. Westbury is strong probable; the combined full name Walter Emmerson remains unresolved.[1][3][9]

    The same relationship makes Marietta and Dove Rogers Moody first cousins. Emerson Westberry and Dove Moody were first cousins once removed under that reconstruction. This Rogers connection complements Marietta's close ties to the Folsom family: her grandfather John Folsom was a brother of pre-Barney landowner William Folsom, while the maternal Rogers line joined her to Joseph's Foster and Barney household.

    The James–Thomas–Aaron–Arthur branch¶

    Ancestry public trees place Thomas F. Rogers, born about 1820, as a son of James Rogers and Nancy Ann Giddens. The same compiled trees place James and Uriah Rogers as sons of William Thomas Rogers and Lucretia Treadwell. No inspected original record names Thomas as James's son or James and Uriah as brothers, so both links remain secondary-supported rather than verified.[10]

    The original local records are consistent with that reconstruction. In the 1844 Lowndes County tax digest, James Rogers and Thomas F. Rogers each returned 163 acres in District 12, Land Lot 199, and Uriah Rogers appeared immediately after Thomas. Thomas later administered James's estate. Those records establish a close Rogers land and estate network, but neither record states the men's kinship.[11][12]

    The later generations are clearer. The 1860 Brooks County census places ten-year-old Aaron Rogers in Thomas F. Rogers's household, and an 1868 guardianship bond appears to include Aaron among Thomas's orphan children. Aaron J. Rogers married Emma Cooper in Brooks County on July 9, 1868. Their son Arthur Richmond Rogers appears with them in the 1880 Colquitt County census, and Arthur's 1926 death certificate names A. J. Rogers and Emma Cooper as his parents. The continuous identity Thomas F. Rogers → Aaron J. Rogers → Arthur Richmond Rogers is therefore strong probable at the first link and verified at the second.[13][14][15]

    Arthur is also the strong-probable A. R. Rogers in Barney's LL321 deeds. Ancil Lawrence sold Arthur R. Rogers one acre there on February 22, 1897. In March 1898, A. R. Rogers sold half an acre to William M. Eatman and conveyed a separate sixty-five-acre tract in northeastern LL321 to G. W. Barrington. The matching name, initials, chronology, and Lawrence setting support the identification, although the later deeds use initials rather than Arthur's full name.[16][17]

    If the compiled parentage is correct, Thomas F. Rogers was Uriah's nephew; Aaron J. Rogers and Joseph J. Rogers were first cousins once removed; Arthur Richmond Rogers and Joseph were first cousins twice removed; and Arthur and Marietta Folsom Westberry were second cousins once removed. These relationships remain conditional until an original probate, family Bible, or other parent-naming record confirms that James and Uriah were brothers and that Thomas was James's son.

    Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom: a separate unresolved branch¶

    Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom, mother of Israel Lee “I. L.” Folsom, belongs in the Rogers research but cannot yet be joined securely to either Rogers branch. Cemetery and compiled family material identify her maiden name as Rogers. The Ancestry trees inspected for this study generally identify James Rogers as her father, but one names Nancy Ann Giddens as her mother while another names Nancy Wallace. Neither tree supplies an original record proving the assignment.

    If James was the father of both Priscilla and Thomas F. Rogers, Priscilla and Thomas were siblings—or half-siblings if the competing maternal assignments reflect two different wives. Under that reconstruction, Thomas's son Aaron J. Rogers and Priscilla's son Israel Lee “I. L.” Folsom were first cousins, and Arthur Richmond Rogers and I. L. Folsom were first cousins once removed. These relationships are secondary-supported and conditional, not established facts. No inspected probate distribution, family Bible, obituary, deed, or death record names Thomas and Priscilla together as James's children.

    Mary Ann Rogers Folsom and Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom should therefore not be described as sisters or cousins without new evidence. The shared Rogers surname and their marriages into nearby Folsom branches are research leads, not proof of kinship.

    Evidence grades¶

    • Verified: Joseph J. Rogers was a son of Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks.
    • Strong probable: Mary Ann Rogers Folsom was their daughter and Joseph's older sister.
    • Verified: Joseph married Nancy J. Hires; Nancy was a daughter of David Hires and Rebecca Yates.
    • Verified: Dove Rogers was Joseph and Nancy's daughter and married James I. Moody.
    • Unresolved: whether Walter H. Rogers was the Walton H. Rogers enumerated as Joseph and Nancy’s son in 1880.
    • Secondary-supported: James Rogers and Uriah Rogers were brothers, sons of William Thomas Rogers and Lucretia Treadwell.
    • Secondary-supported / strong probable: Thomas F. Rogers was James Rogers's son.
    • Strong probable: Aaron J. Rogers was Thomas F. Rogers's son.
    • Verified: Arthur Richmond Rogers was a son of Aaron J. Rogers and Emma Cooper.
    • Strong probable: Arthur Richmond Rogers was the A. R. Rogers who held and conveyed property in Barney's LL321.
    • Secondary-supported / unresolved: Ancestry trees identify James Rogers as Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom's father but disagree whether her mother was Nancy Ann Giddens or Nancy Wallace. If James also fathered Thomas F. Rogers, Priscilla and Thomas were siblings or half-siblings.
    • Family-reported: Joseph J. Rogers's middle name was Jasper.

    What would settle the open questions¶

    The most useful missing records are probate or family papers naming Uriah and Elizabeth's children; a death, obituary, Bible, or probate record naming Mary Ann's parents; James Rogers's complete probate distribution; an original record naming Thomas F. Rogers's parents; and a parent-naming record for Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom. Those sources could confirm Mary Ann's placement, test the proposed James–Thomas line, and determine whether Priscilla belonged to either branch.
