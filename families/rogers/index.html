    The Rogers Family¶

    The Rogers families around Foster and Barney cannot all be joined from surname and proximity alone. The best-documented local branch begins with Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks and continues through their son Joseph J. Rogers. A second child, Mary Ann Rogers Folsom, is strongly probable from the 1850 household and later census chain. The parentage of Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom remains unresolved, so her branch is kept separate.

    Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks¶

    The 1850 Lowndes County census places Uriah and Elizabeth Rogers in one household with Mary A. Rogers, age eighteen, and Joseph Rogers, age eleven. The census did not state family relationships. Joseph's 1922 Georgia death certificate later names his parents as Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks, verifying his place in this family.[1][2]

    Mary's place is less direct. She next appears as Mary Ann Folsom, born about 1832–1833 in Georgia, with Lawrence A. Folsom in the 1860, 1870, and 1880 Berrien County censuses. Her age, birthplace, name, and disappearance from the Rogers household after marriage make her identification as Uriah and Elizabeth's daughter—and Joseph's older sister—strong probable. No inspected probate, Bible, obituary, or death record names her parents directly.[1][3]

    Joseph and Nancy Hires Rogers¶

    Joseph J. Rogers married Nancy J. Hires in Brooks County on October 21, 1866. The original marriage record fixes the couple in Brooks County immediately after the Civil War. Nancy's 1932 death certificate names her parents as David Hires and Rebecca Yates, joining the Rogers household to the local Hires family.[4][5]

    Joseph and Nancy headed a farming household in Brooks County in 1870 and 1880. By 1900 they were enumerated at Barney with children Ophelia, Effie, Everett, Miles, Dove, and Willie. Joseph's 1890 tax return placed him in the Morven district with 340 acres in Land Lot 270. The 1908 Brooks County map marked “J. J. Rogers Res.” near the south end of Land Lot 274, close to the Foster neighborhood. In 1910 Joseph still owned his Barney farm free of mortgage.[6]

    Family tradition gives Joseph's middle name as Jasper. The census, marriage, tax, death, pension, and cemetery records inspected for this research use Joseph J., J. J., or Joe and do not expand the initial. “Joseph Jasper Rogers” is therefore retained as a family-reported name rather than an original-record fact.[6]

    Barney family connections¶

    Dove Rogers and James I. Moody¶

    The 1900 census identifies Dove Rogers as a daughter of Joseph and Nancy. By 1910 she was the wife of Barney merchant James I. Moody. Nancy Rogers died at the Moody home in Barney in 1932. This marriage connected the Rogers and Hires families directly to one of Barney's best-documented mercantile households.[7]

    Walter H. Rogers¶

    The 1880 census places Walton H. Rogers, age nine, in Joseph and Nancy's household. Later records identify him as Walter H. Rogers. His separate Barney household appears in the 1900 census, and he married Lela E. Merchant in 1905. The family remained in the Barney area through the first half of the twentieth century.[8]

    Mary Ann Rogers Folsom and the Westberry line¶

    If the strong-probable sibling identification is correct, Mary Ann Rogers Folsom was Joseph's older sister. Mary married Lawrence A. Folsom. Their daughter Marietta Elizabeth Folsom married General Jackson Westberry on July 30, 1884. Marietta was therefore Joseph's niece, and her son Walter Emmerson “W. E.” Westberry was his great-nephew.[1][3][9]

    The same relationship makes Marietta and Dove Rogers Moody first cousins. W. E. Westberry and Dove Moody were first cousins once removed. This Rogers connection complements Marietta's close ties to the Folsom family: her grandfather John Folsom was a brother of pre-Barney landowner William Folsom, while the maternal Rogers line joined her to Joseph's Foster and Barney household.

    Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom: a separate unresolved branch¶

    Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom, mother of Israel Lee “I. L.” Folsom, belongs in the Rogers research but cannot yet be joined to Uriah's family. Cemetery and compiled family material identify her maiden name as Rogers. Ancestry trees propose James Rogers and Nancy Wallace as her parents, but no inspected original record establishes that parentage or connects her to Uriah and Elizabeth's branch.

    Mary Ann Rogers Folsom and Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom should therefore not be described as sisters or cousins without new evidence. The shared Rogers surname and their marriages into nearby Folsom branches are research leads, not proof of kinship.

    Evidence grades¶

    • Verified: Joseph J. Rogers was a son of Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks.
    • Strong probable: Mary Ann Rogers Folsom was their daughter and Joseph's older sister.
    • Verified: Joseph married Nancy J. Hires; Nancy was a daughter of David Hires and Rebecca Yates.
    • Verified: Dove Rogers was Joseph and Nancy's daughter and married James I. Moody.
    • Strongly supported: Walter H. Rogers was the Walton H. Rogers enumerated as their son in 1880.
    • Unresolved: the parents of Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom and her relationship, if any, to Uriah's branch.
    • Family-reported: Joseph J. Rogers's middle name was Jasper.

    What would settle the open questions¶

    The most useful missing records are probate or family papers naming Uriah and Elizabeth's children; a death, obituary, Bible, or probate record naming Mary Ann's parents; and a parent-naming record for Priscilla Jane Rogers Folsom. Those sources could confirm Mary Ann's placement and determine whether the two Rogers-Folsom women belonged to the same family.
