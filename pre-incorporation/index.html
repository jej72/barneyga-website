    Pre-Incorporation Era, 1897-1903

    Barney developed around the South Georgia Railroad for six years before it became an incorporated town. The pages in this section follow that change from the Foster-area settlement to a railroad community with stores, schools, churches, mills, farms, public services, and local government.

    Explore the era

    • Origins: Foster to Barney — the railroad, postal relocation, first residences, and the origin of the town's name.
    • Transportation and communication — passenger trains, mail routes, roads, and early telephone service.
    • Lumber, turpentine, and timber trams — the industries that supported the new station.
    • Farming, livestock, and markets — agriculture in the district and the railroad's effect on local trade.
    • Stores, trades, and the developing town — merchants, workers, fires, and Barney's business center.
    • People and community life — population, families, travel, and social ties.
    • Schools and churches — education and organized worship before municipal government.
    • Disease, fire, violence, and law — public health, disasters, crime, and local justice.
    • The road to incorporation — the 1903 campaign and act that created the town government.

    Together, these records show that Barney already had the institutions of a town before its incorporation on August 6, 1903.
