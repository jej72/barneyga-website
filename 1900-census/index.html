    Public Analysis

    The 1900 census captures Barney just before incorporation, with 992 people in 201 households. Household size ranged from 1 to 13 people, with an average of 4.9 people per household. The record shows a working sawmill, railroad, farm, mercantile, and turpentine community tied together by kinship, boarding arrangements, and labor networks.

    The relationship counts show why household pages matter: the census includes children, spouses, extended kin, boarders, employees, servants, and neighbors whose later records often explain how Barney families connected across surnames. Age and name readings remain tied to the source notes on the household pages where the encyclopedia flags uncertain handwriting or later-record conflicts.
