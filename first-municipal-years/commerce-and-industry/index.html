    Commerce and industry

    Barney's first municipal years remained tied to timber, rail transportation, farming, and a small business district.

    Taxable property and regional industry

    Brooks County's 1903 tax digest listed the Barney district at $140,730 in taxable value, an increase of $17,426. The figure measures the district's tax base rather than its population, but it provides an economic baseline at the time of incorporation.

    Newspaper coverage in 1905 and 1906 placed Barney & Smith in discussions of timber and railroad development around Milltown, Naylor, and Valdosta. On October 4, 1906, the Valdosta Daily Times reported that three possible Milltown-to-Valdosta routes had been surveyed for N. E. Whitehurst, manager of the Barney-Smith railway interests. The survey recorded timber and open land along each route. These reports do not establish that Barney & Smith owned the South Georgia Railway.

    Edward C. Bridges's 1909 obituary supplies a personal example of the same economy. It says he moved to Brooks County nearly fifteen years earlier, settled at Barney, and worked in sawmilling with the Oglesbys before entering a wider group of lumber, grocery, hardware, banking, and naval-stores businesses.

    A March 27, 1910, Atlanta Journal county profile described the South Georgia Railway from Adel through Quitman to Perry, Florida, and named J. W. Oglesby as its president and superintendent and as a central figure in the Oglesby Lumber and Manufacturing Company. Barney was one town along that regional rail-and-lumber system.

    Cotton remained active as well. On August 13, 1909, the Valdosta Daily Times reported that the gins at Morven and Barney had run full time the previous day.

    The naval-stores trade also continued around Barney. On December 9, 1904, the Moultrie Observer described H. D. Woolard as a naval-stores operator of Barney.

    The business district in 1908

    A December 1908 town profile listed six general stores, a hardware and furniture business, a drugstore, a bank, a cotton gin, a meat market, a hotel, two blacksmith shops, a turpentine plant, a livery stable, a fireproof warehouse, two churches, and a school. It also counted four brick stores and a brick bank building, with two more brick buildings planned.

    The correspondent estimated that Barney conducted about $150,000 in business each year and called it the largest shipping point on the South Georgia Railway between Adel and Greenville except Quitman. Those claims appeared in an argument for a better road to Valdosta and should be read as promotion. The detailed inventory, however, documents the range of businesses operating in the town.

    Stores and fires

    On September 27 and 28, 1906, the Macon Telegraph and Valdosta Daily Times reported a fire at Walker & Moody's general-merchandise store. Fleming Bates Walker, identified in the reports as F. B. Walker, found the building filled with heat and smoke when he opened it at 6 a.m. Damage was estimated at about $1,500, most of it from smoke and water, and the cause was unknown.

    On April 24, 1908, the Valdosta Daily Times reported that P. Feingold's dry-goods store, opened about two weeks earlier, had burned completely. The stock was valued at approximately $2,400, with $2,000 in insurance.

    Banking and land promotion

    The Atlanta Journal reported on September 24, 1905, that Citizens Bank had been organized at Adel with $25,000 in capital and planned a branch at Barney. The announcement does not prove that the proposed branch opened.

    A Bank of Barney was operating by June 25, 1906, when the Atlanta Georgian listed its representative among those expected at a meeting of banks associated with W. S. Witham. The paper printed the representative's initials as B. L. Long. On September 27, the same newspaper identified Ben F. Long as cashier of a bank at Barney. The December 1908 town profile confirms an operating bank and a separate brick bank building.

    In February 1909, The Bankers Magazine reported, “Barney—Bank of Barney; title changed to Barney Banking Co.” A 1909-1910 Georgia business directory named W. D. Manley as president, Fleming Bates Walker as vice-president, and B. F. Long as cashier. The directory printed Walker's name as “F. B. Walker.” The evidence does not establish how the proposed Citizens Bank branch, a later-reported Bank of Adel connection, and the Bank of Barney were related. Both Bank of Barney and Barney Banking Company continued to appear in later records.

    Advertisements in September 1910 promoted farms in and around the Barney district. One listed land less than a quarter mile from Barney's businesses, churches, and schoolhouse. Another promoted Brooks County roads, schools, bridges, tax rates, rising land values, and farms near Barney. These advertisements document the sales pitch used for the area, not an independent assessment of those claims.
