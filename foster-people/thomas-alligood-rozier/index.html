    Thomas Alligood Rozier: online research dossier

    Back to the Foster Profiles index

    Research completed: August 17, 2026
    Subject: Thomas Alligood Rozier (3 November 1855-27 October 1938), Robeson County, North Carolina; Ware and Brooks Counties, Georgia
    Evidence standard: direct records and contemporary newspapers control; later books, memorials, trees, and local tradition are identified as derivative.

    Bottom line

    Thomas A. Rozier was Thomas Alligood Rozier, born in North Carolina about November 1855. A previously documented reading of his full Georgia death certificate gives 3 November 1855 at Lumberton, but the live Ancestry page available in this pass showed only an approximate 1856 birth year. He was a son of Simpson Rozier and a mother whose name appears as Manerva in the 1860 census index but as Maria in 1870 and Maria Carlyle in his Georgia death record. He married Sallie A. Hitch in Brooks County on 19 June 1892. Their three children were Hazel, Robert C., and Catherine or Katharine Rozier; later records identify them as Hazel Stanhope, Robert C. Rozier, and Catherine Lanneau.

    Rozier was in Glenmore, Ware County, by 1886 and is the strong-probable Rozier partner in the Ware County naval-stores firm McNeil & Rozier by 1888. The grade stops short of verified because the decisive 1888 article gives neither partner's initials. From 1890 forward, the identification is direct. He signed Foster's post-office application, worked with McNeil in the Hahira-Folsom Bridge turpentine district, remained in the Foster concern after the Conoly brothers bought an interest, and signed the papers moving the post office from Foster to Barney in 1897.

    His later record is broader than the turpentine story. He farmed, bred Poland China hogs, served two documented terms as Barney postmaster, offered the Edgewood tract for development, promoted Brooks County land nationally, helped organize a proposed regional railroad, served on the county board of education, and held church office. He died at Barney on 27 October 1938 and was buried in the Morven area.

    The major Foster title question remains open. A derivative land index says Jacob Henry Croft conveyed 325 acres on the north side of Land Lot 227 to McNeil & Rozier on 9 October 1890. No deed book, page, recording date, image, consideration, or legal description has been found. It is a useful lead, not a verified deed citation.

    This pass did locate Thomas's original will, dated the day before his death. It proves a different landholding: his undivided one-half interest in 543 acres in Lowndes County Land Lots 139 and 50, owned by "J. T. McNeal and Company", a former firm composed of J. T. McNeal and T. A. Rozier. That direct record must not be conflated with the unverified Croft deed or used by itself to identify J. T. McNeal as James T. McNeill.

    Identity and North Carolina family

    The record chain is consistent:

    • The correct 1860 census entry is "T. A. Rozier", about age five, in North Division, Robeson County, in Simpson and Manerva Rozier's household. Ancestry collection 7667, record 41450029. The index should be checked against the original image before treating "Manerva" as a settled reading.
    • In 1870, Thomas Rozier, age fourteen, lived in Howellsville, Robeson County, with Simpson and Maria Rozier. Ancestry collection 7163, record 34465433; NARA M593, roll 1157, p. 69A.
    • No secure 1880 census entry was identified in the documented search. Same-name results in Louisiana and elsewhere were excluded.
    • The 1900 through 1930 censuses consistently place Thomas in Barney, born in North Carolina about 1855-1856.
    • Georgia death-record collection 2562, record 1031290, gives Thomas Alligood Rozier, age eighty-two, death at Barney on 27 October 1938, father Simpson Rozier, and mother Maria Carlyle.

    The death record and census chain verify the full identity and parentage. The mother's given name remains unresolved between Manerva and Maria. Public trees expand the father to John Simpson Rozier and the mother to Mariah/Maria Carlisle, but those expansions are derivative unless an original record is cited. The exact 3 November date and Lumberton wording are strong probable pending renewed inspection of the full certificate image.

    Marriage, household, and children

    The Brooks County marriage record indexes the groom as "Mr Thos A Rozie" and the bride as Sallie A. Hitch. They married on 19 June 1892; the local license was issued ten days earlier. Ancestry collection 4766, record 282614. A 2 July 1892 Valdosta Times item independently identifies Thomas A. Rozier of Rozier, Conoly & Co. as the groom and Sallie as a daughter of the late Dr. R. M. Hitch of Morven. Newspapers.com image 889759299, clipping 193407281.

    The household sequence is direct:

    Census Household and property details Source
    1900 Thomas, 45, farmer; Sallie, 32; Hazel, 5; Robert, 3; Sallie's sister Mattie Hitch. Thomas owned the farm free of mortgage. Ancestry 7602: 52872998, ED 9, sheet 3
    1910 Main Street: Thomas, 54, farmer; Sallie; Hazel H.; Robert C.; Katharine; Mattie Hitch; nephew Martin Rozier. Ancestry 7884: 3199159, ED 21, sheet 3A
    1920 Thomas and Sallie owned their farm free of mortgage, with a son indexed as "K. C. Rozier" and Mattie Hitch. The son's initials conflict with the otherwise secure Robert C. identity and should not be silently corrected without the image. Ancestry 6061: 7327106, ED 46, p. 17A
    1930 Main Street: Thomas and Sallie; Mattie Hitch; daughter Hazel Stanhope; grandsons Thomas and Fred Stanhope. Home value $1,000. Ancestry 6224: 19963401, ED 16, p. 2A

    The children are verified through the census and later records. Some expanded middle names come from later or compiled material and should remain subordinate to the direct records:

    • Hazel Hitch Rozier Stanhope, born about 1894. A 1924 announcement says Mr. and Mrs. T. A. Rozier announced her 17 December 1923 marriage to Harry Hurst Stanhope in Washington, D.C. Atlanta Journal, 13 January 1924.
    • Robert Carlyle Rozier, born about 1896. He appears as Robert C. in 1910 and was named as R. C. Rozier in Thomas's 1935 profile and Sallie's 1958 obituary.
    • Lois Catherine/Katharine Rozier Lanneau, born about 1900. She appears as Katharine in 1910 and later as Mrs. T. F. Lanneau.

    The Valdosta Daily Times reported on 24 June 1937 that Thomas and Sallie had celebrated their forty-fifth wedding anniversary at their Barney home the preceding Sunday. That agrees with the June 1892 marriage. Newspapers.com image 890575843.

    Ware County and the McNeil & Rozier firm

    Thomas's Ware County presence is now direct. The Savannah Morning News hotel register of 2 December 1886 lists "T. A. Rozier, Glenmore, Ga." Newspapers.com image 830501568. An Ancestry tax-digest index also places Thomas A. Rozier in Glenmore District 1082 during a displayed 1881-1887 residence range, although the source citation says "Georgia Tax Digests 1890". Both labels must be retained. Ancestry collection 1729, record 6711405, linked Georgia Archives image 40881_1020705384_0416-00455.

    On 31 March 1888, the Valdosta Times called McNeil & Rozier a Ware County turpentine firm. The article says McNeil and the firm's woodsman Connolly pursued and shot an unnamed Black laborer who had been recruited from South Carolina, bound for a year, and charged a debt. It is direct evidence of coercive and violent labor conditions, but it omits both partners' initials. Newspapers.com image 889632321; public reprint in the Savannah Morning News, 2 April 1888, p. 6.

    A second notice says 62 barrels of T. P. Jordan's rosin at Argyle depot were levied under an execution in favor of McNeil & Rozier. Images 889632317 and 889632329. This proves a judgment debt and levy. It does not locate a McNeil & Rozier still at Argyle or make the rosin theirs before levy.

    Newspapers then follow the surnames into the Hahira and Folsom Bridge district:

    • 16 March 1889: "McNeil of Hahira" among naval-stores operators, image 889789051.
    • 29 March 1890: McNeil and Rozier doing good turpentine business at Hahira; their hands worked the Folsom Bridge road, images 889794099 and 889794108.
    • 12 April 1890: J. T. McNeil nearly completed opening the road, image 889794327.
    • 24 February 1891: a reprint reports that John Clifton fatally shot Peter Armstrong at McNeil & Rozier's turpentine farm in Brooks County; the inquest returned involuntary manslaughter. Macon Telegraph, image 825352472.
    • 29 August 1891: McNeill and Rozier again had the Hahira-Folsom Bridge road worked, image 889798850.

    Thomas's direct 1886 Glenmore presence makes him the best-documented and strong-probable Rozier in the 1888 firm. It does not retroactively add his initials to the article. The Ware plant's exact site, partnership formation date, and transfer of labor, leases, equipment, or accounts into Brooks County remain unresolved.

    Foster, the Conoly transaction, and firm succession

    Thomas signed the proposed Foster/Rozier post-office location paper on 4 March 1890; the Post Office Department received it on 10 March. One proposed name was "Rozier", tied to the family, while Foster became the approved name. The postmaster appointment register records Thomas at Foster on 4 April 1890. Ancestry collection 1932, record 1630513; NARA M841, roll 22.

    The 14 November 1891 Valdosta Times correction says McNeil & Rozier sold brothers A. J. and H. McLean Conley/Conoly a collective one-half interest in the Foster naval-stores business and that McNeill retained one-fourth. Image 889756841; the initial report is image 889756744. This was a sale of a business interest, not a land deed. It does not divide the brothers' half, identify the owner of the remaining quarter, or state Thomas's retained share.

    The operating name then appears as Rozier, Conoly & Co. or Rozier, Conoly Bros. & Co. in 1892-1893. The marriage item directly identifies Thomas with that concern. Foster columns describe continuing turpentine production and hauling to Hahira. Clipping IDs 193407281, 193407600, 193407422, and 193407548; related images include 889759299, 889761322, and 889763942.

    A 1900 Savannah Morning News report lists T. A. Rozier among Savannah Naval Stores Company stockholders at its annual meeting. The initials and career fit the Barney man, but the item gives no residence, so the identification is strong probable. Image 852595717.

    No partnership agreement, dissolution instrument, or final accounting was located. Thomas's exact entry and exit dates and the disposition of the last quarter interest remain unresolved.

    His 26 October 1938 will supplies direct evidence of another or surviving partnership interest. It calls "J. T. McNeal and Company" a former firm composed of J. T. McNeal and T. A. Rozier and says the firm then owned 543 acres in parts of Land Lots 139 and 50, 12th District, Lowndes County. Thomas devised his undivided half to Sallie. The record uses "McNeal", not "McNeil" or "McNeill", and describes different land from the Foster tract. It is verified as written, but the partner's full identity and the legal connection, if any, to the 1888-1891 McNeil & Rozier concern remain unresolved.

    Foster-to-Barney postal continuity

    The postal chain is one of the best-documented parts of the dossier:

    • Foster: appointed 4 April 1890, Ancestry 1932:1630513.
    • Thomas signed the 12 February 1897 application moving the office from Foster in Land Lot 227 to Barney in Land Lot 320, three miles northwest, on the South Georgia Railroad.
    • Barney: appointed 26 July 1897, Ancestry 1932:1630564.
    • He signed a Topographer's Office questionnaire on 2 November 1897, placing the office about 175 yards west of the railroad.
    • Tobe G. Robinson was appointed "vice Thomas A. Rozier, resigned" on 24 October 1903. Atlanta Journal, 26 October 1903, image 969531602.
    • Thomas was reappointed 20 February 1906, Ancestry 1932:1630569.
    • Paris Culpepper replaced him on 17 February 1916; Atlanta Semi-Weekly Journal, 22 February 1916, image 887733697.

    Thomas served two documented Barney terms, 1897-1903 and 1906-1916. Describing his service as uninterrupted would be wrong.

    Farming, livestock, land promotion, and Edgewood

    Every census from 1900 through 1930 calls Thomas a farmer or places him on his own farm. His advertisements show a specialized livestock interest. In 1904 he sought purebred Poland China hogs. In November 1909 he offered six full-blood pigs from registered stock for $8 each or $15 per pair. Images 889652157, 889664910, and 889664935.

    In August 1914, he placed a national homeseeker advertisement in the Des Moines Register: "BARNEY, BROOKS COUNTY, GA.-COME to Brooks county ... where lands are reasonable cheap. The good people here will give you a hearty welcome. T. A. Rozier." Images 129031594 and 129032073. This verifies his role as a Barney contact and land promoter, not ownership of every tract advertised.

    The January 1911 Edgewood reports say Thomas offered about sixteen acres between his "commodious residence", the Methodist and Baptist churches, and the public school for a Cambridge Realty auction. Valdosta Daily Times, 19 January 1911, image 889696900; weekly reprint 889668494. Advertisements variously proposed fifty or sixty lots. No result article, buyer list, prices, deeds, or plat was found. The tract and planned auction are verified; the number of completed sales is unresolved.

    Local tradition says Thomas built Barney's first house. His 1897 postal role and the documented prominent residence fit the tradition, but no located deed, tax-improvement record, construction notice, or contemporary article calls it the first house. James has identified its approximate location as 31.006130, -83.519237. That is a user-supplied geographic control, not construction-date proof.

    The Croft deed and Land Lot 227

    The 1897 federal postal diagram directly places the existing Foster office in Land Lot 227, 12th District, and the proposed Barney office in Land Lot 320. It does not identify the owner of the Foster land or locate the still and commissary within the lot.

    A derivative land-lot index says Jacob Henry Croft conveyed 325 acres on the north side of Lot 227 to McNeil & Rozier on 9 October 1890. The original instrument has not been found. There is no verified deed book, page, recording date, consideration, spouse release, metes and bounds, or image.

    Thomas's 1890 Morven District tax row lists no land. Ancestry collection 1729, record 716589, image 32306_1220705227_0017-00169. That weighs against calling him the individually assessed owner at that moment, but it does not exclude firm title, a lease, a separate assessment, or an acquisition later in the year.

    The deed claim is therefore a strong-probable lead only. It must not be cited as an established deed reference until the Brooks County index and deed book are inspected.

    Town, church, school, and railroad activity

    The public record shows a range of local roles:

    • The Atlanta Journal listed Thomas A. Rozier of Barney as a Brooks County delegate in an agricultural listing on 19 April 1903, image 969502063.
    • The Valdosta Times reported on 5 December 1903 that he was among Barney's nominated councilmen. Image 889679697. Nomination is verified; election and term are not.
    • The 9 November 1935 Macon Telegraph birthday profile says he served on the board of education and was secretary-treasurer of the Baptist church. Image 826362749.
    • Huxford says he was a Methodist Church trustee in 1899 and senior warden of Barney Masonic Lodge No. 609 in 1911. Those are strong secondary claims until supported by church or lodge records.

    Thomas also helped organize the Georgia, Alabama & Western Railroad Company. Late-1911 notices name him among petitioners or promoters for a proposed 150-mile line through Hahira, Barney, Pavo, Meigs, Colquitt, and Blakely. Images 889588868, 889695710, and 969949004. The state granted the charter in January 1912; images 34095609 and 970010050. By June, reports called him a director and said surveys and cost estimates had been ordered. Image 859960351. Corporate organization is verified; construction of the proposed full line is not.

    Death, burial, and probate

    The Georgia death record establishes death at Barney on 27 October 1938. Find a Grave memorial 111534073, reopened on 17 August 2026, gives birth 3 November 1855, death 27 October 1938, and burial at Camp Ground Cemetery Mount Zion Church near Morven. The memorial links Sallie and the three children. These memorial details are derivative unless supported by a photographed marker or another direct record; the marker itself was not independently transcribed in this pass.

    No clean obituary or funeral notice was found after exact and variant searches across 1935-1940. The Quitman Free Press remains an important coverage gap for October-November 1938.

    Ancestry collection 8635, record 1205180, is Thomas's will, not a stray mention. The index points to Brooks County Record of Wills, 1918-1964, p. 230. The original was inspected at media image 005782588_00313.

    The will is headed "Last Will and Testament of Thomas A. Rozier" and dated 26 October 1938, one day before his death. It:

    1. left burial details to Sallie;
    2. directed payment of debts;
    3. devised to Sallie his undivided one-half interest in 543 acres owned by "J. T. McNeal and Company", described as a former firm composed of J. T. McNeal and T. A. Rozier, in parts of Land Lots 139 and 50, 12th District, Lowndes County;
    4. gave Sallie all other real and personal property; and
    5. appointed Sallie executrix without inventory, appraisal, bond, or returns.

    The witnesses were T. F. Lanneau, P. Culpepper, and S. M. Blease. These terms are verified from the original image. The will does not identify the full name of J. T. McNeal or explain whether this former firm was legally continuous with the earlier McNeil & Rozier partnership.

    Namesake and record exclusions

    • Thomas A. Rozier of Ste. Genevieve, Missouri, was a different man.
    • T. A. Rozier of Bonne Terre, Missouri, was unrelated.
    • P. J. Strozier of Barney was a distinct local person; "Strozier" must not be normalized to Rozier.
    • A Louisiana-born Thomas Rozier in the 1880 census was not the subject.
    • The 1860 Pennsylvania "Thomas Roser", Ancestry 7667:4920131, was a wrong result and was excluded. The correct target is 7667:41450029.
    • "Rosier", "Rozie", and similar forms may be source spellings or OCR variants. They were accepted only when place, relatives, or record continuity established the match.

    Search coverage and limits

    Newspapers.com searches covered 1870-1940 using Rozier's full name, initials, surname variants, associated places, relatives, occupations, and public offices.

    Meaningful gaps:

    • Brooks County deed and plat indexes were not available as a fully searchable online image set. The Croft deed and Edgewood deeds remain uninspected.
    • The original will was inspected, but the loose probate packet, probate order, land deed, and later estate conveyances remain uninspected.
    • No dedicated Barney city directory was found.
    • The Quitman Free Press issue runs needed for February 1900 and October-November 1938 were not located online.
    • No clean Thomas obituary was found in the documented search.
    • The exact Ware still site, partnership agreements, final Foster shares, and partnership dissolution remain unresolved.

    Priority record requests

    1. Brooks County grantor and grantee indexes and deed books, 1888-1905: Croft/Craft, McNeil/McNeill/McNiel, Rozier/Rosier, Conley/Conoly/Connolly, and firm forms. Retrieve the alleged 9 October 1890 instrument and trace it forward.
    2. Brooks deed and plat books, January-March 1911: Thomas and Sallie Rozier, Edgewood, and Cambridge Realty; obtain the plat, buyers, lot numbers, prices, and covenants.
    3. Brooks Court of Ordinary: obtain the loose probate packet, probate order, and any estate or year's-support papers corresponding to will-book p. 230. The will waives inventory, appraisal, bond, and returns, so some usual documents may not exist.
    4. Lowndes County grantor/grantee, deed, mortgage, and tax records: trace "J. T. McNeal and Company", J. T. McNeal/McNeil/McNeill, and T. A. Rozier in Land Lots 139 and 50, 12th District, and identify the acquisition and later conveyance of the 543 acres.
    5. Quitman Free Press: manually browse February 1900 for the reported store/post-office building item and October-November 1938 for death coverage.
    6. Georgia death certificate image: preserve the state certificate number and full informant, cause, burial, and undertaker fields.
    7. Georgia, Alabama & Western corporate file: charter packet, subscriptions, surveys, and any construction or abandonment record.
