    T. P. Jordan Online Dossier

    Back to the Foster Profiles index

    Prepared: August 18, 2026
    Scope: The 1888 Clinch County execution in favor of McNeil & Rozier and the unidentified owner of 62 barrels of rosin at Argyle.

    Bottom line

    T. P. Jordan is documented in one legal event repeated as a sheriff's advertisement. On March 22, 1888, Clinch County sheriff B. E. Mattox levied on 62 round barrels of rosin belonging to T. P. Jordan. The barrels were at the Argyle depot on the Savannah, Florida & Western Railway. The levy was made under an execution from Clinch Superior Court in favor of McNeil & Rozier against Jordan, with sale advertised for the first Tuesday in May. The notice appeared in the Valdosta Times on March 31 and was repeated weekly through April 28. [1]-[5]

    This proves that Jordan owned the rosin when it was levied and that McNeil & Rozier were judgment creditors. It does not show that the firm owned the barrels, operated a still at Argyle, employed Jordan, or produced the rosin. The depot was the storage and levy site; it is not a proved plant site.

    Jordan's full name, residence, occupation, age, family, and later life remain unresolved. No located census, tax, probate, directory, marriage, death, cemetery, or additional newspaper record can be linked to the debtor without relying only on the initials and common surname.

    The notice

    The March 31 text, allowing for OCR punctuation, states that the sheriff would sell 62 round barrels of rosin at the Clinch County courthouse. The rosin was levied on as T. P. Jordan's property to satisfy an execution from the county superior court in favor of McNeil & Rozier. It was then at the depot at Argyle on the S. F. & W. Railway. The notice was dated March 22 and signed B. E. Mattox, sheriff. [1]

    The April 7, 14, 21, and 28 publications repeat the same legal notice. They are corroborating impressions of one advertisement, not four additional lawsuits or seizures. [2]-[5]

    What the evidence supports

    • Verified: T. P. Jordan owned 62 round barrels of rosin subject to levy in March 1888. [1]
    • Verified: McNeil & Rozier held the execution against Jordan. [1]
    • Verified: the rosin was at the Argyle railroad depot when advertised. [1]
    • Strong probable: Jordan participated in the regional naval-stores trade, because he owned a commercial quantity of rosin. The notice does not state whether he was a producer, merchant, factor, carrier, or investor.
    • Unresolved: the debt's basis, judgment amount, case number, original complaint, sale result, and whether Jordan had any later relationship with the firm.
    • Contradicted: the claim that this notice proves McNeil & Rozier owned an Argyle still or the 62 barrels. The notice expressly calls the rosin Jordan's property.

    Identity controls and rejected candidates

    The initials were not expanded. A later newspaper reference to a T. P. Jordan described as a merchant of Saussy, Georgia, lacks a Clinch County, Argyle, or McNeil & Rozier bridge and was excluded. Later Florida and Lake City references involving Mr. or Mrs. T. P. Jordan were also excluded. Initials and surname alone are inadequate when the record supplies no age, residence, spouse, occupation, or kin.

    Foster significance

    The levy helps map McNeil & Rozier's pre-Foster business reach. It places a judgment debtor's rosin at an Argyle shipping point in early 1888, several years before the surviving Foster community reports. It does not establish that the Ware County operation moved from Argyle to Foster, that Jordan worked at Foster, or that any of the seized product came from the later Lot 227 tract. [6]

    Next records to obtain

    1. Clinch Superior Court execution and civil dockets for the term preceding March 22, 1888, under McNeil & Rozier v. T. P. Jordan.
    2. The sheriff's levy book, sale book, and deed or return for May 1888 to determine whether the barrels sold and for how much.
    3. Clinch tax digests and Argyle-area business records for 1887-1889.
    4. Savannah, Florida & Western Railway freight, warehouse, or agent records for Argyle, if surviving.
    5. Original court papers for the debt's consideration, Jordan's residence, and any garnishee or claimant.

    Completion statement

    The available online newspaper corpus, public genealogy indexes, and general web results were searched with exact and variant forms. Only the sheriff's notice and its repeats could be tied safely to this T. P. Jordan. The dossier therefore preserves a narrow verified conclusion and leaves the debtor's identity open rather than merging a namesake.
