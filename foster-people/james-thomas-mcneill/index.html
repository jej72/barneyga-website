    James Thomas McNeill: online dossier

    Back to the Foster Profiles index

    Research completed August 17, 2026; updated August 22, 2026

    Identification

    This dossier concerns James Thomas McNeill, born in Cumberland County, North Carolina, about April 1858. He was the Hahira and Foster-area naval-stores operator documented in south Georgia during the 1880s and 1890s and the later Wewahitchka and Indian Pass operator in Florida.

    The identity chain is verified from the 1880 census, his 1890 marriage record, the 1892 Hahira profile, the 1900-1940 household sequence, and contemporary Georgia and Florida newspapers. Variant spellings and bad indexes include McNeal, McNeil, McNeill, J. T., J. S., J. Q., and James D. Mcmill. Those variants are joined here only where the household, relatives, age, birthplace, place, and occupation establish continuity.

    Conclusions at a glance

    • Verified: His parents were recorded as J. H. McNeill and Effie McIntyre on his 1890 marriage record. No inspected original expands J. H. to John Hector.
    • Strong probable: The ten-year-old James with Effey McNeill in Black River Township in 1870 was the later James T. McNeill. His 1860 household remains unresolved.
    • Verified: He married Virginia Rhodes on January 21, 1890. She died in Franklin County, Florida, in 1905. Her middle initial was B.; the expansion Bostick is unverified.
    • Verified: His second wife was Gypsie A. Atkins, the woman indexed as Epsie, Gypsy, Gypsie, and Cyksis from 1910 through 1950. Their exact marriage date and place remain unresolved.
    • Verified: Herman and Lillian were explicitly recorded as adopted children in 1900. Ruth was recorded as a daughter, but no inspected record states whether she was biological or adopted. James Thomas McNeill Jr. is verified through the 1940 census and his draft card.
    • Verified: McNeill was a large naval-stores operator at Hahira by 1892, moved to Wewahitchka by January 1900, and remained in the Florida turpentine business for decades.
    • Most likely: The "J. T. Neill" who sold McLean Conoly a fast trotting horse at Foster in March 1892 was James T. McNeill. The initials, place, date, and association fit, but the notice does not print the full McNeill surname.
    • Verified: The "J. T. McNeal" named as Thomas A. Rozier's former partner in the 1938 will was James T. McNeill. An original 1890 Lowndes tax page places "McNeil James T" beside "McNeil and Co"; the company held 477 acres in the same Land Lots 50 and 139 later named in the will. The deed chain and the increase to 543 acres remain unresolved.
    • Verified: Contemporary newspapers used "McNeal" for James in the Indian Pass and Port St. Joe family context. They also establish a Wewahitchka Gin Company presidency in 1904, pilotage service by 1905, the known 1913 appointment, and a serious illness in February 1940.
    • Verified: Contemporary reports attributed 12,000 fenced Indian Pass acres to him in 1926 and said that he and associates controlled and operated on 11,000 Gulf-front acres in 1932. The statements describe different years and legal interests and should not be forced into one acreage total.
    • Verified: He died at his Indian Pass home on August 2, 1941, after an illness of more than two years, and was buried at Jehu Cemetery. The photographed marker's 1858-1951 ending year is wrong.
    • Unproved derivative claims: No contemporary or official source found calls him a founder of Indian Pass, names his business the Indian Pass Trading Post, or proves that it became the Indian Pass Raw Bar.

    Parents and early life

    The 1870 census places James McNeill, age ten, and Cathrine McNeill, age seven, with Effey McNeill in Black River Township, Cumberland County. The matching township, mother, surname, and age make this James a strong-probable match. Ancestry record 22831981, image 4277209_00018.

    In 1880 Jas. T. McNeill, 22, lived in the Black River household of M. McIntyre and Effie McIntyre. The census called him Effie's stepson, said he was attending school, and gave his occupation as keeping a grist mill. Ancestry record 19418011, image 4243272-00094; NARA T9, roll 960, page 357C, ED 57.

    His January 21, 1890 Cumberland County marriage record names him as the son of J. H. McNeill and Effie McIntyre. It names his bride, Virginia Rhodes, as the daughter of Eley J. and Elizabeth Rhodes. Ancestry record 4562708, image 42091_329780-00648.

    An Ancestry hint for two-year-old Jas. D. McNeill in 1860 belongs to a different James Duncan McNeill. It is rejected. The names John Hector McNeill and Effie McKay appear in derivative cemetery or tree material but were not established by an inspected original record.

    Georgia naval stores, roads, and Foster

    Ware County and the partnership

    A March 31, 1888 Valdosta Times report identifies McNeil & Rozier as a Ware County turpentine firm. It describes McNeil and the firm's woodsman, Connolly, pursuing a Black worker who had left the operation. McNeil shot the man in the back from about ninety yards; the report says the firm's workers had been brought from South Carolina and bound to one-year labor terms. Newspapers.com image 889632321; reprints include images 852572266 and 34091266.

    The article does not give McNeil's initials. James T. McNeill's identity as that partner is therefore strong probable, supported by the business and place sequence that follows, not directly proved by the 1888 article alone.

    A March-April 1888 Clinch County sheriff's notice made McNeil & Rozier judgment creditors of T. P. Jordan and levied 62 barrels of Jordan's rosin at Argyle. Images 889632317 and 889632329. This proves a debt and regional business reach. It does not place the firm's still at Argyle or make the barrels the firm's property before levy.

    Hahira and McNeill Road

    By March 1890 newspapers placed McNeil and Rozier in the Hahira/Folsom Bridge turpentine and road network. On April 12, 1890, the Valdosta Times said J. T. McNeil had nearly completed a new road from Hahira to Folsom Bridge. Image 889794327. Later notices called it McNeill Road; Lowndes County adopted the Hahira-Folsom Bridge route as a public road in February 1897, and a circa-1910 county map labeled the corridor "McNEIL RD."

    The road construction and its identification with James are verified. The record does not prove that he owned the right-of-way or that every part of the period road followed the modern alignment.

    The Folsom Bridge history places McNeill's Road within the longer history of the crossing and the Foster neighborhood.

    Foster interest and J. T. McNeill & Co.

    The November 14, 1891 correction to a Foster business report states that McNeil & Rozier sold A. J. and H. McLean Conley/Conoly a collective one-half interest in the Foster naval-stores business and that McNeill retained one-fourth. Valdosta Times, image 889756841; initial report image 889756744.

    This was a business-interest transaction, not a proved land conveyance. It does not allocate the brothers' individual shares, identify the owner of the final quarter, or show when McNeill disposed of his remaining interest.

    The J. T. Neill horse notice

    A Foster column dated March 16, 1892, says McLean Conoly bought "J. T. Neill's fast trotting horse." The source plainly prints Neill. In context, however, the seller was most likely James T. McNeill. The initials match, the transaction took place among his Foster associates only months after newspapers named him in the Foster business, and contemporary records already varied his surname among McNeil, McNeill, and McNeal. No second South Georgia man named J. T. Neill has been found in the completed search. Valdosta Times, March 19, 1892, page 1, image 889758320.

    No duplicate notice has been found that expands the seller's name. The horse sale is therefore carried here as a likely McNeill naming variant, with the printed form preserved, rather than as a separate person's dossier.

    An August 13, 1892 profile names James T. McNeill and "J. T. McNeill & Co." at a principal plant one mile east of Hahira. The paper valued the operation at about $30,000, suggested that it represented only half his naval-stores interests, and described him as a farmer, cattle breeder, road builder, and civic participant. Image 889759805. The profile's reference to A. Conley at McNeill's home proves association, not the legal terms of a partnership.

    The follow-up adds a firmer company trail. An 1889 railroad industry report lists "McNeal" at Hahira with annual capacity of 400 barrels of turpentine and 1,600 barrels of rosin. The Hahira, date, and industry make James the strong-probable operator, although the item gives no initials. Images 825344612 and 26867424. In 1893 a team driver worked for "J. T. McNeill & Co."; in 1894 Henry B. Culbreth worked for "J. McNeil & Co." in Hahira turpentine and later died at J. T. McNeil's residence. Images 889766060, 889831644, and 889831679.

    In 1904 "J. T. McNeill & Co. v. Thomas Passmore" reached Lowndes Superior Court. The company asserted a right under an 1889 lease to cut timber on 275 acres. A May 21 report initially said the plaintiff won; a May 24 correction says the defendant won. Images 889683754 and 889683819. This verifies a company-styled 1889 timber claim and litigation fifteen years later. The reports do not name Rozier, the lessor, Land Lots 50 or 139, or recording data, so they strengthen but do not finish the legal-entity chain.

    A separate 1895 execution in favor of J. T. McNeil involved a two-acre Hahira parcel in Land Lot 99. Images 889834575 and 889834711. It shows creditor activity but has no demonstrated connection to the target tract.

    The Lowndes tract and the McNeal spelling

    The original 1890 Lowndes County tax digest supplies the missing land-level connection. In Club House District 658, adjacent entries read "McNeil James T" and "McNeil and Co". The company row reports 477 acres in Land Lots 50 and 139, 12th District, valued at $1,400. Ancestry collection 1729, record 525284, original image 32306_1220706333_0005-00099.

    Thomas A. Rozier's original will, dated October 26, 1938, calls "J. T. McNeal and Company" a former firm composed of J. T. McNeal and T. A. Rozier. It gives Sallie Rozier Thomas's undivided half interest in 543 acres in parts of those same two lots and district. Brooks County Record of Wills, 1918-1964, p. 230; Ancestry collection 8635, record 1205180, image 005782588_00313.

    Together, the adjacent personal and company tax rows, both exact land-lot numbers, the district, the known Rozier partnership, and independent newspaper use of "McNeal" verify that the will's J. T. McNeal was James T. McNeill. The tax digest and will do not prove that the legal entity was unchanged for forty-eight years. That continuity is strong probable until the deeds or partnership papers are found.

    The acreage rose from 477 in 1890 to 543 in 1938, a difference of 66 acres. No checked source explains whether the firm acquired land, corrected a survey or assessment, or changed the way acreage was counted. The difference is a title-history question, not an identity conflict.

    A derivative land-lot index says Jacob Henry Croft conveyed 325 acres on the north side of Brooks County Land Lot 227, District 12, to McNeil & Rozier on October 9, 1890. The deed book, page, consideration, and original image have not been verified. The land claim remains strong probable pending the instrument.

    McNeill remained associated with Hahira through the late 1890s. A January 16, 1900 notice called him formerly of Hahira and said he had moved to Wewahitchka, Florida. Images 889672128 and 889672134.

    Newspaper searches under "McNeal" add several later details. The Florida Senate confirmed J. T. McNeal as a St. Joe pilotage commissioner in 1913. That notice alone names no relative or occupation, but the St. Joe location and later family-linked reports identify him as James. Florida Times-Union, May 30, 1913, image 1257958763. A 1927 item placed "J. T. McNeal and family" at Indian Pass, and a 1937 Hahira item named Mr. and Mrs. J. T. McNeal and their son James of Port St. Joe. Images 353041013 and 890575945. These family-and-residence matches directly verify "McNeal" as a contemporary spelling for James.

    Wives and children

    Virginia Rhodes McNeill

    The 1900 census places James and Virginia B. McNeill at Wewahitchka. James was 42, born in April 1858, and a naval-stores manufacturer. Virginia was born in May 1861; the household said they had married in 1890. Ancestry household record 33980819.

    Virginia died in Franklin County in 1905. Florida death-index record 4817004, original index image VRDUSAFL1877_0106-0068. Her photographed Jehu Cemetery marker reads Virginia B. Rhodes, wife of J. T. McNeill, born in Cumberland County May 15, 1861, died August 7, 1905. Find a Grave memorial 8716041.

    Find a Grave expands her middle initial to Bostick. Neither the marriage record, census, death index, nor marker spells out Bostick, so that expansion is not accepted as proved.

    Gypsie A. Atkins McNeill

    The 1900 census places Gypsie Atkins, born July 1881, as a daughter of Joel P. and Emily V. Atkins at Wewahitchka. Ancestry record 33980835, image 4120037_00444. Joel Atkins's probate record directly names Gypsie McNeill as his child. Ancestry collection 8993, record 659476, image 005885796_00228.

    The household chain verifies that Epsie McNeill in 1910, Gypsie/Gypsy in 1920 and 1930, Cyksis in the bad 1940 index, Gypsy A. in 1945, and Gypsie A. in 1950 were the same woman. Her Social Security claim gives July 25, 1881. Record 3497713.

    The 1910 census says James and Gypsie had been married one year, placing the marriage about 1908-1909. No original license or notice was found. Her marker reads July 25, 1881-February 12, 1966. Find a Grave memorial 8716220. Her Port St. Joe Star obituary says she died February 12 at 3:45 p.m. in a Palatka nursing home, confirming the Florida death index's Putnam County entry. Indian Pass on the memorial is residence or community association, not her death place. Port St. Joe Star, February 17, 1966, issue.

    The three-image probate result indexed under Gypsie is not her estate. It is Joel P. Atkins's will. It names wife Emily and children, gives each child five dollars, and contains no Indian Pass property or McNeill estate terms.

    Children

    The 1900 census explicitly calls Herman McNeill, five, and Lillian G. McNeill, three, James and Virginia's adopted son and adopted daughter. Records 33980821 and 33980822, image 4120037_00443. Later censuses shorten the relationships to son and daughter; that does not undo the direct 1900 adoption wording. James's 1941 obituary again calls both adopted. It places Herman G. McNeill in Brooklyn and identifies Lillian as Mrs. H. M. Barfield of Hahira. Their birth parents remain unresolved.

    Ruth McNeill appears as James and Gypsie's daughter in 1920 and 1930. Records 14621759 and 102905691. May 1936 marriage coverage says Mr. and Mrs. James Thomas McNeill announced her marriage to William Adams Walker and that James gave her away. James's 1941 obituary and Gypsie's 1966 obituary call Ruth their daughter while separately labeling Lillian and Herman adopted or foster children. This verifies Ruth's family identity and strongly supports biological-daughter status, although no birth record was found. Tallahassee Democrat, May 21, 1936, image 244759166; Florida Times-Union, May 13, 1936, image 1258719625; Port St. Joe Star issues.

    James Thomas McNeill Jr. is the five-year-old boy badly indexed in 1930 and Jimmy in 1940. The 1940 census calls him James and Gypsie's son. His draft card gives birth October 1, 1924, at Indian Pass and names J. T. McNeill as next of kin. Ancestry record 12759719, image 44005_12_00024-01932.

    The Barfield identities are now resolved. Mrs. H. M. Barfield was adopted daughter Lillian McNeill. Her husband's 1946 obituary calls her the former Lillian McNeal of Pensacola, and a 1948 engagement notice uses Lillian McNeill of Port St. Joe. Images 889668299, 890592039, and 826525154.

    Mrs. C. L. Barfield was Blanche Rhodes, not another McNeill daughter. The 1920 census calls Blanche Rhodes, 22, James's niece14621763,40660_307913-00143, and21424729. Gypsie's 1966 obituary calls Blanche one of three nieces she reared. The February 1940 column compressed two household relationships when it called both Barfield women daughters. The June 1940 "aunts" wording reflects a family role, not a strict genealogical description for Blanche. Images890419249and890397289`.

    Florida household and business timeline

    • 1900: James, Virginia, Herman, Lillian, and Virginia's brother George W. Rhodes lived at Wewahitchka. James was a naval-stores manufacturer and owned the home free of mortgage. Record 33980819.
    • 1901: The Valdosta Times called J. T. McNeill a large naval-stores manufacturer of Wewahitchka. Image 889668885.
    • 1904: The Wewahitchka Gin Company incorporated for $2,500 with J. T. McNeil as president. Its plans included gins and possible sawmill, rice-huller, grist-mill, and syrup equipment. Miami News, image 297547451.
    • 1905: Governor Broward appointed W. R. Shaw and J. T. McNeill of Wewahitchka as Calhoun County pilot commissioners, moving James's documented pilotage service back eight years from the 1913 confirmation. Weekly True Democrat, September 8, 1905, image 81281249.
    • 1910: At Dalkeith, James worked on his own account in naval stores. Wife Epsie/Gypsie, Herman, Lillian, and Rhodes relatives were present. The index's "Naval / Flour" occupation reading is anomalous and should not be normalized without the image. Record 150738171 for Gypsie; prior household record 2887852.
    • 1920: A bad index calls him James D. Mcmill, but the age, birthplace, wife, children, relatives, and turpentine-plant manager occupation identify James T. McNeill. Household record 14621757.
    • 1925: The McNeills' home at the Lagoon burned. The report gives no cause, injuries, insurance amount, or rebuilding details. Image 352639356.
    • 1926: A Pensacola News Journal feature called McNeill a 25-year Gulf County pioneer, attributed 12,000 fenced acres at Indian Pass to him, and described a Shetland pony enterprise of 50-75 animals. Image 352745970.
    • 1930: James, 71, was a self-employed naval-stores proprietor at Port St. Joe. He owned a $10,000 home and had a radio. Gypsie, Ruth, and the boy later verified as James Jr. were present. Household record 102905673.
    • 1932: A Tallahassee Democrat feature said McNeill first inspected the country west of Apalachicola Bay in January 1900, bought there, and made Indian Pass his home. It called him a large-scale turpentine operator and said he and associates controlled and conducted operations on 11,000 Gulf-front acres under a conservation and reforestation policy. Image 244777993.
    • 1932: On the facing page, Mrs. J. T. McNeill advertised meals, sandwiches, drinks, lodging, Texaco products, and day-and-night service at Indian Pass. Image 244778057. This directly proves a hospitality and service operation, but not the later Trading Post and Raw Bar succession story.
    • 1934: J. T. McNeill of Indian Pass was reelected a director of the West Florida branch of the Children's Home Society of Florida. Image 352638507.
    • 1937: A deed abstract records James and Gypsie McNeill conveying part of Lot 2, Section 12, Township 2 South, Range 26 East, to Maplewood Investment Company in Duval County. This verifies a transaction but not its full terms; the original deed was not inspected. Florida Times-Union, September 1, 1937, image 1258605081.
    • 1940: The man indexed as J. S./J. Q. McNeill in Gulf County was James. He was 82, born in North Carolina, married to Gypsie, and father of Jimmy, 15. He owned a $3,000 home on "McNeils Off Highway" and worked on his own account as an overseer. Record 132600936, image m-t0627-00588-00437, sheet 7A.
    • 1941: James died at his Indian Pass home on August 2 and was buried the next day at Jehu Cemetery. His obituary described him as owning several thousand acres of timberland near Indian Pass. Port St. Joe Star, August 8, 1941, issue.

    The 1926 and 1932 acreage reports must remain separate. The first uses ownership language and says 12,000 acres under fence. The second says 11,000 acres controlled and operated with associates. Neither is a deed total or proof of sole fee ownership.

    The 1906 James M. McNeill probate connection

    A Muscogee County probate citation for James M. McNeill names James T. McNeill and Ola, Clifford, Effie, and Blanche Rhodes of Wewahitchka. The citation collectively calls the listed recipients heirs at law, children of deceased heirs, or parties interested under the will. It does not assign James T. to a particular category or state his kinship.

    The citation therefore verifies a legal-interest connection but not that James M. was James T.'s father, uncle, or other specific relative. Columbus Ledger, April 5-May 3, 1906, images 854810367, 854810487, 854810998, and 854789964. The original will, probate petition, service returns, and settlement are still needed.

    Death

    James died at his Indian Pass home on Saturday, August 2, 1941, age 83, after an illness lasting more than two years. Services were held at the home Sunday, followed by burial at Jehu Cemetery near Wewahitchka. His obituary gives birth April 26, 1858, in Cumberland County and names widow Gypsie, son James, daughter Mrs. W. A. Walker, adopted daughter Mrs. H. M. Barfield, and adopted son Herman G. McNeill. Port St. Joe Star, August 8, 1941, pp. 1 and 3, issue.

    The Florida death index independently records 1941 in Gulf County. Ancestry record 133861. An August 7 Hahira column says the Barfield families were called to Pensacola because of his death and attended his Sunday funeral, independently confirming the week and the "McNeal" spelling. Valdosta Daily Times, image 890424030.

    The photographed marker's 1858-1951 has the right month and day in derivative memorial data but the wrong year. The obituary, state index, 1945 household, Gypsie's 1950 widow status, and a 1947 notice calling James Sr. "late" make 1951 contradicted rather than unresolved. Find a Grave memorial 8716147; Florida Times-Union image 1258886878. The original death certificate remains useful for cause, informant, and certificate details, but it is no longer needed to settle the year or date.

    Land, title, and estate limits

    No online deed chain was found for the Indian Pass acreage. Contemporary newspaper ownership language establishes what was reported, not fee-simple title, exact parcel boundaries, or whether holdings included leases and timber rights.

    No James T. McNeill Gulf County will, probate file, inventory, administrator notice, homestead proceeding, or estate deed was found. Gulf and Calhoun County official-record portals presented a Cloudflare human-verification screen or required registration, so those indexes were not fully searched. No negative inference is made from that access failure.

    The strongest remaining title targets are:

    1. Lowndes County deeds, tax digests, partnership papers, and estate conveyances for McNeal/McNeil/McNeill, Rozier, and Land Lots 50 and 139, 12th District. These should explain the change from 477 acres in 1890 to 543 in 1938.
    2. The alleged October 9, 1890 Croft-to-McNeil & Rozier Brooks County deed and every forward conveyance or firm instrument.
    3. Calhoun County deeds and mortgages under McNeil/McNeill, James T., J. T., and Gypsie from 1899 until Gulf County's creation.
    4. Gulf County deeds, mortgages, tax records, business licenses, estate records, and satisfactions from county creation through 1960.
    5. James M. McNeill's complete Muscogee County probate file.

    Claims not established

    • John Hector McNeill as father: unverified expansion of the direct record's J. H.
    • Effie McKay as mother: derivative and not established by the records inspected.
    • Virginia's middle name Bostick: unverified expansion of B.
    • Herman's and Lillian's birth parents: unresolved; later namesakes were not merged.
    • Exact James-Gypsie marriage: unresolved beyond the census estimate of about 1908-1909.
    • McNeill as the unnamed 1888 Ware partner: strong probable, not directly named by initials in that article.
    • McNeill & Rozier ownership of Argyle rosin or an Argyle still: contradicted or unsupported by the sheriff's notice.
    • Foster business interest as a land conveyance: not established.
    • Exact disposal of McNeill's retained one-quarter Foster interest: unresolved.
    • The printed "J. T. Neill" horse seller as James T. McNeill: most likely, but not independently proved by a second notice or full-name record of the sale.
    • The legal entity remaining unchanged from 1890 "McNeil and Co" to the firm described in 1938: strong probable, not proved by a formation, amendment, dissolution, or deed instrument.
    • Cause of the 477-to-543-acre change in Lowndes Lots 50 and 139: unresolved.
    • 12,000 acres in 1926 and 11,000 acres in 1932 as the same parcel total: not established.
    • Founder of Indian Pass, Indian Pass Trading Post name, and succession to the Indian Pass Raw Bar: derivative and unproved.
    • 1951 marker year: contradicted by the 1941 obituary, death index, funeral report, and later household evidence.

    Search coverage and limits

    The investigation began with local memory, Barney/Foster research, prior McNeill reports, road research, saved newspaper JSON, land-lot work, and evidence ledgers. Three focused lanes then covered:

    • federal censuses from 1870 through 1950 and the 1935/1945 Florida censuses;
    • North Carolina marriage records, Florida death indexes, Social Security claims, probate indexes, draft records, and cemetery pages;
    • Newspapers.com Georgia and Florida titles from 1870 through 1960, with death-focused passes for 1940-1955 and 1949-1953;
    • the Florida Digital Newspaper Library's Port St. Joe Star run, including issue-by-issue 1941 searching and targeted 1966 browsing;
    • Find a Grave memorials and marker photographs for James, Virginia, and Gypsie;
    • public web results and county clerk access points;
    • stored Brooks, Lowndes, Ware, Calhoun, Gulf, Franklin, and Muscogee leads.
