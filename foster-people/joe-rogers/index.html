    Joe Rogers of the Foster neighborhood

    Back to the Foster Profiles index

    Research completed: August 18, 2026

    Bottom line

    The "Mr. Joe Rogers" whose family lived near Foster in March 1894 was probably the later Barney farmer Joseph J. Rogers (22 August 1839-21 August 1922), husband of Nancy J. Hires. Family history gives his full name as Joseph Jasper Rogers [27]. The 1908 county map now supplies an important geographic link: it marks "J. J. Rogers Res." near the south end of Land Lot 274, about 1.5-2 miles by land-lot geometry from Foster's Land Lot 227 [5]. The map and the documented Rogers-Hires-Moody family chain make Joseph the clear leading candidate. The identification remains probable, not verified, because the 1894 newspaper gives no middle initial, spouse, child, or land lot.

    No inspected original record expands his middle initial. The census, marriage, tax, death, pension, and cemetery records use Joseph J., Joe, or J. J. The dossier therefore records "Joseph Jasper Rogers" as the family's name for him while noting that "Jasper" remains unverified in the original records [6, 9, 26-27].

    What the Foster article says

    The Valdosta Times reported on 24 March 1894 that Miss Blanche Clements, who lived near Adel, had been visiting "Mr. Joe Rogers' family near Foster" [1]. It does not call Rogers a turpentine employee, place him in the settlement, or connect him to Rozier, the Conolys, or Land Lot 227. "Near Foster" is the full geographic claim.

    Why Joseph J. Rogers is the leading candidate

    Joseph J. Rogers already had a well-established northern Brooks County household. The 1890 Brooks tax digest places him in the Morven district [2]. The original tax image, as previously reviewed for the Barney household work, records 340 acres in Land Lot 270 of the 12th district; the Ancestry index page confirms the man, year, and Morven district but omits the acreage and lot [2]. This record should not be used as evidence that he was then in the immediate Foster neighborhood. On the 1908 county map, Morven's mapped boundary enters the southern part of Land Lot 270, and the town grid lies directly along the lot's southern side [5]. The tax digest does not locate Joseph's 340 acres within the lot, and the 1908 boundary cannot be projected backward to 1890. The tax entry proves Morven-district landholding, but no more precise residence.

    The 1900 census then enumerates "Joe Rodgers", born August 1839, as a farmer in Barney with wife Nancy and children Ophelia, Effie, Everett, Miles, Dove, and Willie [3, 23]. In 1910 Joseph J. Rodgers still owned a farm at Barney, free of mortgage [4].

    The 1908 Brooks County map prints the exact label "J. J. Rogers Res." beside a residence marker on the road at or just north of the southern boundary of Land Lot 274 [5]. The site is in the southwest part of the lot, immediately north of Land Lot 273 and west of Lots 333 and 234. Foster's Land Lot 227 lies roughly two land-lot widths east-southeast. The road continues toward the junction in or near Land Lot 234. The map does not print "Strickland Road"; identifying this segment with the historic Strickland Road corridor depends on the separate 1907 deed language and the project's road reconstruction. The residence label is nevertheless much more specific than the dossier's earlier description of a location merely south or southeast of Barney.

    This is a strong circumstantial fit: the same Joe/Joseph name, an established family, a mapped J. J. Rogers residence close to Foster, and continued residence in the Barney area. It is still circumstantial. The map dates fourteen years after the article, uses initials only, and does not identify the 1894 newspaper man.

    Hires and Moody family connections

    Nancy J. Hires Rogers was the daughter of David Hires/Hiers and Rebecca Yates. The 1850 census places five-year-old Nancy in David and Rebecca Hiers's Lowndes County household, and Nancy's 1932 Georgia death certificate names her parents as David Hires and Rebecca Yates [20-21].

    In 1876 a Brooks County notice named David Hiers as the contractor building a separate lagoon bridge near Folsom Bridge. County officials directed the Morven District road commissioners to inspect his work before payment [22]. The local contractor is a strong match for Nancy's father, but the notice gives no age, middle initial, or family detail; the same-person identification is therefore strong probable, not fully verified. The work concerned the swamp approach at the Folsom crossing, not repair of the river bridge itself.

    The 1900 census directly identifies Dove Rodgers, age twelve, as a daughter in Joe and Nancy Rodgers's household [23]. By 1910 Dove was the wife of James I. Moody of Barney; the census records the couple as Jim and Dove Moody, and later cemetery evidence supplies the fuller James I. Moody identification [24-25]. This establishes the Rogers-Moody family connection independently of the 1894 Foster notice.

    Documented life of the candidate

    • 1839: Joseph was born in Georgia. His death record gives 22 August 1839, although Ancestry's displayed birth-year field mistakenly reads 1939; age 82 and the 1850-1910 census chain show that 1839 is intended [6].
    • 1850-1860: Joseph appears with Uriah and Elizabeth Rogers, first in Lowndes County and then in Brooks County [7-8].
    • 1866: Joseph J. Rodgers married Nancy J. Hires in Brooks County on 21 October [9]. The original-image index supplies the marriage date and county.
    • 1867: A reconstruction oath places Joseph J. Rogers in Brooks County Election District 7 [10].
    • 1870-1880: Joseph and Nancy headed a Brooks County farming household. The 1880 household included children Emma, Iola, Walton H., David U., Ophelia, Havilah, and Effie [11-12].
    • 1890: The tax digest places Joseph in the Morven district [2].
    • 1894: A Joe Rogers family, not otherwise identified, lived near Foster [1].
    • 1900-1910: Joe/Joseph and Nancy were farmers at Barney [3-4].
    • 1908: The Brooks County map marked "J. J. Rogers Res." near the southern edge of Land Lot 274 [5].
    • By 1910: Their daughter Dove had married James I. Moody and lived with him at Barney [23-25].
    • 1920: J. J. and Nancy Rogers were still in the Barney district. The index places them in a household headed by "Q M Rogers", but the adult child's identity remains uncertain without inspection of the original image [13].
    • 1922: Joseph died at Barney. His Georgia death record names Uriah Rogers and Elizabeth Hicks as his parents and Nancy Rogers as his wife [6]. Gornto Cemetery evidence agrees on the 1839-1922 dates [14].

    Identity conflicts and exclusions

    1. "Jasper" is family-reported. James's family gives Joseph's full name as Joseph Jasper Rogers, but no inspected original record expands the middle initial. The family name belongs in the dossier with that provenance, not as an original-record fact.
    2. The 1894 article supplies no family bridge. Blanche Clements's name did not lead to a documented kinship with Nancy Hires Rogers or one of Joseph's children.
    3. The two land references do not carry the same geographic weight. The 1890 tax land in Lot 270 belongs to the Morven side of the district; the 1908 map even shows Morven's boundary extending into the lot's southern part. That record proves landholding but does not place Joseph near Foster. The separate "J. J. Rogers Res." label in Lot 274 is the useful Foster-proximity evidence, although it dates fourteen years after the newspaper item. Neither reference puts him inside the still settlement or Lot 227.
    4. Other Rogers lines existed. The 1900 Barney roster also contains a separate John Rodgers household. He is not a Joe/Joseph candidate, but his presence warns against treating every local Rogers reference as Joseph J.'s family [15].
    5. The 1920 index is awkward. It calls the head "Q M Rogers" and Joseph and Nancy his parents. The earlier local narrative identified the household differently. The 1920 record proves the older couple's continued Barney-area presence but should not be used to settle the adult child's identity without the original image [13].

    Reproducible search ledger

    • Ancestry: logged-in searches covered the 1850, 1860, 1870, 1880, 1900, 1910, and 1920 censuses; Brooks marriage records; reconstruction oaths; Georgia tax digests; Georgia death records; Find a Grave index; newspaper/obituary indexes; and public-tree results. Exact and variant searches used Joe, Joseph, Joseph J., J. J., Rogers, and Rodgers with Brooks, Quitman, Morven, Barney, Foster, Nancy Hires/Hiers, and 1839-1922.
    • Newspapers.com: Georgia, 1885-1900, searched "Joe Rogers", "J. J. Rogers" Brooks, "Joseph Rogers" Brooks, "Joe Rogers" Foster, and "Joe Rogers" Morven. The March 1894 Foster item was found; no second item supplied a spouse, child, middle initial, or other identity bridge.
    • Georgia Historic Newspapers: exact-name date-range search was attempted, but the returned result set ignored the range and mixed in later material.
    • FamilySearch: the exact death/place search redirected to sign-in. The Georgia death collection's Ancestry page identifies FamilySearch as the source of its extracted data, but a FamilySearch ARK was not obtained.
    • Cemetery: Find a Grave was checked directly and through Ancestry. It supports burial, dates, and the Dove Rogers-James I. Moody identification when read with the censuses, but it does not prove the 1894 Foster identity.
    • Land/probate: the 1890 tax record was inspected. No subject-indexed probate record or deed naming Foster or Lot 227 was found in the documented online search.

    Remaining proof targets

    The best remaining bridge would be an 1894-era Brooks deed, tax row, voter list, church record, school record, or local newspaper item that places Nancy, Dove, or another named member of Joseph J. Rogers's family at Foster. Blanche Clements's family correspondence or an original Quitman Free Press social item might also identify the household. Until one of those appears, Joseph J. Rogers should remain the probable leading candidate, not a settled identification. An original record expanding "J. J." to "Joseph Jasper" would separately verify the family-reported middle name.
