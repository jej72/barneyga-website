    Hope Croft

    Back to the Foster Profiles index

    Research dossier

    Prepared: September 27, 2026
    Subject: The Brooks County child named in the 1887 Stonewall Academy schoolhouse-burning reports
    Research purpose: Establish what the surviving records say about Hope Croft, identify him if possible, trace his later life, and test the proposed connection to the 1885–1886 fires around Morven and Folsom Bridge.

    Bottom line

    Contemporary newspapers identify Hope Croft, a small boy described as under ten, as the child caught near a third improvised schoolhouse in June 1887 with partly burned lightwood splinters. The detailed account says he confessed to burning Stonewall Academy and its temporary replacement because he did not want to attend Rev. Joseph J. Williams's school. These are newspaper reports of a confession; no court, school-board, or juvenile record has been located.[1–4]

    Other reports identify the child as a son of Jacob Henry “Jack” Croft and give his age as eight years five months. Local residents then adopted a resolution requiring Croft and his family to leave within three days and remain twenty-five or thirty miles away. The report says Croft agreed. This was an extra-legal community action, not a court order.[3–4]

    The strongest census candidate for Hope is Jacob's youngest son, recorded as Abba Croft, age two, in 1880 and Abner Croft, age twenty-one and born in January 1879, in 1900. The reported age in July 1887 points to a birth around February 1879. The household, age, sex, and later residence all fit. No inspected census or vital record calls this child Hope, however. The conclusion that Hope was Abba/Abner—later recorded as G. A. or George A. Croft—is therefore strong probable, not verified.[5–10]

    A different Hope Croft died in a sawmill boiler explosion near Barney in 1922. The article says the dead men were between twenty-eight and thirty-five, all married, and that this Hope left three small children. He was far too young to have been the eight-year-old of 1887. The 1922 victim was a namesake and must not be merged with the schoolhouse child.[11]

    The 1887 events

    Stonewall Academy and two temporary schools

    The fullest account places Stonewall Academy about nine miles northeast of Quitman in a well-settled neighborhood. Rev. Joseph J. Williams had served as principal for about a year. The academy burned on a Sunday night. Neighbors then converted an outbuilding on Mr. Sepell's plantation into a temporary schoolhouse, which burned after about a week of use. They next converted a house on Mr. Rountree's place into a school.[1–2]

    Near two o'clock one morning, according to the report, barking dogs and cries for help awakened Mr. Hill, Rountree's tenant. Hill found Hope near the third schoolhouse with a bundle of lightwood splinters, some partly burned. Hope first said that his father had sent him to find cows and that he had become lost. After questioning, the paper says, he confessed to burning the first two school buildings and intending to burn the third. It attributed his actions to his opposition to attending Williams's school.[1–2]

    The account says Hope had prepared the splinters the previous afternoon and had traveled about a mile through woods and swamp. It describes him as “not over ten years old” and very small for his age. Other reports give the more precise age of eight years five months.[1–4]

    The parents' reported knowledge

    The detailed report prints the father as “Mr. H. J. Croft,” while the shorter reports and removal resolution call him “Mr. Jack Croft.” Jacob Henry Croft ordinarily appears as J. H. Croft, so “H. J.” may be a transposition, but the printed conflict remains unresolved.[1–4]

    The paper described the father as a poor but respectable farmer and said he appeared to have known nothing of the fires. Hope's mother reportedly said that the family slept in one room, that Hope's bed was within her reach, and that he had left without anyone knowing.[1–2]

    The community resolution

    The Quitman Free Press, as reprinted by the Valdosta Times, reported that local residents met and adopted a resolution requiring Jack Croft and his family to leave within three days and not return within twenty-five or thirty miles. Croft reportedly agreed. Another account said the community had notified him that he must leave or his son would be prosecuted “to the full extent of the law.”[1,4]

    No located record shows that Hope was arrested, charged, tried, committed to an institution, or placed under formal county supervision. The family's reported expulsion was community coercion, not a judicial sentence.

    Identity reconstruction

    The child in Jacob Croft's household

    The 1880 census places Jacob H. Croft and Jerona Ann Croft in the Morven district of Brooks County. Their youngest listed son was Abba Croft, age two. In 1900, Jacob's household in Robinson district, Colquitt County, included Abner Croft, age twenty-one, born in January 1879. Both entries fit a boy who was eight years five months old in July 1887.[5–6]

    The two entries also fit the family's documented move. Jacob was living in Colquitt County by February 1889. In October 1890 he sold about 325 acres on the north side of Brooks County Land Lot 227 to McNeil & Rozier. The property belongs to the pre-company landscape of the Morven district; the later Foster village developed after the Crofts left.[12–13]

    Abner, G. A., and George A. Croft

    The later record chain supports Abner's adult identity:

    • In 1900, Abner Croft, twenty-one and single, lived with Jacob and Eliza Croft in Robinson district.[6]
    • G. A. Croft married Anna Scott in Colquitt County on March 3, 1907.[7]
    • In 1910, an index reading “Alener Croft,” corrected by researchers to Abner, headed a Robinson district farm household with wife Anna and daughters Ella and Etta. He was reported as thirty and married for two years.[8]
    • In 1920, George A. Croft headed a Robinson district farm household with wife Mary A. and five children. The record gives his age as forty-five, an evident disagreement with the 1879 birth reported in 1900.[9]
    • A Georgia death-record index for G. A. Croft names spouse Anna Scott and child Etta Hart. A cemetery index calls him George Abington Croft, gives only 1876–1928, names Jacob Henry and Jeronia Ann Croft as his parents, and places him in Hempstead Church Cemetery near Berlin.[10]

    Together, these records support a single chain from Abba to Abner to G. A./George A. Croft. They do not prove that “Hope” was his nickname or legal given name. “George Abington” is supplied by a derivative cemetery index; the original records inspected here establish only Abba, Abner, and the initials or name George A.

    Identity grade

    Hope Croft = Jacob and Jerona's son Abba/Abner/George A. Croft: strong probable.

    The identification rests on four points: Hope was called Jack Croft's son; Jacob's 1880 household contains only one son whose age closely matches the reported age; Abner's 1900 birth month and year fall within about one month of that report; and Abner remained in the father's household after the family's move to Colquitt County. The remaining gap is direct: no located original record says Hope, Abba, Abner, G. A., and George A. were the same person.

    The 1885–1886 fire question

    The Morven district experienced repeated suspected arson during the two years before Hope was caught:

    • a schoolhouse at Gum Pond burned around November 1885;
    • a country schoolhouse burned in the January 1886 cluster that also destroyed Folsom Bridge, William Ryals's gin house, and the miller's dwelling at Dr. Bourquin's mill;
    • another country schoolhouse was reported burned near the damaged bridge in February 1886;
    • Gum Pond School House burned again in November 1886; and
    • a June 1887 summary said four schoolhouses, three churches, a mill, and other property had been destroyed by incendiaries in the Morven district during the preceding two years.[14–17]

    Hope's reported conduct makes a connection plausible, especially because the Croft family lived in the same general district and one 1886 cluster included a schoolhouse. It is still unproved. No located source names Hope as a suspect in any 1885 or 1886 fire, connects him to Gum Pond, Folsom Bridge, Ryals, or Bourquin, or dates his confessed fires earlier than the Stonewall Academy sequence described in June 1887. The detailed account identifies the two confessed fires as Stonewall Academy and its temporary replacement.[1–4,14–17]

    The 1922 namesake

    On May 10, 1922, a boiler exploded at a small mill on C. J. Downing's land near Barney. The explosion killed A. L. Evans Jr., Clifford Jones, and a man named Hope Croft. The report identifies Croft as the fireman, says he was married and left three small children, and places the dead men's ages between twenty-eight and thirty-five. It also names Hubert Croft as his brother.[11]

    The schoolhouse child would have been about forty-three in 1922. The article's age range makes the mill worker a different man. The older candidate, George A. Croft, appears in the 1920 census with five children and survives in cemetery and death indexes to 1928.[9–11]

    Conflicts and cautions

    1. Father's initials: the detailed account prints H. J. Croft; other reports say Jack Croft. Jacob Henry ordinarily appears as J. H. Croft.
    2. Hope's age: one report says “not over ten”; others say eight years five months.
    3. Name sequence: Abba, Abner, G. A., and George A. form a strong record chain, but “Hope” appears only in the schoolhouse report.
    4. Birth year: 1900 gives January 1879; later records imply about 1875–1880, and the cemetery index says 1876.
    5. Full adult name: “George Abington Croft” comes from a derivative cemetery index. The inspected original indexes support G. A. Croft and George A. Croft.
    6. Newspaper allegations: the confession, motive, and family statements are newspaper accounts, not court findings.

    Research gaps

    No located record supplies an original birth record for Hope or Abner, explicitly links Hope with Abba/Abner/George A., records a court or school-board proceeding from the fires, proves a connection to an 1885 or 1886 fire, or expands George A.'s middle initial in an original record.

    The best remaining targets are Brooks County Superior Court minutes and grand-jury presentments, county school records, original Quitman Free Press issues, family or church records explaining the name Hope, and the original Georgia death certificate for G. A. Croft.
