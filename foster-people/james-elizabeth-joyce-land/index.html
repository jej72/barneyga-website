    James J. and Elizabeth Wells Joyce: Land Lot 227 Dossier

    Back to the Foster Profiles index

    Prepared: August 18, 2026
    Scope: The Joyce family, the derivative claim that Elizabeth held 175 acres in the south part of Land Lot 227, and the relationship of that claim to Foster.
    Evidence labels: verified, strong probable, unresolved, and contradicted.

    Bottom line

    The Joyce connection to Land Lot 227 is important but not yet deed-proved. A public historical-map KML says James J. Joyce's property was advertised for sale in August 1852, was to be divided in May 1859, and included 245 acres in the north half of Lot 227. The same KML assigns his widow, Mrs. Elizabeth Wells Joyce, 175 acres in 1861 and the 1869, 1871, and 1872 tax lists. Those entries are useful derivative leads, not substitutes for the cited tax pages, court minutes, sale papers, or division return. [1]

    The evidence strongly indicates that this James J. Joyce was also known as Jackson Joyce and that Elizabeth's maiden name was Wells. Their daughter Jane Joyce Pike's Georgia death certificate names her parents Jackson Joyce and Elizabeth Wells. The certificate does not mention Lot 227, so it proves the family identity rather than title. [2]

    No located source shows that either Joyce founded Foster, operated its post office, or participated in McNeil & Rozier's naval-stores business. Foster's later development occurred on the Croft-to-McNeil & Rozier tract. The Joyce land is part of the earlier land history that must be reconciled with that deed.

    Land evidence and its limits

    The KML's Lot 227 notes give this sequence: [1]

    Date Derivative entry Assessment
    September 12, 1831 Lot 227 granted to Finch Davis of Jasper County Lead; original grant not inspected in this research pass
    August 1852 Property of James J. Joyce to be sold Lead; sale advertisement or court paper not located
    1857 Estate of James J. Joyce in Superior Court Minutes, Book A, pp. 48-49 Specific archival target; pages not inspected
    May 1859 Joyce property to be divided; 245 acres in the north half Lead; division return not located
    1861 Mrs. Elizabeth Joyce, 175 acres Lead from a derivative tax transcription
    1869, 1871, 1872 Mrs. Elizabeth Wells Joyce, 175 acres Lead from derivative tax transcriptions

    The acreage does not form a clean chain. The same map assigns 325 acres on the north side of Lot 227 to Jacob Henry Croft and 175 acres to Elizabeth Joyce. That totals 500 acres, while the standard Irwin County lottery lot was 490 acres. The map also describes the Joyce estate's earlier interest as 245 acres in the north half. The ten-acre overage and the change from 245 to 175 acres remain unexplained. These figures must not be drawn as exact, non-overlapping boundaries. [1][3]

    McNeil & Rozier's original October 9, 1890 deed from Jacob Henry Croft conveys about 325 acres on the north side of Lot 227 for $1,000. That deed verifies Croft's conveyance but does not identify Elizabeth Joyce, recite the Joyce estate chain, or prove whether the two descriptions overlap. [4]

    Identity and community context

    Jane Joyce Pike's death certificate supplies the strongest direct identity bridge: her father was Jackson Joyce and her mother was Elizabeth Wells. Because the Land Lot 227 index uses James J. Joyce and Elizabeth Wells Joyce in the same place and period, James J. Joyce = Jackson Joyce is strong probable, not absolute. An original estate paper naming the widow or heirs would raise that conclusion to verified. [1][2]

    An 1831 deed transcription names James J. Joyce as one of five trustees for the Methodist Episcopal camp, church, and burying ground at Mount Zion. The other trustees were William Blair, William Hendry, Lawrence Folsom, and William G. Hall. Huxford said he copied the transcription from the original deed; the recorded copy in Lowndes Deed Book A, pp. 654-655, burned about 1859 and was not re-recorded. The original manuscript was not located online. This places Joyce in the older local religious community, but it does not connect Mount Zion's Lot 271 land to his Lot 227 estate. [5]

    Joyce's Ferry is the Miller Bridge crossing, in accordance with the resolved local geographic finding. The 1908 Brooks County map places Miller Bridge at the east edge of Land Lot 189, where the road crosses Little River. It is not Folsom Bridge, which the same map places upstream in Lot 228. This confirms that the Miller Bridge crossing was separate from Foster and Lot 227. The 1908 map does not, by itself, establish the precise footprint of Washington Joyce's earlier ferry landing. [6]

    The Folsom Bridge history covers the separate upstream crossing and its relationship to Foster.

    What is proved and what remains open

    • Verified: Jane Joyce Pike's death record names Jackson Joyce and Elizabeth Wells as her parents. [2]
    • Verified from an original deed image: Croft conveyed about 325 acres on the north side of Lot 227 to McNeil & Rozier in 1890. [4]
    • Strong probable: James J. Joyce of the Lot 227 entries was Jackson Joyce, husband of Elizabeth Wells and father of Jane Joyce Pike. [1][2]
    • Derivative only: the public KML assigns Elizabeth Joyce 175 acres in Lot 227 in 1861, 1869, 1871, and 1872; the original tax pages have not been inspected. [1]
    • Unresolved: how the 245-acre Joyce estate interest became Elizabeth's 175 acres; the heirs and division shares; whether any portion overlapped Croft's later 325-acre tract; and the exact tract boundaries.
    • Not shown: any Joyce role in founding Foster, operating the post office, or conducting the McNeil & Rozier business.

    Namesake and geography controls

    No other James, Jackson, or Elizabeth Joyce was merged into this family without a relationship record. Later Brooks County Joyce households are not evidence of this specific title chain merely because of the surname. Joyce's Ferry has been fixed at Miller Bridge, which the 1908 map places in Lot 189. It must not be moved to Folsom Bridge or used as a Lot 227 boundary without a deed call.

    Next records to obtain

    1. The cited Superior Court Minutes Book A, pp. 48-49, for the 1857 estate entry.
    2. The August 1852 sale proceeding and the May 1859 division return, checking Lowndes records before Brooks County's creation and Brooks records afterward.
    3. Original Lowndes and Brooks tax digest pages for 1861, 1869, 1871, and 1872.
    4. Deed and estate indexes under Joyce, Pike, Croft, Davis, and the known heirs to bridge 175, 245, and 325 acres.
    5. A metes-and-bounds comparison of every original instrument before assigning the Foster site to one predecessor tract.
