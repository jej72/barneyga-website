    Historical information currently draws from:

    • Irwin County recorded deed, Newman Pounds to Lawrence Folsom, November 27, 1823, Deed Book 1, pp. 65-66, FamilySearch DGS 008032657, images 50 and 51; original-record proof that Lawrence was an Irwin County resident and owner of District 12, Land Lot 187, by that date.
    • Folks Huxford's The History of Brooks County, Georgia, 1858-1948
    • Unsigned, “A Sketch of the Early History of Lowndes County, Georgia,” The South Georgia Historical and Genealogical Quarterly (1922), pp. 1–7, edited by Folks Huxford; original Internet Archive scan and AHGP transcription. Used for the 1821 quartet tradition and the article's acknowledgment that its data came chiefly from Hortense W. Baker's recent Valdosta Times sketches. The quartet follows immediately after that acknowledgment but has no separate attribution to Baker.
    • National Park Service, "Summer 1814: The Treaty of Ft. Jackson ends the Creek War," used for the August 9, 1814 Treaty of Fort Jackson, the nearly 22-million-acre Creek cession, and the disputed southern buffer-land context, nps.gov/articles/treaty-of-fort-jackson.htm
    • James Bagley Clements, History of Irwin County, 1932, chapter 2; used for the Tallassee country description, Creek possession and hunting-ground context, the December 1818 act creating Early, Irwin, and Appling counties from the late cessions, and the original Irwin County survey totals of 6,421 full 490-acre lots plus 161 fractional lots in sixteen districts. The full lots equal 3,146,290 acres (about 4,916 square miles); the additional fractional boundary lots support the rounded estimate of approximately 5,000 square miles.
    • Georgia Archives, "1820 Land Lottery," used for original Irwin County's sixteen land districts and 490-acre lot size, georgiaarchives.org/research/1820_land_lottery
